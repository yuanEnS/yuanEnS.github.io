Sample Size and Power Calculation for Continuous Outcomes in Open-Cohort Stepped Wedge Designs With Multiple Interventions

Shiyu Feng, Meng Zheng, Yi Zhang, Xuezhi Liang, Minglu Yuan, Wen Chen

摘要

Stepped wedge designs with multiple interventions (M-SWDs) are increasingly used to evaluate complex implementation strategies. In many such trials, participants enter and leave over time, giving rise to open-cohort sampling and complex correlation structures. Existing sample size and power calculation methods are largely restricted to single-intervention designs, closed cohorts, or simplified correlation assumptions. We develop a unified, parameter-based framework for sample size and power calculation in open-cohort M-SWDs. The proposed methodology accommodates several common multi-intervention designs, including concurrent, cross-over, supplementation, and factorial designs. Flexible open-cohort sampling schemes are incorporated to account for participant turnover, and both block-exchangeable and decay correlation structures are considered to capture within-cluster and within-individual dependence over time. Closed-form and generalized variance expressions for intervention effect estimators are derived, enabling efficient power calculation under a wide range of design settings. Extensive simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed formulas provide accurate power estimates, while further power analyses illustrate how the key correlation parameters and participant retention jointly influence power. This work provides practical and theoretically grounded guidance for planning multi-intervention stepped wedge trials under open-cohort sampling, facilitating more realistic and transparent design-stage decision-making.

Optimal e-values for testing the mean of a bounded random variable against a composite alternative

S Arnold, E Clerico

摘要

We derive explicitly the e-values with optimal (relative) growth rate in the worst case for testing the mean of a bounded random variable, thereby providing the first application of the (RE)GROW quality criteria beyond the assumption of mutually absolutely continuous hypotheses for e-values originally proposed by Grünwald et al. (2024). For both criteria, we explicitly characterise the alternatives that are most difficult to test against and show that they admit a meaningful interpretation. We give two important examples in which REGROW provides a powerful quality criterion to choose optimal e-variables whereas GROW leads to trivial solutions.

SiM Genetics & Omics · 发表于 2026-11-03，收录于 2026-11-06

Factor-Augmented Neural Conditional Density Estimation with Application to Drug Response Prediction

Weineng Zhu, Xiaoyu Sheng

摘要

High-dimensional pharmacogenomic data pair thousands of molecular features with hundreds of cancer cell lines, complicating conditional density estimation. We study factor-augmented CINDES, which estimates conditional response densities from principal-component scores through binary classification. For an honest pilot/main-sample construction, assuming fresh-score PCA recovery, we derive a risk bound separating conditional-density learning, PCA score error, and representation bias. This guarantee does not cover the implemented procedure, which reuses observations for PCA and CINDES. In favorable strong-factor simulations, PCA improved a fixed CINDES pipeline, but PCA-MDN achieved lower negative log-likelihood (NLL) and integrated L 1 error in the matched comparison. Stress tests showed losses from omitted response-relevant low-variance directions and weak factor recovery, with sample-size-dependent factor-count effects. Across all 692 eligible source-specific genomics of drug sensitivity in cancer (GDSC) units and five splits, mean held-out NLL was 1.5414 for PCA-CINDES, versus 1.5661 and 1.5557 for ElasticNet and PCA/Ridge Gaussian models; PCA-MDN was better at 1.5156. A previously fixed 35-unit audit favored PCA-CINDES over Raw-X CINDES (mean paired NLL difference - 0 . 178 ) and found lower measured computational cost. The evidence supports PCA compression when retained factors capture response information and are accurately estimated; it does not establish robustness to general nonlinear or nonfactor structures, or CINDES as the preferred second-stage estimator. The application concerns preclinical prediction rather than clinical utility.

Biometrics Clinical Trials Longitudinal Data · 发表于 2026-10-31，收录于 2026-11-06

Sample size determination for randomized decentralized clinical trials with longitudinal outcomes

Chunyi Zhang, Ying Yuan, Ruitao Lin, Suyu Liu

摘要

Decentralized clinical trials (DCTs) offer significant advantages in enhancing trial accessibility, reducing patient burden, and improving population representativeness. These benefits are particularly pronounced in longitudinal trials, where patient outcomes are measured repeatedly over time. However, existing literature lacks appropriate sample size formulas tailored to longitudinal DCTs, which must account for heterogeneous measurement sources and increased uncertainty introduced by decentralized data collection. We propose a unified sample size calculation method for randomized longitudinal DCTs under the generalized linear model framework. The proposed formula is available in closed form and flexibly accommodates a variety of data collection designs, including the simple hybrid design, where all patients contribute both onsite and offsite data, and the stratified hybrid design, where different patient groups may undergo distinct data collection protocols. We also develop estimation procedures to estimate the unknown parameters and compute the sample statistics. Our method further generalizes to accommodate heterogeneous intraclass correlation coefficients, unequal variances between treatment arms, and varying numbers of data collection types or strata. Extensive simulation studies show that the proposed sample size formula achieves robust control of the type I error rate and maintains the desired power across a wide range of realistic scenarios.

Scale-Transformed Power Priors for External Data Integration in Longitudinal Studies

Md Tuhin Sheikh

摘要

Incorporating external data can improve precision in longitudinal studies, but effective borrowing requires that information from different studies be comparable. Within a Bayesian framework, power priors are widely used for external data integration through likelihood discounting. However, they assume that parameters are on a common information scale. In longitudinal settings, differences in follow-up duration, measurement variability, and correlation structure, along with random effects, can lead to differences in effective Fisher information across studies. This complicates parameter comparability and poses challenges for existing methods, which can result in biased or inefficient inference. We propose a scale-transformed power prior for external data integration in longitudinal studies. The method is developed within a linear mixed-effects modeling framework and rescales historical parameters to the current study scale using Fisher information before borrowing. Conditional Fisher information accounts for unobserved random effects and adjusts for study-specific variability. To address differences in variability and correlation structure, we implement a partial borrowing strategy that applies borrowing to fixed effects while allowing variance components to remain study-specific. We introduce a generalized formulation with an additive adjustment component to allow for remaining differences after scaling. Theoretical results characterize how differences in information scale lead to bias under conventional power priors and establish conditions under which the proposed method improves bias and precision. Simulation studies demonstrate improved performance and provide guidance on settings where scale transformation is beneficial. An application to prostate cancer clinical trial data demonstrates the importance of the proposed method.

Dimension reduction for characterizing sexual dimorphism in biomechanics of the temporomandibular joint

Sung Hee Park, Xin Zhang, Elizabeth H Slate, Shuchun Sun, Hai Yao

摘要

Sexual dimorphism is a critical factor in many biological and medical research fields. In biomechanics and bioengineering, understanding sex differences is crucial for studying musculoskeletal conditions such as temporomandibular disorder. This paper focuses on the association between the craniofacial skeletal morphology and temporomandibular joint related masticatory muscle attachments to discern sex differences. Data were collected from 11 female and 10 male cadaver heads to investigate sex-specific relationships between the skull and muscles. We propose a conditional cross-covariance reduction (CCR) model, designed to examine the dynamic association between two sets of random variables conditioned on a third binary variable (e.g., sex), highlighting the most distinctive sex-related relationships between skull and muscle attachments in the human cadaver data. Under the CCR model, we employ a sparse singular value decomposition algorithm. We further introduce a sequential permutation for selecting sparsity method to identify important variables and determine the optimal number of selected variables.

Semiparametrically efficient semi-supervised statistical inference

Z Xu, D Witten, A Shojaie

摘要

Summary: We consider statistical inference under a semi-supervised setting where we have access to both labelled and unlabelled observations. We ask the question: under what circumstances, and by how much, can incorporating the unlabelled data improve upon inference using the labelled data? To answer this question, we investigate semi-supervised inference through the lens of semiparametric efficiency theory. For general pathwise differentiable targets under semiparametric models, we derive the efficiency lower bound for asymptotically linear semi-supervised estimators. Perhaps not surprisingly, this bound coincides with the results in classical missing data theory when ‘positivity’ holds, i.e., when the limiting proportion of labelled observations is strictly positive. However, unlike missing data theory, our theory can handle the boundary regime in which the proportion of labelled observations converges to zero, which is in fact the setting of primary interest in the context of semi-supervised learning. We further characterize a broad class of well-specified targets for which unlabelled data cannot yield efficiency gains. These results shed insight into the promise and limitations of the very active field of prediction-powered inference. We propose an efficient semi-supervised estimator that attains the efficiency bound, and a prediction-powered estimator that incorporates black-box predictions and is guaranteed to be no worse than the supervised baseline in asymptotic variance. We illustrate our theoretical results via simulation studies and a real-data application.

P4MC: A Monte Carlo Classification Method Based on Partial Variables for Postoperative Complication Prediction

Lingyi Hu, Yao Cui, Qi Yang, Shan Tang, Shanjun Mao

摘要

Predicting postoperative complications is a critical task for improving patient care and optimizing resource management. We formulate the task as a binary classification problem based on two‐stage clinical data: preoperative and intraoperative variables. A key difficulty is that intraoperative data are systematically missing for preoperative prediction. Existing methods, which typically rely solely on preoperative data or single intraoperative indicator imputation, generally fail to capture important latent intraoperative information. To provide a more robust solution, we propose P4MC (Preoperative, Partial intraoPerative, and Postoperative Monte Carlo classification). This approach explicitly models the dependence between the two stages to recover latent intraoperative information. Specifically, P4MC first applies partial sufficient dimension reduction (PSDR) to compress intraoperative variables into a low‐dimensional sufficient reduction. We then estimate the conditional distribution of this reduction given preoperative variables using nonparametric density estimation and generate latent intraoperative features for classification via Monte Carlo sampling. A simulation study demonstrates the improved predictive performance and stability of P4MC when combined with standard machine learning methods. Applications to two clinical cohorts, laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy and hepatocellular carcinoma, further show that P4MC outperforms conventional approaches and yields clinically interpretable insights. An open‐source R program is provided in the P4MC package, available at https://github.com/501fox/P4MC.git .

Multivariate Poisson intensity estimation via low-rank tensor decomposition

Haotian Xu, Carlos Misael Madrid Padilla, Oscar Hernan Madrid Padilla, Daren Wang

摘要

In this work, we propose new matrix- and tensor-based methodologies for estimating multivariate intensity functions of inhomogeneous point processes. By viewing multivariate intensity functions as infinite-dimensional matrices or tensors within function spaces, our algorithms attain the optimal bias-variance trade-off, yielding rate-optimal estimation error, with model complexity governed by matrix or tensor ranks. They substantially improve estimation accuracy, while simultaneously reducing computational cost. To illustrate the adaptivity of the proposed framework, we show that many fundamental classes of multivariate functions, including additive and mean-field models, admit finite-rank tensor representations. We apply our method to a four-dimensional U.S. Geological Survey earthquake dataset, comprising features such as latitude, longitude, depth, and magnitude. Our tensor estimator recovers localized seismicity patterns (California, Oklahoma, Pacific Northwest, north-central U.S.), whereas the kernel baseline oversmooths them.

Bivariate Copula Models With Event-Free Cure Effect for Survival Extrapolations and Individual-Level Correlation Analysis

Daniel J Sharpe, Georgia Yates, Tuli De, Renuka Kandikatla, Prianka Singh, Matthew Dixon, Kiran Davé, Jackie Vanderpuye-Orgle, Murat Kurt

摘要

OBJECTIVES: Overall survival (OS) extrapolations from mixture cure models (MCMs) are often sensitive to the cure fraction, which is challenging to estimate when data are immature. Furthermore, in some cancer populations, it may be unreasonable to assume curative effects among patients who are not event-free. We addressed these problems using bivariate copula MCMs to jointly model OS and progression- (PFS) or recurrence-free survival (RFS), with a single event-free cure fraction parameter. METHODS: We proposed a practical framework for parametric survival copula model selection and interpretation, which involves estimating five representative candidate copula functions and examining OS distributions conditioned on intermediate event times. Bivariate MCMs with event-free cure fraction, based on previously selected marginal distributions, were estimated for two studies of nivolumab with contrasting survival patterns, namely, in previously treated advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (OS-PFS correlation) and resected melanoma (OS-RFS correlation). RESULTS: OS extrapolations from bivariate MCMs with an effective PFS (or RFS) cure fraction were more conservative than from univariate MCMs. Although patient-level OS-PFS/RFS correlation coefficients were sensitive to the copula, model averaging and inspecting estimated conditional OS distributions enabled clinically meaningful inference on the prognostic impact of intermediate events. CONCLUSIONS: Bivariate MCMs with event-free cured subpopulation incorporated curative effects into OS extrapolations more cautiously than conventional MCMs, based on a more rapidly maturing composite endpoint. The model is especially useful when OS data are immature and if post-event cure is clinically implausible. Employing alternative copulas that cover archetypal association patterns mitigated model selection bias in patient-level correlation analysis.

Two-Stage Estimation for Causal Inference Involving Semi-Continuous Exposures

Xiaoya Wang, Richard J Cook, Yeying Zhu, Tugba Akkaya-Hocagil, R Colin Carter, Sandra W Jacobson, Joseph L Jacobson, Louise M Ryan

摘要

Methods for causal inference are well developed for binary and continuous exposures, but in many settings, the exposure has a substantial mass at zero-such exposures are called semi-continuous. We propose a general causal framework for such semi-continuous exposures, together with a two-stage estimation strategy. A two-part propensity structure is introduced for the semi-continuous exposure, with one component for exposure status (exposed vs unexposed) and another for the exposure level among those exposed, and incorporates both into a marginal structural model that disentangles the effects of exposure status and dose. The two-stage procedure sequentially targets the causal dose-response among exposed individuals and the causal effect of exposure status at a reference dose, allowing flexibility in the choice of propensity score methods in the second stage. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality for the resulting estimators, and characterize their limiting values under misspecification of the propensity score models. Simulation studies evaluate finite sample performance and robustness, and an application to a study of prenatal alcohol exposure and child cognition demonstrates how the proposed methods can be used to address a range of scientific questions about both exposure status and exposure intensity.

Online Updating Mixture Cure Models for Large-Scale Survival Data Streams

Li Jin, Tao Hu, Liming Xiang

摘要

The growing availability of large-scale survival data streams present significant challenges for statistical inference particularly in the presence of a cure fraction, which complicates estimation and increases computational burden. To address these challenges, we propose an online updating mixture cure model framework. Our approach employs a batchwise renewable estimation procedure that aggregates historical information into summary statistics to update the model without retaining historical individual-level data, and thus eliminating the need for full data storage. A renewable penalized expectation-maximization algorithm is developed to enable simultaneous parameter estimation and variable selection. Furthermore, a tuning-free strategy is incorporated to enhance computational efficiency. The asymptotic properties of the proposed method are established. Simulation studies and an application to breast cancer data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results database demonstrate the method's estimation accuracy, predictive performance, and computational scalability.

Developing an Innovative Jackknife Estimator for Quantifying Between-Study Variance in Meta-Analysis of Proportions

Muhammad Mohsin, Stephan Morgenthaler

摘要

The DerSimonian and Laird (DL) method, commonly used in meta-analysis, often underestimates the between-study variance. The underestimation causes the confidence intervals to have less than the expected 95% coverage, resulting in inconclusive research findings. The present study introduces the use of the jackknife technique to improve the DL between-study variance estimator for meta-analyses of proportions from single-arm studies, as well as suggests certain transformations in combination with the jackknife technique. The proposed methodology is intended for settings in which the parameter of interest is an event probability or proportion, particularly for small numbers of studies with large variation. Simulation studies for binomial observations are conducted to compare the relative biases and coverage probabilities of the mean ( μ ) obtained by the DL, Paule and Mandel (PM), Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML), and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) methods with our proposed DerSimonian and Laird jackknife (DLJ) and transformed DerSimonian and Laird jackknife (TDLJs) methods. The simulation results show that our proposed DLJ and TDLJ estimators better quantify the between-study variance in meta-analysis of binary events, especially for small numbers of studies with larger variation and high probability of μ respectively. Three real life examples are presented to determine the performance of our new proposed between-study variance estimators.

Can discrete-time analyses be trusted for stepped wedge trials with continuous recruitment?

Hao Wang, Guangyu Tong, Heather Allore, Kelsey L Grantham, Monica Taljaard, Fan Li

摘要

In stepped wedge cluster randomized trials (SW-CRTs), the intervention is rolled out to clusters sequentially over multiple periods. It is common practice to analyze data from SW-CRTs using linear mixed models that treat time as discrete. However, a recent systematic review found that 95.1% of cross-sectional SW-CRTs recruit individuals continuously over time. Despite the prevalence of continuous recruitment, there has been limited guidance on how to draw model-robust inference for such designs. In this article, we conduct simulation studies to examine the performance of discrete-time linear mixed models in the case of continuous recruitment designs with a continuous outcome. Specifically, we generate outcomes under a continuous-time exponential decay correlation structure with either discrete or continuous fixed period effects. We consider constant and exposure-time-dependent intervention effects, with and without random intervention effects across clusters. We then analyze the simulated data using linear mixed models under three discrete-time working correlation structures: simple exchangeable, nested exchangeable, and discrete-time exponential decay, with robust sandwich variance estimation. Our results demonstrate that discrete-time analysis often yields negligible bias and that the robust variance estimator with the Mancl and DeRouen correction consistently achieves nominal coverage and type I error rate. However, when recruitment patterns vary systematically between control and intervention periods, discrete-time analyses give slightly biased intervention effect estimates. Finally, we illustrate these findings by reanalyzing a completed SW-CRT.

Test of partial effects for Fréchet regression on Bures-Wasserstein manifolds

Haoshu Xu, Hongzhe Li

摘要

We propose a novel test for assessing partial effects in Fréchet regression on the Bures-Wasserstein manifold. Under the null hypothesis, we show that the statistic admits a degenerate V-statistic approximation whose limiting distribution is aweighted mixture of chi-squared random variables, with weights determined by the eigenvalues of an integral operator associated with a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) kernel. Sensitivity Analysis for the Test-Negative Design

Soumyabrata Kundu, Peng Ding, Jingshu Wang, Xinran Li

摘要

The test-negative design has become popular for evaluating the effectiveness of post-licensure vaccines using observational data. In addition to its logistical convenience on data collection, the design is also believed to control for the differential health-care-seeking behavior between vaccinated and unvaccinated individuals, an important while often unmeasured confounder between the vaccination and infection. Hence, the design has been employed routinely to monitor seasonal flu vaccines and more recently to measure the COVID-19 vaccine effectiveness. Despite its popularity, the design has been questioned, in particular about its ability to fully control for the unmeasured confounding. In this paper, we explore deviations from a perfect test-negative design, and propose various sensitivity analysis methods for estimating the effect of vaccination measured by the causal odds ratio on the subpopulation of individuals with good health-care-seeking behavior. We start with point identification of the causal odds ratio under a test-negative design, comparing different forms of identification assumptions and their corresponding estimands. We then propose two approaches for conducting sensitivity analysis, addressing the influence of the unmeasured confounding in two different ways. Specifically, one approach investigates partial control for unmeasured confounding in the test-negative design, while the other examines the impact of unmeasured confounding on both vaccination and infection. Furthermore, we combine these approaches to provide narrower bounds on the true causal odds ratio, and further sharpen the bounds by restricting the treatment effect heterogeneity. Finally, we apply the proposed methods to evaluate the effectiveness of COVID-19 vaccines using observational data from test-negative designs.

POLAR: A Pessimistic Model-based Policy Learning Algorithm for Dynamic Treatment Regimes

Ruijia Zhang, Xiangyu Zhang, Zhengling Qi, Yue Wu, Yanxun Xu

摘要

Dynamic treatment regimes (DTRs) provide a principled framework for optimizing sequential decision-making in domains where decisions must adapt over time in response to individual trajectories, such as healthcare, education, and digital interventions. However, existing statistical methods often rely on strong positivity assumptions and lack robustness under partial data coverage, while offline reinforcement learning approaches typically focus on average training performance, lack statistical guarantees, and require solving complex optimization problems. To address these challenges, we propose POLAR, a novel pessimistic model-based policy learning algorithm for offline DTR optimization. POLAR estimates the transition dynamics from offline data and quantifies uncertainty for each history-action pair. A pessimistic penalty is then incorporated into the reward function to discourage actions with high uncertainty. Unlike many existing methods that focus on average training performance or provide guarantees only for an oracle policy, POLAR directly targets the suboptimality of the final learned policy and offers theoretical guarantees, without relying on computationally intensive minimax or constrained optimization procedures. To the best of our knowledge, POLAR is the first model-based DTR method to provide both statistical and computational guarantees, including finite-sample bounds on policy suboptimality. Empirical results on both synthetic data and the MIMIC-III dataset demonstrate that POLAR outperforms state-of-the-art methods and yields near-optimal, history-aware treatment strategies. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

Localizing support-set changepoints in high-dimensional linear models

Junfeng Cui, Junhui Wang, Changliang Zou, Yan Zhou

摘要

Existing changepoint localization methods for high-dimensional regression models typically focus on scenarios where the regression coefficients follow a piecewise constant structure over time. However, this assumption can be overly restrictive in many practical applications due to the complex temporal dynamics of the coefficients. Additionally, in sparse regression models, the primary interest sometimes lies not in changes in the magnitudes of the coefficients, but rather in identifying the time points at which covariates transition between inactive and active states. In this paper, we propose a simple yet effective method for detecting changepoints in the support set, allowing the coefficients to vary continuously over time. An R package "SCP" is developed to implement the proposed method.

General Frameworks for Conditional Two-Sample Testing

Seongchan Lee, Suman Cha, Ilmun Kim

摘要

We study the problem of conditional two-sample testing, which aims to determine whether two populations have the same distribution after accounting for confounding factors. This problem commonly arises in various applications, such as domain adaptation and algorithmic fairness, where comparing two groups is essential while controlling for confounding variables. We begin by establishing a hardness result for conditional two-sample testing, demonstrating that no valid test can have significant power against any single alternative without proper assumptions. We then introduce two general frameworks that implicitly or explicitly target specific classes of distributions for their validity and power. Our first framework allows us to convert any conditional independence test into a conditional two-sample test in a black-box manner, while preserving the asymptotic properties of the original conditional independence test. The second framework transforms the problem into comparing marginal distributions with estimated density ratios, which enables direct application of existing methods for marginal two-sample testing. We demonstrate this idea in a concrete manner with classification and kernel-based methods. Finally, simulation studies are conducted to illustrate the proposed frameworks in finite-sample scenarios.

Bayesian multiple imputation for functional data

Ziren Jiang, Lei Xuan, Eric F Lock, Erjia Cui

摘要

Missing data are pervasive in modern functional datasets, where trajectories are often sparsely or irregularly observed. Although functional principal component analysis is widely used to reconstruct incomplete curves, existing approaches typically employ single imputation, leading to overly optimistic inferences in downstream analyses. To address these challenges, we develop a novel Bayesian multiple imputation framework for functional data (BAMIFun). Asymptotic theory of the quadratic assignment procedure for dyadic data analysis

Lei Shi, Peng Ding

摘要

The quadratic assignment procedure (QAP) is a popular tool for analysing dyadic data in medical and social sciences. To test the association between 2 dyadic measurements represented by 2 symmetric matrices, QAP calculates the p-value by permuting the units, or equivalently, by permuting the rows and columns of 1 matrix in the same way. Its extension to the regression setting, known as the multiple regression QAP, has also gained popularity, especially in psychometrics. However, the statistical theory for QAP has not been fully established in the literature. We fill the gap in this paper. We formulate the dyadic models underlying various QAPs. We derive (a) the asymptotic sampling distributions of some canonical test statistics and (b) the corresponding asymptotic permutation distributions induced by QAP under strong and weak null hypotheses. Task (a) relies on applying the theory of U-statistics, and task (b) relies on applying the theory of double-indexed permutation statistics. Our asymptotic theory combines tasks (a) and (b), and suggests that we must use properly studentized statistics in QAP to ensure that it is finite-sample exact under the strong null hypothesis and preserves the asymptotic type I error rate under the weak null hypothesis.

A Tail Likelihood Ratio Method for Large-scale Causal Mediation Tests in Epigenome-Wide Studies

Haoyu Yang, Ruoyu Wang, Yuzhou Lin, Xihong Lin

摘要

Large-scale mediation testing has attracted considerable attention in epigenome-wide studies, intending to determine whether the effect of an exposure on an outcome is mediated through specific DNA methylation sites. Statistical inference for the mediation effects is challenged by the complexity of testing a large number of composite null hypotheses. We propose a tail likelihood ratio (TLR) test that is robust to variations in the proportion of null cases, signal strength, and signal distribution, achieving higher power for marginal mediation testing than existing methods while maintaining robust error rate control across diverse scenarios, with false discovery rate control established theoretically under independence and supported by simulation under dependence. The proposed TLR method features a powerful test statistic whose distribution under the composite null is derived analytically. Meanwhile, since methylations are often correlated, we account for the correlation of dependent test statistics using a principal component-based approximation. Simulations demonstrate that the proposed method maintains accurate error rate control in multiple comparisons and exhibits power gains compared to existing methods. Uniform ergodicity and spectral gap of importance-tempered MCMC with heavy-tailed targets

Quan Zhou

摘要

We study the importance-tempered random-walk Metropolis (RWM) algorithm on Rd, which targets the tempered density πβ(x)∝(π(x))β with β∈(0,1), and corrects for the resulting bias by importance weighting. The dynamics of this sampler can be described by a continuous-time Markov chain whose embedded chain is the tempered random-walk Metropolis. We prove that when the tails of π decay at rate ‖x‖−γ, this continuous-time chain is uniformly ergodic if and only if dγ&lt;β&lt;(γ−2)γ. Furthermore, the global spectral gap of this chain can be controlled via the spectral gap of the chain restricted to a ball with radius growing polynomially in d. Our theory and and simulation studies show that, with a suitable choice of β, importance tempering can substantially reduce sensitivity to initialization and significantly improve efficiency over the standard random-walk Metropolis.

Enhancing visual interpretability and explainability in functional survival trees and forests

Giuseppe Loffredo, Elvira Romano, Fabrizio Maturo

摘要
