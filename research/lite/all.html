Weisfeiler-Lehman Meets Optimal Transport: Theory, Algorithms, and Implications

Samantha Chen, Sunhyuk Lim, Facundo Mémoli, Zhengchao Wan, Yusu Wang

摘要

We introduce the Weisfeiler–Lehman (WL) distance, a pseudometric defined on labeled measure Markov chains (LMMCs)—a general class of structures encompassing labeled graphs. This distance is computable in polynomial time and is closely aligned with the classical WL graph isomorphism test: it assigns a positive value if and only if the WL test distinguishes the two input graphs. The WL distance captures fine structural differences in the underlying LMMCs and is, consequently, more discriminative than the state-of-the-art Wasserstein WL graph kernel. The distance motivates a neural network architecture on LMMCs, which we call Markov chain neural networks (MCNNs), that is universal for continuous functions over LMMCs (hence all graphs) under the topology induced by the WL distance. This finding further extends to the analysis of graph neural networks (GNNs). Additionally, the WL distance admits an alternative characterization through Markovian couplings and Causal Optimal Transport (COT), offering a stochastic-process perspective that uncovers a surprising connection between the seemingly unrelated WL test and COT. Finally, we show that the WL distance is stable under a natural variant of the Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distance that we propose. The GW distance is generally NP-hard to compute and hence the WL distance provides a polynomial-time computable lower bound for it.

Stochastic Bilevel Optimization with Heavy-Tailed Noise

Zhuanghua Liu, Luo Luo

摘要

This paper considers the smooth bilevel optimization in which the lower-level problem is strongly convex and the upper-level problem is possibly nonconvex. We focus on the stochastic setting where the algorithm can access the unbiased stochastic gradient evaluation with heavy-tailed noise, which is prevalent in many machine learning applications, such as training large language models and reinforcement learning. We propose a nested-loop normalized stochastic bilevel approximation (N$^2$SBA) for finding an $\epsilon$-stationary point with the stochastic first-order oracle (SFO) complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(\kappa^{\frac{7p-3}{p-1}} \sigma^{\frac{p}{p-1}} \epsilon^{-\frac{4 p - 2}{p-1}}\big)$, where $\kappa$ is the condition number, $p\in(1,2]$ is the order of central moment for the noise, and $\sigma$ is the noise level. Furthermore, we specialize our idea to solve the nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax optimization problem, achieving an $\epsilon$-stationary point with the SFO complexity of $\tilde{\mathcal O}\big(\kappa^{\frac{2p-1}{p-1}} \sigma^{\frac{p}{p-1}} \epsilon^{-\frac{3p-2}{p-1}}\big)$. All the above upper bounds match the best-known results under the special case of the bounded variance setting, i.e., $p=2$. We also conduct the numerical experiments to show the empirical superiority of proposed methods.

Sharp Generalization for Nonparametric Regression in Interpolation Space by Over-Parameterized Neural Networks Trained with Preconditioned Gradient Descent and Early Stopping

Yingzhen Yang, Ping Li

摘要

We study nonparametric regression using an over-parameterized two-layer neural network trained with algorithmic guarantees in this paper. We consider the setting where the training features are drawn uniformly from the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^d$, and the target function lies in an interpolation space commonly studied in statistical learning theory. We demonstrate that training the neural network with a novel Preconditioned Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm, equipped with early stopping, achieves a sharp regression rate of $\mathcal O(n^{-\frac{2\alpha s'}{2\alpha s'+1}})$, where $\alpha$ parametrizes the polynomial eigenvalue decay of the regular Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK), $\lambda_j\asymp j^{-2\alpha}$, and $2\alpha=d/(d-1)$ under the spherical uniform design considered here. The target function is assumed to belong to the interpolation space $[\mathcal H_K]^{s'}$ with $s'\ge3$. This rate is even sharper than the currently known nearly-optimal rate of $\mathcal O(n^{-\frac{2\alpha s'}{2\alpha s'+1}})\log^2(1/\delta)$, where $n$ is the size of the training data and $\delta \in (0,1)$ is a small probability. This rate is also sharper than the standard kernel regression rate of $\mathcal O(n^{-\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha+1}})$ obtained under the regular Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime when training the neural network with the vanilla gradient descent (GD), where $2\alpha = d/(d-1)$. Our analysis is based on two key technical contributions. First, we present a principled decomposition of the network output at each PGD step into a function in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) of a newly induced integral kernel, and a residual function with small $L^{\infty}$-norm. Second, leveraging this decomposition, we apply local Rademacher complexity theory to tightly control the complexity of the function class comprising all the neural network functions obtained in the PGD iterates. Our results show how PGD changes the effective kernel in the reference training dynamics, inducing an integral kernel with lower complexity than the regular NTK arising from vanilla GD.

Semidefinite programming relaxations and debiasing for MAXCUT-based clustering

Shuheng Zhou

摘要

In this paper, we consider the problem of partitioning a small data sample of size $n$ drawn from a mixture of $2$ subgaussian distributions in $\mathbb{R}^p$. We consider semidefinite programming relaxations of an integer quadratic program that is formulated essentially as finding the maximum cut on a graph, where edge weights in the cut represent dissimilarity scores between two nodes based on their $p$ features. We define the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) as $s^2 := \min\{n p \gamma^2, \Delta^2\}$, where $\Delta^2 := p \gamma$ denotes the $\ell_2^2$ distance between the two cluster centers. For both balanced and unbalanced cases, we allow each population to have distinct covariance structures $\Sigma_1, \Sigma_2$ with diagonal matrices as special cases. Our contributions are twofold. First, we provide a unified framework for analyzing three computationally efficient algorithms: SDP1, BalancedSDP, and Spectral clustering, yielding universal polynomial-rate misclassification guarantees for all three algorithms. Moreover, our framework accom-modates a full range of tradeoffs between $n, p,$ and $\gamma$, allowing for partial recovery (success rate $\lt 100\%$) when $s^2$ is lower bounded by a constant. Second, we prove that the misclassification errors for SDP1 and BalancedSDP decay exponentially with respect to the SNR $s^2$ and the BalancedSDP requires no explicit debiasing when the two clusters have equal sizes. To our knowledge, this is the first time such results are obtained for semidefinite relaxations of MAXCUT in population clustering. We provide simulation evidence illuminating the theoretical predictions.

Self-selection bias in evaluating the effectiveness of mammography screening: A sensitivity analysis on data from the German screening program

Maria Veronica Vinattieri, Laura Buschmann, Jürgen Wellmann, André Karch, Heike Minnerup, Keith Humphreys

摘要

Self-selection bias in observational studies assessing the effectiveness of a breast cancer screening program can arise when non-participants of screening have worse general health conditions or lower compliance with treatments than screening participants. Building on recently described methodology for tumour progression modelling and causal survival analysis of screening data, our main contribution is to describe approaches to conduct sensitivity analyses for self-selection effects when studying screening effects on all-cause survival and breast cancer cumulative incidence, among screen-detected cases, in a population where all individuals are offered screening. Sensitivity analyses are inspired by an approach to correcting for self-selection bias that has been used for observational studies of mortality. We report simulation studies that demonstrate the impact that a correction factor has on all-cause survival and on the cumulative incidence of breast cancer death following diagnosis. We also apply our methods to estimate the benefit of early detection in non-overdiagnosed screen-detected cancers using data from the German mammography screening program. For sensitivity parameter values, we leverage on data on other (non-breast cancer) cause survival among participants and non-participants of mammography screening in the same study and estimate how counterfactual all-cause survival is improved, and breast cancer cumulative incidence is reduced for patients with screen-detected cancers, when accounting for self-selection bias.

Scale-Transformed Power Priors for External Data Integration in Longitudinal Studies

Md Tuhin Sheikh

摘要

Incorporating external data can improve precision in longitudinal studies, but effective borrowing requires that information from different studies be comparable. Within a Bayesian framework, power priors are widely used for external data integration through likelihood discounting. However, they assume that parameters are on a common information scale. In longitudinal settings, differences in follow-up duration, measurement variability, and correlation structure, along with random effects, can lead to differences in effective Fisher information across studies. This complicates parameter comparability and poses challenges for existing methods, which can result in biased or inefficient inference. We propose a scale-transformed power prior for external data integration in longitudinal studies. The method is developed within a linear mixed-effects modeling framework and rescales historical parameters to the current study scale using Fisher information before borrowing. Conditional Fisher information accounts for unobserved random effects and adjusts for study-specific variability. To address differences in variability and correlation structure, we implement a partial borrowing strategy that applies borrowing to fixed effects while allowing variance components to remain study-specific. We introduce a generalized formulation with an additive adjustment component to allow for remaining differences after scaling. Theoretical results characterize how differences in information scale lead to bias under conventional power priors and establish conditions under which the proposed method improves bias and precision. Simulation studies demonstrate improved performance and provide guidance on settings where scale transformation is beneficial. An application to prostate cancer clinical trial data demonstrates the importance of the proposed method.

Scalable finite mixture of regression models for clustering species responses in ecology

Francis K C Hui, Patricia Menéndez, Scott Foster, Skip Woolley

摘要

When modeling species assemblages in ecology, clustering species with similar environmental responses can facilitate a more parsimonious understanding of species compositions and improve the prediction of the overall community. One statistical method that implements this idea is species archetype models (SAMs), a type of finite mixture of regression model where species are grouped according to the shape of their environmental response. However, a current shortcoming of SAMs is their computational complexity, hindering their usefulness in cases involving hundreds or thousands of sites and/or species. In this paper, we propose approximate and scalable SAMs (asSAMs) to overcome this drawback. The key to asSAMs lies in constructing a quadratic approximation to individual species-archetype contributions to the marginal likelihood. This results in an objective function resembling a finite mixture of multivariate normal densities, which can be maximized via fully closed-form iterative updates. We demonstrate how asSAMs also promote fast uncertainty quantification using bootstrapping, and variable selection through an additional regularization penalty. Simulation studies and an application to presence-absence records of over 230 species from the Great Barrier Reef Seabed biodiversity project demonstrate that asSAMs can achieve strong estimation, selection, and predictive performance, while being computationally much faster than current methods for fitting SAMs. This makes asSAMs a practical approach for clustering species' environmental responses in high-dimensional species assemblage datasets.

Scalable Estimation of Crossed Random Effects Models via Multi-way Discretization

Shota Takeishi, Shonosuke Sugasawa

摘要

Cross-classified data frequently arise in scientific fields such as education, healthcare, and social sciences. A common modeling strategy is to introduce crossed random effects within a regression framework. However, this approach often encounters serious computational bottlenecks, particularly for non-Gaussian outcomes. In this paper, we propose a scalable and flexible method that approximates the distribution of each random effect by a discrete distribution, effectively partitioning the random effects into a finite number of representative values. This approximation allows us to express the model as a multi-way discrete structure, which can be efficiently estimated using a simple and fast iterative algorithm. The proposed method accommodates a wide range of outcome models and remains applicable even in settings with more than two-way cross-classification. We theoretically establish the consistency and asymptotic normality of the estimator under general settings of classification levels. Through simulation studies and real data applications, we demonstrate the practical performance of the proposed method in logistic, Poisson, and ordered probit regression models involving cross-classified structures.

Sample-Efficient Regret-Minimizing Double Oracle in Two-Player Zero-Sum Extensive-Form Games

Xiaohang Tang, Chiyuan Wang, Chengdong Ma, Ilija Bogunovic, Stephen McAleer, Yaodong Yang

摘要

Extensive-Form Game (EFG) represents a fundamental model for analyzing sequential interactions among multiple agents and the primary challenge to solve it lies in mitigating sample complexity. Existing research indicates that Double Oracle (DO) can reduce the sample complexity dependence on the information set number $|\mathcal{S}|$ to a restricted game size $X$ in solving two-player zero-sum EFGs. This is attributed to the early convergence of full-game Nash Equilibrium (NE) through iteratively solving restricted games. However, our analysis of Extensive-Form Double Oracle (XDO) yields a sample-complexity upper bound exponential in the number of restricted games, due to its exponentially increasing restricted game expansion frequency. Here we introduce Adaptive Double Oracle (AdaDO) to significantly alleviate sample complexity to polynomial by deploying the optimal expansion frequency. Furthermore, to comprehensively study the principles and influencing factors underlying sample complexity of DO, we introduce a novel theoretical framework Regret-Minimizing Double Oracle (RMDO) to provide directions for designing efficient DO algorithms. Empirical results demonstrate that AdaDO attains the superior approximation of NE with less sample complexity than the strong baselines including Linear CFR, MCCFR and existing DO. Importantly, combining RMDO with warm starting and stochastic regret minimization further improves convergence rate and scalability, thereby paving the way for addressing complex multi-agent tasks.

Sample size determination for randomized decentralized clinical trials with longitudinal outcomes

Chunyi Zhang, Ying Yuan, Ruitao Lin, Suyu Liu

摘要

Decentralized clinical trials (DCTs) offer significant advantages in enhancing trial accessibility, reducing patient burden, and improving population representativeness. These benefits are particularly pronounced in longitudinal trials, where patient outcomes are measured repeatedly over time. However, existing literature lacks appropriate sample size formulas tailored to longitudinal DCTs, which must account for heterogeneous measurement sources and increased uncertainty introduced by decentralized data collection. We propose a unified sample size calculation method for randomized longitudinal DCTs under the generalized linear model framework. The proposed formula is available in closed form and flexibly accommodates a variety of data collection designs, including the simple hybrid design, where all patients contribute both onsite and offsite data, and the stratified hybrid design, where different patient groups may undergo distinct data collection protocols. We also develop estimation procedures to estimate the unknown parameters and compute the sample statistics. Our method further generalizes to accommodate heterogeneous intraclass correlation coefficients, unequal variances between treatment arms, and varying numbers of data collection types or strata. Extensive simulation studies show that the proposed sample size formula achieves robust control of the type I error rate and maintains the desired power across a wide range of realistic scenarios.

Sample Size and Power Calculation for Continuous Outcomes in Open-Cohort Stepped Wedge Designs With Multiple Interventions

Shiyu Feng, Meng Zheng, Yi Zhang, Xuezhi Liang, Minglu Yuan, Wen Chen

摘要

Stepped wedge designs with multiple interventions (M-SWDs) are increasingly used to evaluate complex implementation strategies. In many such trials, participants enter and leave over time, giving rise to open-cohort sampling and complex correlation structures. Existing sample size and power calculation methods are largely restricted to single-intervention designs, closed cohorts, or simplified correlation assumptions. We develop a unified, parameter-based framework for sample size and power calculation in open-cohort M-SWDs. The proposed methodology accommodates several common multi-intervention designs, including concurrent, cross-over, supplementation, and factorial designs. Flexible open-cohort sampling schemes are incorporated to account for participant turnover, and both block-exchangeable and decay correlation structures are considered to capture within-cluster and within-individual dependence over time. Closed-form and generalized variance expressions for intervention effect estimators are derived, enabling efficient power calculation under a wide range of design settings. Extensive simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed formulas provide accurate power estimates, while further power analyses illustrate how the key correlation parameters and participant retention jointly influence power. This work provides practical and theoretically grounded guidance for planning multi-intervention stepped wedge trials under open-cohort sampling, facilitating more realistic and transparent design-stage decision-making.

SVarM: Linear Support Varifold Machines for Classification and Regression on Geometric Data

Emmanuel Hartman, Nicolas Charon

摘要

Despite progress in the rapidly developing field of geometric deep learning, performing statistical analysis on geometric data—where each observation is a shape such as a curve, graph, or surface—remains challenging due to the non-Euclidean nature of shape spaces, which are defined as equivalence classes under invariance groups. Building machine learning frameworks that incorporate such invariances, notably to shape parametrization, is often crucial to ensure generalizability of the trained models to new observations. This work proposes SVarM to exploit varifold representations of shapes as measures and their duality with test functions $h:\R^n \times S^{n-1} \rightarrow \R$. This method provides a general framework akin to linear support vector machines but operating instead over the infinite-dimensional space of varifolds. We develop classification and regression models on shape data sets by introducing a neural network-based representation of the trainable test function $h$. This approach demonstrates strong performance and robustness across various shape graph and surface data sets, achieving results comparable to state-of-the-art methods while significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters.

Robustness intervals for competing risks analysis with causes of failure missing not at random

Giorgos Bakoyannis, Aristofanis Rontogiannis, Ying Zhang, Wanzhu Tu, Ann Mwangi, Constantin T Yiannoutsos

摘要

Analysis of competing risks data is often complicated by incomplete or selectively missing information on the cause of failure. Standard approaches typically assume that the cause of failure is missing at random (MAR), an assumption that is generally untestable and frequently implausible in observational studies. We propose a novel sensitivity analysis framework for the proportional cause-specific hazards model that accommodates missing-not-at-random (MNAR) scenarios. A sensitivity parameter is used to quantify the association between missingness and the unobserved cause of failure. Regression coefficients are estimated as functions of this parameter, and a simultaneous confidence band is constructed via a wild bootstrap procedure. This allows identification of a range of MNAR scenarios for which effects remain statistically significant; we refer to this range as a robustness interval. The validity of the proposed approach is justified both theoretically, via empirical process theory, and empirically, through simulation studies. We apply the method to the analysis of data from an HIV cohort study in sub-Saharan Africa, where a substantial proportion of causes of failure are missing and the MAR assumption is implausible. The analysis shows that key findings regarding risk factors for care interruption and mortality are robust across a broad spectrum of MNAR scenarios, underscoring the method's utility in situations with MNAR causes of failure.

Robust transfer learning for accelerated failure time model

Chaoqi Wang, Jie Ding, Xiaoguang Wang

摘要

Limited sample sizes can impair the statistical accuracy of traditional survival models, resulting in potentially unsatisfactory conclusions. To address this issue, we propose a robust transfer learning framework for the accelerated failure time model (RTrans-AFT) that enables efficient and flexible information borrowing from an external source. The method accommodates both individual-level and summary-level source data and models differences in regression coefficients between cohorts without requiring identical covariate distributions. To control the degree of transfer, we impose an adaptive lasso penalty to control the discrepancy between the source and target parameters. This allows the method to selectively borrow information from the source cohort in the presence of heterogeneity, while fully incorporating external information when the two cohorts are comparable. Importantly, the framework is robust to potential data irregularities, such as outliers, which are common in real-world survival datasets. We establish the consistency and asymptotic normality of the proposed estimator. Simulation studies demonstrate that RTrans-AFT substantially improves estimation accuracy and achieves notable variance reduction, leading to enhanced statistical efficiency. The practical utility of the method is further illustrated through an analysis of lung adenocarcinoma data, where the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) registry data serve as the external source.

Residual connections provably mitigate oversmoothing in graph neural networks

Ziang Chen, Zhengjiang Lin, Shi Chen, Yury Polyanskiy, Philippe Rigollet

摘要

Graph neural networks (GNNs) have achieved remarkable empirical success in processing and representing graph-structured data across various domains. However, a significant challenge known as "oversmoothing" persists, where vertex features become nearly indistinguishable in deep GNNs, severely restricting their expressive power and practical utility. In this work, we analyze the asymptotic oversmoothing behavior of deep GNNs with and without residual connections by deriving explicit convergence rates for a normalized vertex similarity measure. Our analytical framework is grounded in the multiplicative ergodic theorem. Furthermore, we demonstrate that adding residual connections effectively mitigates or prevents oversmoothing across several broad families of parameter distributions. The theoretical findings are strongly supported by numerical experiments.

Reluctant Interaction Inference after Additive Modeling

Yiling Huang, Snigdha Panigrahi, Guo Yu, Jacob Bien

摘要

Additive models enjoy the flexibility of nonlinear models while still being readily understandable to humans. By contrast, other nonlinear models, which involve interactions between features, are not only harder to fit but also substantially more complicated to explain. Guided by the principle of parsimony, a data analyst therefore may naturally be reluctant to move beyond an additive model unless it is truly warranted. To put this principle of interaction reluctance into practice, we formulate the problem as a hypothesis test with a fitted sparse additive model (SPAM) serving as the null. Because our hypotheses on interaction effects are formed after fitting a SPAM to the data, we adopt a selective inference approach to construct p-values that properly account for this data adaptivity. Our approach makes use of external randomization to obtain the distribution of test statistics conditional on the SPAM fit, allowing us to derive valid p-values, corrected for the over-optimism introduced by the data-adaptive process prior to the test. Through experiments on simulated and real data, we illustrate that—even with small amounts of external randomization—this rigorous modeling approach enjoys considerable advantages over naive methods and data splitting.

Regression Adjusted Target Effects Using Auxiliary Strata Under Design-Based Inference

Zixuan Zhao, Yuehan Yang

摘要

We propose a design-based covariate adjustment that leverages information from auxiliary strata to improve the efficiency of average treatment effect estimation when target samples are sparse. The estimator borrows strength from related strata that may be correlated with the target, yielding gains for studies with limited observations or rare subpopulations. We present the estimator, develop a Neyman-style conservative variance estimator for hypothesis testing and confidence interval construction, and establish its large-sample properties. The procedure is straightforward to implement within standard randomization-based workflows and does not require a correctly specified outcome model or stochastic error assumptions. In simulations, the method delivers lower variance and narrower confidence intervals than conventional design-based or regression-adjusted estimators across a range of sparsity and correlation patterns. Applications to a schooling experiment and the ACTG 175 randomized trial illustrate practical gains in precision.

Probabilistic Truly Unordered Rule Sets

Lincen Yang, Matthijs van Leeuwen

摘要

Rule set learning has long been studied because individual rules can be directly read and understood by domain experts, making them particularly valuable in high-stakes decision-making settings. Existing methods have several shortcomings though. First, most existing methods impose an order among rules, either explicitly or implicitly, which makes the comprehension of any rule's condition depend on the conditions of preceding rules. Second, due to the difficulty of handling conflicts caused by overlaps (i.e., instances covered by multiple rules), existing methods often do not consider probabilistic rules. Third, learning classification rules for a multi-class target is understudied, as most existing methods focus on either a native binary classification task, or a multi-class task decomposed into binary tasks by adopting the one-versus-rest learning strategy. To address these shortcomings, we propose TURS, for Truly Unordered Rule Sets. To resolve conflicts caused by overlapping rules, we formulate a probabilistic model class based on rule sets that encourages overlap consistency—rules that overlap should give similar probabilistic outputs. We next formalize the problem of learning a TURS model based on the minimum description length (MDL) principle and develop a carefully designed heuristic algorithm. We benchmark against a wide range of rule-based methods and demonstrate that our method learns rule sets that have lower model complexity and highly competitive predictive performance.

PhiBE: A PDE-Based Bellman Equation for Continuous-Time Policy Evaluation

Yuhua Zhu

摘要

In this paper, we study policy evaluation in continuous-time reinforcement learning (RL), where the state follows an unknown stochastic differential equation (SDE) but only discrete-time data are available. We first highlight that the discrete-time Bellman equation (BE) is not always a reliable approximation to the true value function because it ignores the underlying continuous-time structure. We then introduce a new Bellman equation, PhiBE, which integrates the discrete-time information into a continuous-time PDE formulation. By leveraging the smooth structure of the underlying dynamics, PhiBE provides a provably more accurate approximation to the true value function, especially in scenarios where the underlying dynamics change slowly or the reward oscillates. Moreover, we extend PhiBE to higher orders, providing increasingly accurate approximations. We further develop a model-free algorithm for PhiBE under linear function approximation and establish its convergence under model misspecification, together with finite-sample guarantees. Directly applying classical discrete-time LSTD bounds to the continuous-time RL setting yields a model-misspecification bound with an $O({\Delta t}^{-1/2})$ stability factor and a sample-complexity bound of order $O({\Delta t}^{-2})$. By exploiting the smooth structure of the underlying dynamics, our analysis instead gives a model-misspecification bound with a stability factor independent of ${\Delta t}$ and a sample-complexity bound of order $O({\Delta t}^{-1})$. Moreover, we identify a fundamental trade-off between discretization error and sample error that is intrinsic to continuous-time policy evaluation: finer time discretization reduces bias but amplifies variance, so excessively frequent sampling does not necessarily improve performance. This is an insight that does not arise in classical discrete-time RL analyses. Numerical experiments are provided to validate the theoretical guarantees we propose.

Parsimonious modeling of category-specific effects in partial proportional odds models based on trees

Nikolai Spuck, Ingo Gräff, Moritz Berger

摘要

The proportional odds model is arguably the most widely applied regression approach for ordinal response variables. It assumes that the effects of the covariates are constant across the response categories allowing a parsimonious model specification. A violation of this assumption, however, may lead to biased effect estimates and decreased predictive ability. To overcome this issue, partial proportional odds models, where proportionality is only enforced for a smaller subset of covariates, can be applied. While statistical tests to check the proportional odds assumption are available, a generalized framework for data-driven selection of category-specific effects has not been established. In this paper, we introduce a novel tree-structured algorithm that forms clusters of categories sharing the same effect on the ordinal response, with a focus on model parsimony and ease of interpretation. The proposed model appeared to be superior to alternatives in a clinical application to data from the University Hospital Bonn on patients presenting in the emergency department. The predictive ability and selection properties of the proposed approach were also compared to alternative approaches, such as LASSO-penalized regression, in a simulation study.

P4MC: A Monte Carlo Classification Method Based on Partial Variables for Postoperative Complication Prediction

Lingyi Hu, Yao Cui, Qi Yang, Shan Tang, Shanjun Mao

摘要

Predicting postoperative complications is a critical task for improving patient care and optimizing resource management. We formulate the task as a binary classification problem based on two-stage clinical data: preoperative and intraoperative variables. A key difficulty is that intraoperative data are systematically missing for preoperative prediction. Existing methods, which typically rely solely on preoperative data or single intraoperative indicator imputation, generally fail to capture important latent intraoperative information. To provide a more robust solution, we propose P4MC (Preoperative, Partial intraoPerative, and Postoperative Monte Carlo classification). This approach explicitly models the dependence between the two stages to recover latent intraoperative information. Specifically, P4MC first applies partial sufficient dimension reduction (PSDR) to compress intraoperative variables into a low-dimensional sufficient reduction. We then estimate the conditional distribution of this reduction given preoperative variables using nonparametric density estimation and generate latent intraoperative features for classification via Monte Carlo sampling. A simulation study demonstrates the improved predictive performance and stability of P4MC when combined with standard machine learning methods. Applications to two clinical cohorts, laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy and hepatocellular carcinoma, further show that P4MC outperforms conventional approaches and yields clinically interpretable insights. An open-source R program is provided in the P4MC package, available at https://github.com/501fox/P4MC.git.

Nonparametric modelling of continuous-time Markov chains with scalable exact and approximate gradients
