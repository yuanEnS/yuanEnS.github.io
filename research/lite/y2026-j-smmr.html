SMMR Clinical Trials · 发表于 2026-11-12

Sample size estimation for pre- and post-intervention design with incomplete observations of count outcomes

Jijia Wang, Song Zhang, Chul Ahn

摘要

The pre- and post-intervention design is frequently utilized in medical and behavioral research, with each subject typically providing a pair of observations collected before and after the intervention. However, not all subjects furnish complete pairs of data. Some subjects may contribute either pre- or post-intervention information only. In this study, we proposed a sample size formula based on the generalized estimating equation (GEE) method for clinical trials where subjects are supposed to provide pre- and post-intervention measurements of a count outcome. The sample size formula is flexible to accommodate different missing patterns, magnitude of missingness and correlation. We compared it with the crude sample size adjustment approach for missing data, which is commonly used in practice and involves dividing the required sample size under complete observations by the proportion of subjects with complete data. Through simulation studies, we evaluated the empirical performance of the proposed sample size formula in preserving desired power and type I error. Furthermore, we presented a clinical trial example to demonstrate its application.

SMMR Causal Inference · 发表于 2026-11-10

Efficient estimation of average causal effect with mismeasured exposures

Zijian Sui, Min Zeng, Hong Zhang

摘要

Exposure mismeasurement is commonly seen in causal inference based on observational data. Recent evidence suggests that 35%–61% of medical studies using diagnostic tools report non-negligible exposure mismeasurement. For example, in the Global Enteric Multicenter Study, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA)-based rotavirus detection showed substantial discordance with polymerase chain reaction-based TaqMan Array Card (TAC) testing: 20.1% of ELISA-negative cases tested TAC-positive, whereas 19.5% of ELISA-positive cases tested TAC-negative. The existing methods handling exposure measurement error usually require that the mismeasurement distribution is known or can be estimated from a subset of study subjects with validated exposure statuses. This condition, however, is often violated when the mismeasurement mechanism is complex. In this article, we develop a novel method for estimating average causal effect using samples with exposures subject to misclassification and limited validation subsamples with correctly ascertained exposures. Our method does not require making any modeling assumptions about exposure mismeasurement. We establish some large-sample properties for the proposed method, based on which statistical inference can be easily carried out. The superiority of our proposed estimator is illustrated through extensive simulation studies. Our proposed estimator is shown to achieve a 39%–53% reduction in variance over the estimator relying solely on validation data in our simulation situations. In the application to the Global Enteric Multicenter Study, the proposed method reveals a substantial adjustment of pathogen-specific diarrhea associations that were previously distorted by inaccurate diagnoses.

SMMR Causal Inference · 发表于 2026-11-06

Heterogeneous treatment effect estimation with instrumental variable methods

Amir Aamodt Kazemi, Joseph Sexton, Inge Christoffer Olsen

摘要

Instrumental variable (IV) methods are widely used to address unmeasured confounding in observational studies but typically estimate the local average treatment effect (LATE) for compliers. If there exist effect modifying variables that also affect the choice of treatment, the LATE may have limited clinical relevance. To address this limitation, we propose a novel framework for estimating the conditional average treatment effect (CATE) for a categorical treatment variable in the presence of both observed confounding and effect-modifying variables in addition to valid IVs. Building on recent developments in data fusion between randomized trial and observational data, we combine IV estimators with estimators based on covariate adjustment. By making parametric assumptions about the residual unobserved confounding effect, we obtain a consistent estimator of the CATE with relatively high efficiency. In a simulation study with nonlinear effect modification and unobserved confounding, we show that our estimator outperforms competing IV and covariate-adjusted estimators in terms of mean squared error. We further illustrate the applicability of the method, utilizing it to compare tumor necrosis factor alpha inhibitors as the first line biologic treatment of rheumatoid arthritis conditional on age. The analysis illustrates how the proposed method can estimate population-relevant, clinically interpretable treatment effects and suggests smaller differences between treatments among older patients. This work provides a general framework for combining instrumental and confounding information to estimate individualized causal effects when treatment effects are heterogeneous and there are more than two treatment alternatives.

Self-selection bias in evaluating the effectiveness of mammography screening: A sensitivity analysis on data from the German screening program

Maria Veronica Vinattieri, Laura Buschmann, Jürgen Wellmann, André Karch, Heike Minnerup, Keith Humphreys

摘要

Self-selection bias in observational studies assessing the effectiveness of a breast cancer screening program can arise when non-participants of screening have worse general health conditions or lower compliance with treatments than screening participants. Building on recently described methodology for tumour progression modelling and causal survival analysis of screening data, our main contribution is to describe approaches to conduct sensitivity analyses for self-selection effects when studying screening effects on all-cause survival and breast cancer cumulative incidence, among screen-detected cases, in a population where all individuals are offered screening. Sensitivity analyses are inspired by an approach to correcting for self-selection bias that has been used for observational studies of mortality. We report simulation studies that demonstrate the impact that a correction factor has on all-cause survival and on the cumulative incidence of breast cancer death following diagnosis. We also apply our methods to estimate the benefit of early detection in non-overdiagnosed screen-detected cancers using data from the German mammography screening program. For sensitivity parameter values, we leverage on data on other (non-breast cancer) cause survival among participants and non-participants of mammography screening in the same study and estimate how counterfactual all-cause survival is improved, and breast cancer cumulative incidence is reduced for patients with screen-detected cancers, when accounting for self-selection bias.

Robust transfer learning for accelerated failure time model

Chaoqi Wang, Jie Ding, Xiaoguang Wang

摘要

Limited sample sizes can impair the statistical accuracy of traditional survival models, resulting in potentially unsatisfactory conclusions. To address this issue, we propose a robust transfer learning framework for the accelerated failure time model (RTrans-AFT) that enables efficient and flexible information borrowing from an external source. The method accommodates both individual-level and summary-level source data and models differences in regression coefficients between cohorts without requiring identical covariate distributions. To control the degree of transfer, we impose an adaptive lasso penalty to control the discrepancy between the source and target parameters. This allows the method to selectively borrow information from the source cohort in the presence of heterogeneity, while fully incorporating external information when the two cohorts are comparable. Importantly, the framework is robust to potential data irregularities, such as outliers, which are common in real-world survival datasets. We establish the consistency and asymptotic normality of the proposed estimator. Simulation studies demonstrate that RTrans-AFT substantially improves estimation accuracy and achieves notable variance reduction, leading to enhanced statistical efficiency. The practical utility of the method is further illustrated through an analysis of lung adenocarcinoma data, where the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) registry data serve as the external source.

SMMR Bayesian Methods High-Dimensional Genetics & Omics · 发表于 2026-11-04，收录于 2026-11-06

Improving gene-based analysis of rare variants through Bayesian variable selection

Jingxiong Xu, Nanwei Wang, Yonathan Brhane, Christopher I Amos, Laurent Briollais

摘要

A common approach for detecting rare variants (RVs) associated with complex human diseases is to perform a gene-based analysis or a region-based test of association. Typically, in a gene-based analysis, all the RVs pertaining to the gene considered are included in the statistical test, which potentially reduces its power since most RVs are likely not associated with the outcome of interest. As paradigm shift, we propose to add a variable selection step to choose the RVs that compose the gene-based test statistic as a way to enhance the power of the test. We propose a Bayes factor (BF) test statistic derived from the generalized linear model and its conjugate prior. A key component of our approach is the selection of the important RVs within a gene, which is performed through a birth-death Markov Chain Monte Carlo algorithm. Our approach can also leverage functional annotation at the RV level through the elicitation of the conjugate prior. Through simulation studies, we show that the proposed BF outperformed competing approaches both in terms of gene ranking and power to detect gene-based associations. The power of BF was improved by the use of the variable selection procedure and this gain of power was more substantial when functional annotation was included in this procedure. The application of our new approach to a large whole-exome sequencing data set comparing 1658 individuals with lung cancer to 1492 healthy controls was able to identify new genes associated with lung cancer and pointed towards interesting cancer-related pathways.

SMMR Clinical Trials Bayesian Methods · 发表于 2026-11-04，收录于 2026-11-06

Dynamic borrowing via covariate-adjusted self-adapting mixture priors in clinical trials

Yeonhee Park

摘要

Incorporating historical data into clinical trials can enhance statistical power, but it risks introducing bias if historical and current populations differ. Existing Bayesian borrowing methods often apply a single, prespecified weight to historical data, failing to account for patient-level covariate differences. To address this, we propose a covariate-adjusted self-adapting mixture (CASAM) prior, a Bayesian framework that enables covariate-dependent dynamic borrowing from external data. CASAM adaptively determines the degree of information borrowing by assessing the compatibility between historical and current data conditional on covariates, automatically down-weighting historical information when residual heterogeneity is detected. To further address covariate distribution mismatch between historical and current trials, we develop a propensity-score-integrated implementation of CASAM, which first re-weights historical controls to align their covariate distribution with that of the current control arm before applying the CASAM prior. This layered strategy allows CASAM to focus on structural similarity while the propensity-score adjustment corrects for population-level imbalance. Together, CASAM and its propensity-score-based implementation provide an empirical-Bayes framework for adaptive borrowing that combines covariate alignment with outcome-model compatibility assessment.

Can discrete-time analyses be trusted for stepped wedge trials with continuous recruitment?

Hao Wang, Guangyu Tong, Heather Allore, Kelsey L Grantham, Monica Taljaard, Fan Li

摘要

In stepped wedge cluster randomized trials (SW-CRTs), the intervention is rolled out to clusters sequentially over multiple periods. It is common practice to analyze data from SW-CRTs using linear mixed models that treat time as discrete. However, a recent systematic review found that 95.1% of cross-sectional SW-CRTs recruit individuals continuously over time. Despite the prevalence of continuous recruitment, there has been limited guidance on how to draw model-robust inference for such designs. In this article, we conduct simulation studies to examine the performance of discrete-time linear mixed models in the case of continuous recruitment designs with a continuous outcome. Specifically, we generate outcomes under a continuous-time exponential decay correlation structure with either discrete or continuous fixed period effects. We consider constant and exposure-time-dependent intervention effects, with and without random intervention effects across clusters. We then analyze the simulated data using linear mixed models under three discrete-time working correlation structures: simple exchangeable, nested exchangeable, and discrete-time exponential decay, with robust sandwich variance estimation. Our results demonstrate that discrete-time analysis often yields negligible bias and that the robust variance estimator with the Mancl and DeRouen correction consistently achieves nominal coverage and type I error rate. However, when recruitment patterns vary systematically between control and intervention periods, discrete-time analyses give slightly biased intervention effect estimates. Finally, we illustrate these findings by reanalyzing a completed SW-CRT.

Enhancing visual interpretability and explainability in functional survival trees and forests

Giuseppe Loffredo, Elvira Romano, Fabrizio Maturo

摘要

Functional survival models are key tools for analyzing time-to-event data with complex predictors, such as functional or high-dimensional inputs. Despite their predictive strength, these models often lack interpretability, which limits their value in practical decision-making and risk analysis. This study investigates two key survival models: the functional survival tree (FST) and the functional random survival forests (FRSFs). It introduces novel methods and tools to enhance the interpretability of FST models and improve the explainability of FRSF ensembles. Applications to simulated data and to a real intensive care dataset illustrate that the proposed approaches provide post-hoc graphical and functional summaries that facilitate the interpretation of FSTs and the explanation of forest-level predictions.

SMMR Optimization & Learning Theory · 发表于 2026-10-25，收录于 2026-10-26

A seamless dose-optimization design for monotherapy and combination therapy

Kentaro Takeda, Masahiro Kojima

摘要

The emergence of molecular-targeted agents and immune-oncology therapies has fundamentally transformed oncology drug development, necessitating evolution beyond traditional dose-finding approaches designed for cytotoxic agents. While conventional agents exhibit predictable monotonic dose-response relationships, novel anticancer agents often demonstrate plateau-effect patterns where higher doses may compromise therapeutic benefit, requiring identification of optimal biological doses that balance efficacy and tolerability. The FDA's Project Optimus initiative emphasizes comprehensive dose optimization through parallel randomized cohorts and patient backfilling to better understand pharmacological profiles across multiple dose levels. Contemporary drug development increasingly prioritizes combination therapy alongside monotherapy evaluation, yet existing designs typically assume equivalent roles for both agents, diverging from clinical practice where novel agents combine with established treatments having limited dose options. This article proposes a seamless dose-optimization design that adaptively evaluates both monotherapy and combination therapy based on efficacy and toxicity outcomes through adaptive subtrials with patient backfilling capabilities. The model-assisted framework employs predetermined Bayesian optimal boundaries, eliminating real-time model fitting while accommodating evaluation of both monotherapy and combination therapy and enabling sequential enrollment with strategic backfilling. Simulation studies demonstrate robust performance across diverse dose-response patterns relevant to contemporary oncology. The design addresses critical gaps between methodological assumptions and clinical reality, offering a practical approach that integrates monotherapy and combination therapy evaluation with efficacy-toxicity-based backfilling. This framework ensures straightforward implementation while maintaining flexibility for complex dose-response relationships characteristic of novel anticancer agents, providing significant advantages for clinical implementation in the era of targeted and immune-oncology therapeutics.

Joint modeling of longitudinal measurements and survival outcomes in heart valve transplantation data

Avinash Kumar, M S Panwar, Sushil Kumar

摘要

A joint modeling technique for longitudinal data and time-to-event data is advanced statistical methodology to analyze repeated measurements and time-to-event outcomes. In the present article, a shared random effects approach is considered to account for both longitudinal data and survival outcomes. The longitudinal data is submodeled by a linear mixed-effects model that captures the individual response over time. Whereas the time-to-event sub-model uses the generalized exponential distribution as the baseline distribution for the proportional hazard model to incorporate survival times as well as time-independent covariates. To estimate model parameters, the Expectation-Maximization approach has been used, which is effective with latent random effects since random effects are unobservable in nature. In the E-step, the expected log-likelihood is derived based on current parameter estimates, and in the M-step, we maximize this likelihood to update the parameters. To show the efficacy of the proposed approach, an extensive simulation study has been conducted. Furthermore, the model is tested on a real-world medical dataset, heart valve data, to demonstrate its efficacy in analyzing the link between longitudinal responses and survival outcomes.

A multivariate Bayesian biomarker screening algorithm that incorporates clinical covariates

Maria Veronica Vinattieri, Federico Panero, Qingchun Jin, Anna Sf Lok, Francesco C Stingo, Nabihah Tayob

摘要

Advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has limited treatment options and poor survival. Early detection of HCC improves survival, but current ultrasound screening is inconsistent and poorly adhered to. Biomarker-based blood tests offer a more accurate, accessible, and reliable alternative. Recent developments in methods for early detection of HCC have focused on the trajectories of multiple biomarkers, assuming that biomarkers follow a joint hierarchical mixture model with random changepoints. We extend this model, allowing covariates to influence three aspects of the model: biomarker trajectories, diagnosis times, and subject-specific prior risks. We evaluated whether covariates could capture unexplained heterogeneity in biomarker levels to improve early detection. A Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm was implemented to derive posterior distributions used in risk calculations among future patients and to determine whether a patient has a positive screen. The screening algorithm was evaluated through simulation studies under a range of possible covariate extensions. The Hepatitis C Antiviral Long-term Treatment against Cirrhosis (HALT-C) trial was used to further study the proposed biomarker screening algorithms for HCC using cross-validation.

Treatment comparisons using a measurement during follow-up and a right-censored time-to-event outcome

Michal Kicinski

摘要

In clinical trials, the comparison between treatment arms of a measurement is often complicated by an event such as death or progression that can make this measurement irrelevant or difficult to perform. In this article, an approach is proposed to compare treatment arms using the net treatment benefit or the win ratio. A more favorable outcome (win) for the experimental arm is defined as the event of (1) a longer time to event for a patient in the experimental arm compared to a patient from the control arm until a prespecified restriction time ϵ or (2) a more favorable outcome with respect to the measurement during follow-up for a patient from the experimental arm and the time to event greater than ϵ for both patients. An estimator is proposed. A simulation study showed approximately no bias. The methodology is illustrated using a leukemia trial.

SMMR Survival Analysis Nonparametric & Semiparametric · 发表于 2026-10-15，收录于 2026-10-26

ROC-based methods for comparing two survival curves under non-proportional hazards with right, left, and doubly censored data

Mohammod Mahmudur Rahman, Leonidas E Bantis

摘要

Studies comparing two survival curves to assess treatment differences are commonplace. The widely used log-rank test is designed to operate best under the proportional hazards (PH) assumption. Alternative methods, such as the Fleming-Harrington weighted log-rank tests, rely on prior knowledge of survival patterns, which is often difficult to specify before a study. The recently developed MaxCombo test does not require such prior information; however, it leaves substantial room for power improvement, particularly when survival curves cross. To address these issues, we propose new nonparametric and semiparametric methods for comparing two survival curves based on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve length and the overlap coefficient, along with a joint inference approach combining both measures. Our methods are applicable under non-PH settings and can accommodate right, left, and double censoring. We evaluate our methods through extensive simulation studies under different non-PH configurations. Our results show improved performance of the proposed methods compared to existing nonparametric methods, particularly under early and crossing effects. Real-world examples involving various non-PH scenarios are provided, illustrating the superiority of our methods in detecting survival differences. We further provide an R package named ''ROCsurvcomp" that implements all our proposed approaches and is available on CRAN.

SMMR Missing Data & Measurement Error Clinical Trials Longitudinal Data · 发表于 2026-10-15，收录于 2026-10-26

Cluster-level analysis in three-level cluster randomised trials with multi-level covariate-dependent missingness

Satish Pradhan, Priyanka Majumder

摘要

Community-based public health interventions often use three-level cluster randomised trials (CRTs), for example, individuals are nested within households which are again nested within communities. In these trials, outcome missingness commonly depends on baseline covariates measured at multiple levels. Although two-level CRTs are well studied, the effects of subcluster-level attrition and hierarchical covariate correlations on bias and variance are not yet fully explored. We develop an analytical framework for three-level CRTs under multi-level covariate-dependent missingness (CDM), explicitly incorporating within- and between-subcluster covariate correlations. We examine the performance of unadjusted and covariate-adjusted cluster-level treatment effect estimators using Monte Carlo simulations and a plasmode simulation based on the 2021 Madagascar Demographic and Health Survey. Our study reveals that unadjusted cluster-level analyses produce biased estimates under multi-level CDM. Linear mixed models are unbiased when correctly specified under missing-at-random assumptions but may be computationally unstable in small samples. The covariate-adjusted cluster-level estimator remains unbiased when covariate effects and missingness mechanisms are homogeneous across treatment arms. Subcluster-level attrition can lead to greater variance inflation than individual-level attrition. For CRTs with fewer than 30 clusters per arm and homogeneous treatment effects, covariate-adjusted cluster-level analysis may serve as a practical alternative to mixed models. A decision flowchart is provided to guide method selection.

Mixture cure rate model with artificial neural network for interval-censored data

Junyao Ren, Shishun Zhao, Dianliang Deng, Shuwei Li

摘要

Mixture cure rate models represent a valuable tool to delineate the presence of a cured subgroup in the entire population under study. Among the vast literature, a popular research line is to regress the probability of an individual being cured on covariates through a parametric model, such as the logistic model. However, when the assumed parametric model is mis-specified, one could only attain a biased parameter estimate. In this work, motivated by the robustness and powerful approximation ability of neural networks, we offer a flexible mixture cure rate modeling approach for analyzing interval-censored data, which arise frequently in many scientific fields involving periodic follow-up or cross-sectional screening. In particular, we utilize the artificial neural network to model the cured probability and the proportional hazards model to characterize the latent event time distribution related to uncured individuals. After approximating the cumulative baseline hazard function with monotone splines, we develop a stable expectation-maximization algorithm coupled with a multiple imputation strategy and resilient backpropagation to locate the sieve maximum likelihood estimator. We establish the consistency and convergence rate of the proposed sieve estimator, as well as the asymptotic normality and semiparametric efficiency of the regression parameter estimator. Simulation experiments demonstrate that our proposed method works well and substantially outperforms the comparative methods. We then apply the proposed method to a real-world data set, revealing new findings and a better predictive performance.

A Bayesian prevalence incidence cure model for estimating survival using electronic health records with incomplete baseline diagnoses

Matilda Pitt, Robert JB Goudie

摘要

Retrospective cohorts can be extracted from electronic health records (EHRs) to study prevalence, time until disease or event occurrence, and cure proportion in real-world scenarios. However, EHRs are collected for patient care rather than research, and thus typically have complexities, such as patients with missing baseline disease status. Prevalence incidence (PI) models, which use a two-component mixture model to account for this missing data, have been proposed. However, PI models are biased in settings in which some individuals will never experience the endpoint (they are ‘cured’). To address this, we propose a prevalence-incidence-cure (PIC) model, a three-component mixture model that combines the PI model framework with a cure model. Our PIC model enables estimation of the prevalence rate, time-to-incidence, and the cure proportion, and allows for covariate effects on each component. We adopt a Bayesian inference approach, and focus on the interpretability of the prior. We show in a simulation study that the PIC model has smaller bias than PI and cure models for the survival probability; and we compare inference under vague, informative and misspecified priors. We illustrate our PIC model using a dataset of 1964 patients who underwent treatment for diabetic macular oedema, and demonstrate improved fit under our model.

SMMR Causal Inference Bayesian Methods · 发表于 2026-10-01

A flexible Monte Carlo quantitative bias analysis for unmeasured confounding

Emily Kawabata, Chin Yang Shapland, Tom Palmer, David Carslake, Kate Tilling, Rachael Hughes

摘要

Quantitative bias analysis (QBA) for unmeasured confounding is essential for observational studies. A probabilistic QBA incorporates external information about the unmeasured confounders via prior distributions for certain parameters (known as bias parameters ) that specify the relationship between the unmeasured confounders, U , and the study data. It can be implemented as a Bayesian or Monte Carlo QBA. Software implementations of probabilistic QBAs to unmeasured confounding are scarce and mainly limited to unadjusted analyses of binary variables. We propose a Monte Carlo QBA applicable to a generalised linear model or survival proportional hazards model that allows for: (i) binary, continuous, or categorical exposure and measured confounders C , (ii) dependence between C and U , and (iii) one or multiple binary or continuous U . To minimise the number of bias parameters, our proposed bias model does not model U directly but instead models the part of U not explained by C . Using a data example and simulation studies, we show that our Monte Carlo QBA, with informative priors, can virtually eliminate the bias due to unmeasured confounding in a wide range of scenarios and performs as well as a Bayesian QBA. Our Monte Carlo QBA will be available as Stata command and R package, qbaconfound .

SMMR Bayesian Methods Nonparametric & Semiparametric · 发表于 2026-10-01

A Bayesian adaptive shift-mixture approach for estimating incubation period and exposure time in small outbreaks

Daisuke Yoneoka, Takayuki Kawashima, Yuta Tanoue, Shuhei Nomura, Akifumi Eguchi

摘要

Rapid estimation of infection time and the incubation-period distribution is essential for evidence-based quarantine and contact tracing. However, outbreak data typically mix primary and higher-generation cases with unobserved exposure times. We propose the infection-induced adaptive shifted-knots Dirichlet process mixture (IASK-DPM) model, a nonparametric Bayesian framework applying a Dirichlet process prior on the shift parameter in a three-parameter lognormal kernel. Incorporating infectious dynamics, the base measure is derived from a kernel-density estimate of observed onset times, concentrating mixture components where the epidemic curve is dense while preserving support in the left tail, where earlier exposure may plausibly lie. The proposed framework is intended primarily for small-outbreak or early-phase epidemic settings in which infections are concentrated around a limited number of dominant transmission episodes or narrow exposure windows. Posterior inference uses Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and is implemented in R/Stan . In nine Monte Carlo scenarios representing COVID-19, measles, and O-157 outbreaks, IASK-DPM reduced mean squared error in shift parameter estimates (i.e. exposure time) by 35% to 70% and in the 95th incubation percentile by 30% to 68%, relative to fixed finite-mixture models. These gains persisted even when the competing Dirichlet-process model used a conventional base measure. Applied to the 2015 Korean MERS outbreak, the method recovered four exposure dates (4.7–19.5 days after May 10, 2015), coinciding with field-investigated superspreading events, and estimated a posterior median incubation of 5.8 days (95th percentile 13.7 days), aligning with exhaustive contact tracing. By allowing the data, rather than the analyst, to determine latent exposure generations, IASK-DPM enables accurate inference on key epidemic parameters with minimal tuning.

A regularized semiparametric cure-rate model with high-dimensional imaging data

Jiahui Feng, Muye Nanshan, Haolun Shi, Kin Yau Wong, Kwok Fai Lam, Jiguo Cao

摘要

Structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is informative for studying progression from mild cognitive impairment (MCI) to Alzheimer’s disease (AD), but incorporating high-dimensional images into survival models requires methods that accommodate irregular brain domains and yield interpretable spatial effects. We propose a regularized semiparametric mixture cure-rate model for high-dimensional imaging predictors that separates susceptibility to AD conversion from time to AD among susceptible subjects. Baseline MRI images are represented by bivariate Bernstein spline basis functions over a triangulation of the brain domain, and sparsity-inducing penalties are imposed on imaging effects in both incidence and latency components. This construction enables direct estimation of localized coefficient functions describing how image regions are associated with susceptibility and conversion time. We develop an expectation—maximization algorithm that updates latent susceptibility probabilities and solves penalized logistic and Cox regression subproblems, together with an efficient tuning strategy. Simulations show that the method can recover localized active regions and provide useful incidence and latency discrimination. An application to Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative data demonstrates susceptibility discrimination and interpretable brain-region coefficient maps.

Generalizing the two sources capture–recapture estimator for covariate information

Patarawan Sangnawakij, Rattana Lerdsuwansri, Parawan Pijitrattana, Peter Schlattmann, Antonello Maruotti, Dankmar Böhning

摘要

Dual system estimation is a statistical tool used to estimate the hidden population where each of the two sources covers part of the population. In estimation, the widely used Lincoln–Petersen and Chapman estimators are used under the assumption of two-source independence. However, this situation is often unrealistic, and the traditional estimators might have a large bias, especially if the two sources experience a large dependence. To improve the methodology, covariate information is used for stratification. We formulate the strata-adjusted Chapman estimator for estimating the population size. Furthermore, the log-linear modeling approach is adopted to cope with covariate information. Two semi-parametric (imputed) bootstrap methods using the log-linear model with and without incorporating the model selection procedure are proposed to quantify the uncertainty surrounding the estimates of the unknown population size. Across all designs, methods that exploit covariate information—either via stratification or within the log-linear modeling framework—consistently reduce bias and mean squared error relative to unadjusted estimators that ignore heterogeneity. For interval estimation, the imputed bootstrap that incorporates model selection in each replicate achieves coverage close to the nominal level across heterogeneous and sparse regimes, typically outperforming both the fixed-model bootstrap and normal-theory intervals for the strata-adjusted Chapman. We illustrate these approaches in an application to estimate the hidden number of suicides in Cambodia and heroin users in Thailand.

A new bivariate cure rate model and its application to diabetic retinopathy

Matilda Pitt, Katherine Davies

摘要

Modeling the time until an event occurs is often of interest, and in particular when two events occur to one subject, often modeled via bivariate distributions. Further scenarios occur where some subjects in the population are cured and will not experience the event(s) of interest, often modeled via a cure rate mixture model. Various bivariate cure rate mixture models exist, with this article developing a further model using the Moran–Downton Weibull distribution. This article suggests a new bivariate cure rate model, derives and develops an EM algorithm to fit the model and applies it to a dataset on diabetic retinopathy to suggest insight on the effect of laser treatment.

Doubly robust estimation of causal survival effects via inverse probability of treatment and censoring weighting and semi-parametric AFT models with variable selection

Chien-Lin Su

摘要

Clinical trials and observational studies frequently encounter survival data with right censoring and high-dimensional confounders. Adaptive design strategies for ethical and efficient clinical trials using simulated annealing

Rosamarie Frieri, Francesco Mariani, Marco Novelli

摘要
