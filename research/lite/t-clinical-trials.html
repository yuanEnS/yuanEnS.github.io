Sample size determination for randomized decentralized clinical trials with longitudinal outcomes

Chunyi Zhang, Ying Yuan, Ruitao Lin, Suyu Liu

摘要

Decentralized clinical trials (DCTs) offer significant advantages in enhancing trial accessibility, reducing patient burden, and improving population representativeness. These benefits are particularly pronounced in longitudinal trials, where patient outcomes are measured repeatedly over time. However, existing literature lacks appropriate sample size formulas tailored to longitudinal DCTs, which must account for heterogeneous measurement sources and increased uncertainty introduced by decentralized data collection. We propose a unified sample size calculation method for randomized longitudinal DCTs under the generalized linear model framework. The proposed formula is available in closed form and flexibly accommodates a variety of data collection designs, including the simple hybrid design, where all patients contribute both onsite and offsite data, and the stratified hybrid design, where different patient groups may undergo distinct data collection protocols. We also develop estimation procedures to estimate the unknown parameters and compute the sample statistics. Our method further generalizes to accommodate heterogeneous intraclass correlation coefficients, unequal variances between treatment arms, and varying numbers of data collection types or strata. Extensive simulation studies show that the proposed sample size formula achieves robust control of the type I error rate and maintains the desired power across a wide range of realistic scenarios.

Sample Size and Power Calculation for Continuous Outcomes in Open-Cohort Stepped Wedge Designs With Multiple Interventions

Shiyu Feng, Meng Zheng, Yi Zhang, Xuezhi Liang, Minglu Yuan, Wen Chen

摘要

Stepped wedge designs with multiple interventions (M-SWDs) are increasingly used to evaluate complex implementation strategies. In many such trials, participants enter and leave over time, giving rise to open-cohort sampling and complex correlation structures. Existing sample size and power calculation methods are largely restricted to single-intervention designs, closed cohorts, or simplified correlation assumptions. We develop a unified, parameter-based framework for sample size and power calculation in open-cohort M-SWDs. The proposed methodology accommodates several common multi-intervention designs, including concurrent, cross-over, supplementation, and factorial designs. Flexible open-cohort sampling schemes are incorporated to account for participant turnover, and both block-exchangeable and decay correlation structures are considered to capture within-cluster and within-individual dependence over time. Closed-form and generalized variance expressions for intervention effect estimators are derived, enabling efficient power calculation under a wide range of design settings. Extensive simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed formulas provide accurate power estimates, while further power analyses illustrate how the key correlation parameters and participant retention jointly influence power. This work provides practical and theoretically grounded guidance for planning multi-intervention stepped wedge trials under open-cohort sampling, facilitating more realistic and transparent design-stage decision-making.

Dynamic borrowing via covariate-adjusted self-adapting mixture priors in clinical trials

Yeonhee Park

摘要

Incorporating historical data into clinical trials can enhance statistical power, but it risks introducing bias if historical and current populations differ. Existing Bayesian borrowing methods often apply a single, prespecified weight to historical data, failing to account for patient-level covariate differences. To address this, we propose a covariate-adjusted self-adapting mixture (CASAM) prior, a Bayesian framework that enables covariate-dependent dynamic borrowing from external data. CASAM adaptively determines the degree of information borrowing by assessing the compatibility between historical and current data conditional on covariates, automatically down-weighting historical information when residual heterogeneity is detected. To further address covariate distribution mismatch between historical and current trials, we develop a propensity-score-integrated implementation of CASAM, which first re-weights historical controls to align their covariate distribution with that of the current control arm before applying the CASAM prior. This layered strategy allows CASAM to focus on structural similarity while the propensity-score adjustment corrects for population-level imbalance. Together, CASAM and its propensity-score-based implementation provide an empirical-Bayes framework for adaptive borrowing that combines covariate alignment with outcome-model compatibility assessment.

Can discrete-time analyses be trusted for stepped wedge trials with continuous recruitment?

Hao Wang, Guangyu Tong, Heather Allore, Kelsey L Grantham, Monica Taljaard, Fan Li

摘要

In stepped wedge cluster randomized trials (SW-CRTs), the intervention is rolled out to clusters sequentially over multiple periods. It is common practice to analyze data from SW-CRTs using linear mixed models that treat time as discrete. However, a recent systematic review found that 95.1% of cross-sectional SW-CRTs recruit individuals continuously over time. Despite the prevalence of continuous recruitment, there has been limited guidance on how to draw model-robust inference for such designs. In this article, we conduct simulation studies to examine the performance of discrete-time linear mixed models in the case of continuous recruitment designs with a continuous outcome. Specifically, we generate outcomes under a continuous-time exponential decay correlation structure with either discrete or continuous fixed period effects. We consider constant and exposure-time-dependent intervention effects, with and without random intervention effects across clusters. We then analyze the simulated data using linear mixed models under three discrete-time working correlation structures: simple exchangeable, nested exchangeable, and discrete-time exponential decay, with robust sandwich variance estimation. Our results demonstrate that discrete-time analysis often yields negligible bias and that the robust variance estimator with the Mancl and DeRouen correction consistently achieves nominal coverage and type I error rate. However, when recruitment patterns vary systematically between control and intervention periods, discrete-time analyses give slightly biased intervention effect estimates. Finally, we illustrate these findings by reanalyzing a completed SW-CRT.

Calibrated Bayes Analysis of Cluster-Randomized Trials

Ruyi Liu, Joshua L Warren, Yuki Ohnishi, Donna Spiegelman, Liangyuan Hu, Fan Li

摘要

In cluster-randomized trials (CRTs), entire clusters of individuals are randomized to treatment, and outcomes within a cluster are typically correlated. While frequentist approaches are standard practice for CRT analysis, Bayesian methods have emerged as a strong alternative. Previous work has investigated the use of Bayesian hierarchical models for continuous, binary, and count outcomes in CRTs, but these approaches focus on model-based treatment effect coefficients as the target estimands, which may have ambiguous interpretation under model misspecification and informative cluster size. In this article, we introduce a calibrated Bayesian procedure for estimand-aligned analysis of CRTs even in the presence of potentially misspecified models. We propose estimators targeting both the cluster-average treatment effect (cluster-ATE) and individual-average treatment effect (individual-ATE), particularly in scenarios with informative cluster sizes. We additionally explore strategies for summarizing the posterior samples that can achieve the frequentist coverage even under working model misspecification. We provide simulation evidence to demonstrate the model-robustness property of the proposed estimators in CRTs, and further investigate the impact of covariate adjustment as well as the use of more flexible Bayesian nonparametric working models in the CRT context.

Which Small-Sample Correction Should be Used When Analyzing Stepped-Wedge Designs With Time-Varying Treatment Effects?

Yongdong Ouyang, Monica Taljaard, James P Hughes, Fan Li

摘要

Stepped-wedge cluster randomized trials (SW-CRTs) evaluate interventions that are sequentially rolled out across clusters. Standard analyses of SW-CRTs typically use immediate-treatment (IT) models, which assume the treatment effects begin at crossover and remain constant thereafter. When treatment effects vary with exposure duration, IT models may misrepresent the target effects. Exposure-time indicator (ETI) models address this by allowing treatment effects to differ by time since exposure and can properly target the time-averaged treatment effect (TATE) and long-term effect (LTE). Like IT models, ETI models require specification of a random-effects structure, which may be misspecified. While robust variance estimators (RVEs) theoretically protect against such misspecification, their empirical performance under ETI models is not well understood. We review RVEs for ETI models and evaluate them in comprehensive simulation studies with continuous and binary outcomes under correctly specified (binary only) and misspecified random-effects structures. We compare the classic sandwich variance, Kauermann-Carroll (KC), Mancl-DeRouen (MD), and Morel-Bokossa-Neerchal (MBN) bias-corrected sandwich variance estimators for inference on the TATE and LTE in primary simulations and additionally evaluate Fay-Graubard (FG) in a sub-study. Our simulations show that under misspecified random-effects structures, model-based standard errors (SE) produced undercoverage, whereas RVEs improved performance under the ETI models. For continuous outcomes, MD with a t-distribution and degrees of freedom equal to the number of clusters minus two gave the closest to nominal coverage probabilities. Surprisingly, however, MBN was the only consistently reliable option for binary outcomes. MD, however, could be unstable in one-cluster-per-sequence designs because of data sparsity. Across scenarios, both model-based SE and RVE for LTE were unstable, indicating that greater caution is needed when targeting LTE under ETI models.

Treatment comparisons using a measurement during follow-up and a right-censored time-to-event outcome

Michal Kicinski

摘要

In clinical trials, the comparison between treatment arms of a measurement is often complicated by an event such as death or progression that can make this measurement irrelevant or difficult to perform. In this article, an approach is proposed to compare treatment arms using the net treatment benefit or the win ratio. A more favorable outcome (win) for the experimental arm is defined as the event of (1) a longer time to event for a patient in the experimental arm compared to a patient from the control arm until a prespecified restriction time ϵ or (2) a more favorable outcome with respect to the measurement during follow-up for a patient from the experimental arm and the time to event greater than ϵ for both patients. An estimator is proposed. A simulation study showed approximately no bias. The methodology is illustrated using a leukemia trial.

Estimating Marginal Odds Ratios for Cluster Randomized Trials: The Cluster-Level GLM

Jennifer A Thompson, Brennan C Kahan, Andrew Copas

摘要

BACKGROUND: Methods for estimating marginal effects in cluster randomized trials are limited. We describe and evaluate a cluster-level generalized linear model (CL-GLM) that applies a GLM to cluster-level summaries (means, proportions, or rates) and can estimate differences, ratios, or odds ratios. We focus on odds ratios. METHODS: We conducted a simulation study with 10-50 clusters, intracluster correlation coefficients of 0.05 or 0.15, and a mean cluster size of 120, either fixed or variable. Informative cluster size, where the intervention effect varies with cluster size, was also considered. Control arm prevalence was set to 50%. We compared cluster-level GLMs with normal and gamma distributions and logit link to generalized estimating equations (GEE) and independence estimating equations (IEEs). Weighted and unweighted analyses were examined to target cluster-average and individual-average estimands. RESULTS: CL-GLM with a normal distribution produced unbiased marginal cluster-average odds ratios with appropriate confidence interval coverage. CL-GLM with a gamma distribution resulted in over-coverage. Unweighted IEE produced unbiased marginal individual-average odds ratios with correct coverage. GEE was unbiased when cluster size was uninformative but biased under informative cluster size. Weighted CL-GLM led to under-coverage, whereas weighted IEE resulted in over-coverage. The CL-GLM had similar power to GEE and unweighted IEE. CONCLUSIONS: The cluster-level GLM with a normal distribution provides a robust alternative to estimating equation approaches for marginal odds ratios in cluster randomized trials, with good performance under both informative and uninformative cluster size and no loss of power. We recommend the cluster-level GLM with a normal distribution when the marginal cluster-average estimand is of interest, and unweighted IEEs when the marginal individual-average estimand is of interest.

Cluster-level analysis in three-level cluster randomised trials with multi-level covariate-dependent missingness

Satish Pradhan, Priyanka Majumder

摘要

Community-based public health interventions often use three-level cluster randomised trials (CRTs), for example, individuals are nested within households which are again nested within communities. In these trials, outcome missingness commonly depends on baseline covariates measured at multiple levels. Although two-level CRTs are well studied, the effects of subcluster-level attrition and hierarchical covariate correlations on bias and variance are not yet fully explored. We develop an analytical framework for three-level CRTs under multi-level covariate-dependent missingness (CDM), explicitly incorporating within- and between-subcluster covariate correlations. We examine the performance of unadjusted and covariate-adjusted cluster-level treatment effect estimators using Monte Carlo simulations and a plasmode simulation based on the 2021 Madagascar Demographic and Health Survey. Our study reveals that unadjusted cluster-level analyses produce biased estimates under multi-level CDM. Linear mixed models are unbiased when correctly specified under missing-at-random assumptions but may be computationally unstable in small samples. The covariate-adjusted cluster-level estimator remains unbiased when covariate effects and missingness mechanisms are homogeneous across treatment arms. Subcluster-level attrition can lead to greater variance inflation than individual-level attrition. For CRTs with fewer than 30 clusters per arm and homogeneous treatment effects, covariate-adjusted cluster-level analysis may serve as a practical alternative to mixed models. A decision flowchart is provided to guide method selection.

Bayesian Methods Incorporating Baseline Covariates for Borrowing Historical Control Data in Randomized Clinical Trials: A Neutral Comparison Study

David Jesse, Diane Uschner, Markus C Elze, Tim Friede, Francois Mercier

摘要

The idea of leveraging historical control data in randomized clinical trials (RCTs) has gained a lot of attention, in particular for rare disease and pediatric indications where conducting traditional and fully powered RCTs can be challenging. In this regard, Bayesian dynamic borrowing (BDB) methods, which adapt the amount of borrowing based on the outcome heterogeneity between the current and historical data, are well established. In recent years, an increasing number of related methods have been published that combine the BDB framework with approaches that account for baseline covariates. The underlying rationale is that some of the heterogeneity may be explained systematically by variations in the distributions of these characteristics. While many such methods are now available, direct comparisons of them are scarce and there is no consensus on which one can be considered the state of the art. We address this issue by conducting a neutral comparison of three methods of this class, namely the propensity score weighted commensurate prior, propensity score weighted multi-source exchangeability models, and Bayesian additive regression trees. We assess their performance for continuous outcomes through extensive simulations and further evaluate them using two examples based on real RCT data. Our study reveals potential issues with some of these approaches and provides recommendations for choosing a particular method for real RCTs.

Addressing Missing Data in Clinical Trials With a Hybrid Control Arm

Joshua Wasserman, Herbert Pang, Jiawen Zhu

摘要

Randomized trials have long been the standard for evaluating experimental treatments in medical and pharmacological studies, but the associated time and cost have spurred research into designs that augment randomized studies with data from external sources. Hybrid control arms offer the promise of improved precision-at the possible expense of bias-by forming a control arm from patients randomized to control along with historical controls or patients found in a registry. Although recent reviews have published guidance on minimizing and addressing missing data in clinical trials, the issue has received little attention in the context of trials with a hybrid control arm. We propose novel treatment effect estimators that handle missing outcomes in both the trial and external control datasets, even when the missingness patterns differ between them. These estimators are built from augmented weighted estimating equations and are doubly robust when outcomes are missing at random (MAR). In addition, we introduce a sensitivity analysis using a pattern-mixture model to assess robustness of the effect estimates under violations of the MAR assumption. We compare these estimators to a popular parametric modeling technique through simulation and an application to a real-world trial.

Valid Data-Driven Subgroup Selection With Uniform Guarantees in Clinical Trials

Manuel M Müller, Björn Bornkamp, Frank Bretz, Timothy I Cannings, Wei Liu, Henry W J Reeve, Richard J Samworth, Nikolaos Sfikas, Fang Wan, Konstantinos Sechidis

摘要

Subgroup selection in clinical trials is essential for identifying patient groups that react differently to a treatment, thereby enabling personalized medicine. In particular, subgroup selection can identify patient groups that respond particularly well to a treatment or that encounter adverse events more often. However, this is a post-selection inference problem, which may pose challenges for traditional techniques used for subgroup analysis, such as increased Type I error rates and potential biases from data-driven subgroup identification. In this paper, we discuss two recently developed methods for subgroup selection in regression problems: one based on generalized linear modeling and another on isotonic regression. We demonstrate how these methods can be used for data-driven subgroup identification in the analysis of clinical trials, focusing on two distinct tasks: identifying patient groups that are safe from manifesting adverse events and identifying patient groups with high treatment effect, while controlling for Type I error in both cases. A thorough simulation study is conducted to evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of each method, providing detailed insight into the sensitivity of the Type I error rate control to modeling assumptions.

Using Joint Models in Phase I Dose-Finding Designs in Oncology: Considerations for Frequentist Approaches

Xijin Chen, Pavel Mozgunov, Richard D Baird, Thomas Jaki

摘要

Dose-finding trials for oncology studies are traditionally designed to assess safety in the early stages of drug development. With the rise of molecularly targeted therapies and immuno-oncology compounds, biomarker-driven approaches are increasingly importance. In this article, we propose a novel approach to dose-finding that incorporates biomarkers which predict for the subsequent development of clinical toxicity. The proposed joint model framework, which incorporates repeated biomarker measurements, is compared to the likelihood-based continual reassessment method (CRM) using only binary toxicity data, across various dose-toxicity relationship scenarios. Our findings highlight a critical limitation of likelihood-based approaches in early-phase dose-finding studies with small sample sizes: estimation challenges that have been previously overlooked in the phase I dose-escalation setting. We explore potential remedies to address these challenges and emphasize the appropriate use of likelihood-based methods. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed joint model framework, by integrating biomarker information, can alleviate estimation problems in the likelihood-based CRM and improve the proportion of correct selection. However, we highlight that the inherent data limitations in early phase dose-finding studies remain a significant challenge that cannot be fully overcome in the frequentist framework.

Nothing to See Here? A Non-Inferiority Approach to Parallel Trends

Alyssa Bilinski, Laura A Hatfield

摘要

Difference-in-differences is a popular method for observational health policy evaluation. It relies on a causal assumption that in the absence of intervention, treatment groups' outcomes would have evolved in parallel to those of comparison groups. Researchers frequently look for parallel trends in the pre-intervention period to bolster confidence in this assumption. The popular "parallel trends test" evaluates a null hypothesis of parallel trends and, failing to find evidence against the null, concludes that the assumption holds. This tightly controls the probability of falsely concluding that trends are not parallel, but may have low power to detect non-parallel trends. When used as a screening step, it can also introduce bias in treatment effect estimates. We propose a non-inferiority/equivalence approach that tightly controls the probability of missing large violations of parallel trends, measured on the scale of the treatment effect. Our framework nests several common use cases, including linear trend tests and event studies. We show that our approach may induce no or minimal bias when used as a screening step under commonly assumed error structures and, absent violations, can offer a higher-power alternative to testing treatment effects in more flexible models. We illustrate our ideas by reconsidering a study of the impact of the Affordable Care Act's dependent coverage provision.

Mendelian Randomization Methods for Causal Inference: Estimands, Identification and Inference

Minhao Yao, Anqi Wang, Xihao Li, Zhonghua Liu

摘要

Mendelian randomization (MR) has become an essential tool for causal inference in biomedical and public health research. By using genetic variants as instrumental variables, MR helps address unmeasured confounding and reverse causation, offering a quasi-experimental framework to evaluate causal effects of modifiable exposures on health outcomes. Despite its promise, MR faces substantial methodological challenges, including invalid instruments, weak instrument bias, and design complexities across different data structures. In this tutorial review, we aim to provide a systematic overview of MR methods for causal inference, emphasizing clarity of causal interpretation, study design comparisons, availability of software tools, and practical guidance for applied scientists. We organize the review around causal estimands, ensuring that analyses are anchored to well-defined causal questions. We discuss the problems of invalid and weak instruments, comparing available strategies for their detection and correction. We integrate discussions of population-based versus family-based MR designs, analyses based on individual-level versus summary-level data, and one-sample versus two-sample MR designs, highlighting their relative advantages and limitations. We also summarize recent methodological advances and software developments that extend MR to settings with many weak or invalid instruments and to modern high-dimensional omics data. Real-data applications, including UK Biobank and Alzheimer's disease proteomics studies, illustrate the use of these methods in practice. This review aims to serve as a tutorial-style reference for both methodologists and applied scientists.

Group Sequential Sample Size for Comparing Two Survival Probabilities at a Specific Time Point

Susan Halabi, Lu Liu, Chenxi Yu, Yuan Wu

摘要

We propose a novel method that simultaneously determines the sample size for testing two survival probabilities at a pre-specified time while guaranteeing Type I error control in both fixed and group-sequential trial designs. Simulations across varying hypothesized differences, failure distributions, censoring proportions, and nominal powers demonstrate consistent performance, while interim analyses highlight maintained nominal Type I error control and increased power at each look relative to the standard binomial approach, regardless of the underlying failure time distribution or spending function. Importantly, our method is especially useful for evaluating survival outcomes at a fixed time in randomized trials where one treatment arm includes neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgery while the other involves surgery alone. Furthermore, it is advantageous when the proportional hazards assumption is not satisfied, as often occurs in immunotherapy trials with delayed or time-varying treatment effects or crossing survival curves. The method is also applicable to randomized Phase II trials, where smaller sample sizes and the use of intermediate or surrogate time-to-event endpoints demand efficient data use and robust error control. We illustrate the approach with motivating examples in renal and prostate cancer. An accompanying R Shiny application enables investigators to compute sample sizes interactively, facilitating practical trial planning in diverse settings.

Generalizing Conditional Average Treatment Effects From Nested Randomized Trials to Trial-Eligible Individuals

Lan Wen, Issa J Dahabreh, Yu-Han Chiu

摘要

Randomized controlled trials often enroll participants whose characteristics differ from those of a target population. When the distribution of effect modifiers differs between trial participants and the target population, treatment effects estimated within the trial may not be directly applicable to all trial-eligible individuals. Although substantial progress has been made in generalizing average treatment effects from randomized trials to target populations, clinical and policy decisions are often informed by conditional average treatment effects (CATEs) that characterize heterogeneity across selected effect modifiers in the target population. In this work, we provide a unified approach for estimating CATEs in a target population of trial-eligible individuals under a nested trial design. Our approach first estimates nuisance functions using data-adaptive methods and constructs pseudo-outcomes from conditional influence functions. These pseudo-outcomes are subsequently regressed on pre-specified effect modifiers using local linear (kernel) regression to obtain a smoothed estimate of the CATE function. We establish asymptotic properties under sample splitting and cross-fitting, and provide conditions under which first-stage nuisance estimation has negligible impact on the final estimator. We assess the finite-sample performance of the proposed estimator via simulation studies, and illustrate its practical use through an application to the Coronary Artery Surgery Study (CASS).

Evaluating Informative Cluster Size in Cluster Randomized Trials

Bryan S Blette, Zhe Chen, Brennan C Kahan, Andrew Forbes, Michael O Harhay, Fan Li

摘要

In cluster randomized trials, the individual-average treatment effect (i-ATE) can be different from the cluster-average treatment effect (c-ATE) when informative cluster size (ICS) is present, that is, when treatment effects or participant outcomes depend on cluster size. In such scenarios, mixed-effects models and generalized estimating equations (GEEs) with exchangeable correlation structure are biased for both the i-ATE and c-ATE estimands, whereas GEEs with an independence correlation structure or analyses of cluster-level summaries are recommended in practice. However, when cluster size is non-informative, mixed-effects models and GEEs with exchangeable correlation structure can provide unbiased estimation and notable efficiency gains over other methods. Thus, hypothesis tests for ICS would be useful to assess this key phenomenon under cluster randomization. In this work, we develop model-based, model-assisted, and randomization-based tests for ICS in cluster randomized trials. We conduct simulation studies to examine the operating characteristics of these tests, show they have appropriate Type I error rate control and meaningful power, and contrast them to existing model-based tests used in the observational study setting. The proposed tests are then applied to data from a recent cluster randomized trial, and practical recommendations for using these tests are discussed.

Estimands and Doubly Robust Estimation for Cluster-Randomized Trials With Survival Outcomes

Xi Fang, Bingkai Wang, Liangyuan Hu, Fan Li

摘要

Cluster-randomized trials (CRTs) are experimental designs where groups or clusters of participants, rather than the individual participants themselves, are randomized to intervention groups. Analyzing CRT requires distinguishing between treatment effects at the cluster level and the individual level, which requires a clear definition of the estimands under a causal inference framework. For analyzing survival outcomes, it is common to assess the treatment effect by comparing survival functions or restricted mean survival times (RMSTs) between treatment groups. In this article, we formally characterize cluster-level and individual-level treatment effect estimands with right-censored survival outcomes in CRTs and propose doubly robust estimators for targeting such estimands. Under censoring dependent on baseline covariates, our estimators ensure consistency when either the censoring model or the outcome model is correctly specified, but not necessarily both. We explore different modeling options for the censoring and outcome models to estimate the censoring and survival distributions, and investigate a deletion-based jackknife method for variance and interval estimation. Extensive simulations demonstrate that the proposed methods perform adequately in finite samples. Finally, we illustrate our method by analyzing a completed CRT with survival endpoints.

Clinically Interpretable Time-To-Event Testing in Randomized Clinical Trials: Milestone Survival Combined With Restricted Mean Survival Time

Siwen He, Ludovic Trinquart

摘要

The log-rank test and hazard ratio (HR) are widely used to compare survival curves in event-driven randomized clinical trials (RCTs). The optimality of the log-rank test and interpretation of the HR rely on the proportional hazards (PH) assumption, which is frequently violated in oncology due to delayed, waning, or crossing hazards. Although combination procedures built from weighted log-rank tests improve robustness to non-PH, hazard-based summaries remain difficult to interpret clinically. We propose combination tests that integrate two clinically interpretable estimands: the between-group difference in milestone survival probability S ( τ ) and in restricted mean survival time (RMST) μ ( 0 , τ ) . Using Monte Carlo simulations under a milestone-based design (uniform follow-up to τ ) and supplementary event-driven designs, we compared the proposed combinations with the log-rank and MaxCombo tests across diverse survival patterns, sample sizes, and censoring rates. We evaluated Type I error and power. The milestone combination procedures showed rejection probabilities close to the nominal level under the estimand-null configurations examined and showed competitive to superior power in non-PH settings, including transient effects and crossing hazards. Applications to three oncology RCTs illustrate that, when HRs hover near unity while absolute estimands differ, the milestone combinations can detect benefits at clinically meaningful horizons. These findings support aligning design, estimands, and analysis around a prespecified milestone. Careful selection of τ is essential to balance information and interpretability. Milestone-based combination tests provide a viable, patient-centered alternative or complement to HR-centric methods, particularly when non-PH is anticipated.

A Bayesian Approach to Estimate Causal Average Treatment Effects Under Unmeasured Confounding

Jinghong Zeng

摘要

One major bias source in causal inference for clinical trials is unmeasured confounding. We propose an innovative, practical Bayesian modeling approach to adjust for unmeasured confounding effects and obtain precise causal average treatment effect estimates for two-arm randomized controlled clinical trials. This approach includes model reparameterization and an iterative algorithm, with a causal inference framework incorporated with unmeasured confounders and related statistical distributions. Model non-identifiability resulting from adjusting for unmeasured confounding is a major inferential problem. Reparameterization transforms one or multiple unmeasured confounders into a single reparameterized unmeasured confounder and can remove model non-identifiability from the model specification of unmeasured confounders. The iterative algorithm consists of detailed steps for inference after model reparameterization and can remove model non-identifiability from prior sensitivity to unmeasured confounders. It includes iterating the prior distribution of the reparameterized unmeasured confounder by certain rules, aggregating posterior means and variances over different prior choices, and obtaining posterior estimates for the average treatment effect. Its essential idea is to make unreliable prior information on unmeasured confounders as close to data information as possible. Compared with usual methods, our approach produces robust effect estimates and correctly concludes statistical significance. From an example using real clinical data, this approach effectively adjusts for confounding effects when we do not adjust for measured confounders. Our approach is also generalizable to other clinical study designs and may be beneficial to applications where data collection is difficult for certain variables or causal relationships are not well understood.

Statistical Inference with Mixed-Effect Model for Covariate-Adaptive Randomized Experiments

Yang Liu, Lucy Xia, Feifang Hu

摘要

Recent clinical studies increasingly involve a long factor with many levels, e.g., investigation sites, resulting in a large number of strata that must be accounted for either through design or subsequent analysis. This complication has prompted concerns from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA, 2023) regarding the adequacy of standard statistical methods, whose performance may deteriorate, and their properties become unclear when the number of strata is relatively large. In this work, we offer a first-time rigorous solution by employing mixed-effect models in covariate-adaptive randomized experiments.
