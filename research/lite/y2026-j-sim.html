Reproducible Life‐Expectancy Eligibility Criteria for Oncology Studies: Uncertainty Quantification for Machine Learning‐Derived Survival Screening Rules

Vladimir Svetnik, Matteo Sesia, Michael Johnson, Peining Tao, Mehmet Burcu

摘要

Life‐expectancy eligibility criteria appear in approximately one quarter of oncology trials but typically rely on subjective clinician judgment and cannot be replicated in retrospective electronic health record studies. Survival prediction models provide a natural basis for objective, data‐driven operationalization of such criteria, but their practical use is complicated by four challenges: Possible model miscalibration; the need to quantify uncertainty, including about the realized operating characteristics in a random future cohort; the need to support flexible selection of the model and screening threshold by a clinician after inspecting the data; and the right‐censoring intrinsic to retrospective survival data. We propose a bootstrap‐based method that addresses all four challenges using an independent, right‐censored calibration dataset from the target population. It constructs confidence and prediction bands for cohort yield and positive predictive value as functions of the screening threshold, jointly valid over any desired collection of models and thresholds, and therefore compatible with exploratory, clinician‐driven analysis. The method is model‐agnostic, treating each survival model as an arbitrary black box without requiring it to be well calibrated. We apply it to data from over 47 000 patients with advanced non‐small cell lung cancer from the Flatiron Health Research Database, across different survival models and prediction horizons, and find that it yields stable and interpretable screening rules with reliable uncertainty quantification for future cohort‐level performance.

Selection Bias in Hybrid Randomized Controlled Trials Using External Controls: A Simulation Study

Han Chang Chiam, Franz König, Martin Posch

摘要

Hybrid randomized controlled trials (hybrid RCTs) integrate external control data, such as historical or concurrent data, with data from randomized trials. While numerous frequentist and Bayesian methods, such as the test‐then‐pool and the meta‐analytic‐predictive prior, have been developed to account for potential disagreement between the external control and randomized data, they cannot ensure strict Type I error rate control. However, these methods can reduce biases stemming from systematic differences between external controls and trial data. A critical yet underexplored issue in hybrid RCTs is the prespecification of external data to be used in the analysis. The validity of statistical conclusions in hybrid RCTs depends on the assumption that external control selection is independent of historical trial outcomes. In practice, historical data may be accessible during the planning stage, potentially influencing important decisions, such as which historical datasets to include or the sample size of the prospective part of the hybrid trial, thus introducing bias. Such data‐driven design choices can be an additional source of bias, which can occur even when historical and prospective controls are exchangeable. Through a simulation study, we quantify the bias introduced by outcome‐dependent selection of historical controls in hybrid RCTs using both Bayesian and frequentist approaches, and discuss potential strategies to mitigate this bias. Our scenarios consider variability and time trends in the historical studies, distributional shifts between historical and prospective control groups, sample sizes and allocation ratios, as well as the number of studies included. The impact of different hypothetical selection mechanisms for selecting external controls is demonstrated using a clinical trial example.

SIMEX –Trans: High‐Dimensional Transfer Learning With Covariates Subject to Measurement Error

Kaida Cai, Zhengyan Wang, Hanning Chen, Hanwen Liu

摘要

Gene expression studies typically involve small sample sizes, high‐dimensional covariates, and measurement error, posing significant challenges for accurate statistical modeling. We propose simulation–extrapolation (SIMEX)–based high‐dimensional transfer learning methods (SIMEX–Trans) that (i) correct measurement error and (ii) leverage information from informative auxiliary studies. We consider settings where the informative auxiliary studies are either known or unknown, and develop Lasso‐ and SCAD‐penalized estimators. When the informative studies are known, we establish support recovery and asymptotic normality for the SIMEX‐based SCAD‐penalized estimator. Simulation results show that SIMEX–Trans outperforms competing methods in estimation accuracy and achieves reliable variable selection performance. In a GTEx brain‐tissue analysis of cross‐tissue prediction of hypothalamus JAM2 expression with limited target samples and heterogeneous auxiliary tissues, the proposed SIMEX–Trans–Selector approach achieves the lowest cross‐validated prediction error and remains stable under sensitivity analysis.

Improving Multi‐Trait Polygenic Risk Score Prediction of Drug Response Using Fine‐Mapping and Ensemble Learning

Song Zhai, Bin Guo, Judong Shen

摘要

Multi‐trait Polygenic Risk Score (mtPRS) methods improve prediction accuracy compared to single‐trait PRS (stPRS) by integrating genetically correlated traits. Current mtPRS methods primarily use three approaches: (1) aggregating genome‐wide association studies (GWAS) summary statistics; (2) combining individual PRSs with weights from Principal Component Analysis (PCA); or (3) combining individual PRSs with multivariate or penalized regression‐derived weights. However, existing mtPRS methods face two main challenges: (1) selected variants are often non‐causal, lowering prediction power; and (2) no method is universally superior across diverse genetic architectures and multi‐trait correlation structures. To improve prediction performance and robustness, we propose mtPRS‐FIMEL (multi‐trait Polygenic Risk Score using FIne‐Mapping and Ensemble Learning), an ensemble learning approach that integrates fine‐mapping with multi‐trait PRS construction. mtPRS‐FIMEL computes mtPRS by combining multiple complementary mtPRS and stPRS methods and strategically leveraging causal variants identified through fine‐mapping analysis. Specifically, it uses GWAS summary statistics to estimate causal effects and learn optimal weights for a set of PRSs from complementary mtPRS (including mtPRS‐PCA, a PCA based method, and mtPRS‐ML, an elastic‐net based machine learning method, and stPRS). Our extensive simulations demonstrate that mtPRS‐FIMEL outperforms existing mtPRS and stPRS methods in terms of prediction accuracy, PRS association strength, and patient stratification performance in the simulation settings examined. Validation with PGx GWAS data from a randomized clinical trial successfully predicted the drug response of low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‐C) change from baseline, while integrating three LDL‐C‐related traits (LDL, HDL, and Triglycerides) from disease GWAS, confirming its promising performance and robustness via a proof‐of‐concept internally cross‐validated approach.

Orthogonal Factorial Designs for Trials of Therapist‐Delivered Interventions: Randomizing Intervention‐Therapist Combinations to Patients

R. E. A. Walwyn, R. A. Bailey, A. Singh, N. Corrigan, S. G. Gilmour

摘要

It is recognized that treatment‐related clustering should be allowed for in the sample size and analyses of individually‐randomized parallel‐arm trials that evaluate therapist‐delivered interventions such as psychotherapy. Here, interventions are a treatment factor, but therapists are not. If the aim of a trial is to separate effects of therapists from those of interventions, we propose that interventions and therapists should be regarded as two potentially interacting treatment factors (one fixed and one random) with a factorial structure. We consider the specific design where each therapist delivers each intervention (crossed therapist‐intervention design), and the resulting therapist‐intervention combinations are randomized to patients. We adopt a classical Design of Experiments (DoE) approach to propose a family of orthogonal factorial designs and their associated data analyses, which allow for therapist learning and center too. We set out the associated data analyses using ANOVA and regression and report the results of a small simulation study conducted to explore the performance of the proposed randomization methods in estimating the intervention effect and its standard error, the between‐therapist variance and the between‐therapist variance in the intervention effect. We conclude that more purposeful trial design has the potential to lead to better evidence on a range of complex interventions and outline areas for further methodological research.

Leveraging Relational Evidence: Population Size Estimation on Tree‐Structured Data With the Weighted Multiplier Method

Mallory J. Flynn, Paul Gustafson

摘要

Populations of interest are often hidden from data for a variety of reasons, though their magnitude remains important in determining resource allocation and appropriate policy. One popular approach to population size estimation, the multiplier method, is a back‐calculation tool requiring only a marginal subpopulation size and an estimate of the proportion belonging to this subgroup. Another approach is to use Bayesian methods, which are inherently well‐suited to incorporating multiple data sources. However, both methods have their drawbacks. A framework for applying the multiplier method which combines information from several known subpopulations has not yet been established; Bayesian models, though able to incorporate complex dependencies and various data sources, can be difficult for researchers in less technical fields to design and implement. Increasing data collection and linkage across diverse fields suggests accessible methods of estimating population size with synthesized data are needed. We propose an extension to the well‐known multiplier method which is applicable to tree‐structured data, where multiple subpopulations and corresponding proportions combine to generate a population size estimate via the minimum variance estimator. The methodology and resulting estimates are compared with those from a Bayesian hierarchical model, for both simulated and real world data. Subsequent analysis elucidates which data are key to estimation in each method, and examines robustness and feasibility of this new methodology.

Biomarkers Selection and Combination Based on the Weighted Youden Index

Ao Sun, Zhanwang Deng, Jiahui Zhao, Hang Li, Xiao‐Hua Zhou

摘要

In clinical practice, multiple biomarkers are used for disease diagnosis, but their individual accuracies are often suboptimal, with only a few proving directly relevant. Effectively selecting and combining biomarkers can significantly improve diagnostic accuracy. Existing methods often optimize metrics like the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) or the Youden index. However, optimizing AUC does not yield estimates for optimal cutoff values, and the Youden index assumes equal weighting of sensitivity and specificity, which may not reflect clinical priorities where these metrics are weighted differently. This highlights the need for methods that can flexibly accommodate such requirements. In this article, we present a novel framework for selecting and combining biomarkers to maximize a weighted version of the Youden index. We introduce a smoothed estimator based on the weighted Youden index and propose a penalized version using the SCAD penalty to enhance variable selection. To handle the non‐convexity of the objective function and the non‐smoothness of the penalty, we develop an efficient algorithm, also applicable to other non‐convex optimization problems. Simulation studies demonstrate the performance and efficiency of our method, and we apply it to construct a diagnostic scale for dermatitis.

Bayesian Hierarchical Piecewise‐Exponential Cause‐Specific Hazards for Posterior Predictive Validation of Competing‐Risks Models

Hao Chen, Clara Grazian

摘要

Competing‐risks prediction requires validation methods that assess predictive support for both event time and event type. This is difficult for cause‐specific hazard models because out‐of‐sample log‐scores require positive, well‐defined hazards over the validation horizon, and rare event causes can lead to unstable cause‐specific estimates. We develop the Bayesian hierarchical piecewise‐exponential cause‐specific hazards model (), a competing‐risks model designed for posterior predictive validation using cross‐validated log‐scores. The model specifies positive piecewise‐exponential baseline hazards for each cause and places hierarchical priors on cause‐specific regression coefficients, allowing covariate effects to borrow information across causes while retaining cause‐specific interpretation. Predictive performance is evaluated using cross‐validated posterior predictive log‐scores under the cause‐specific hazards representation. Simulation studies show that the Bayesian piecewise‐exponential framework improves out‐of‐sample predictive support and model‐selection performance across settings with varying signal strength, sample size, censoring, cause imbalance, and cross‐cause heterogeneity. Hierarchical pooling provides the largest benefit when cross‐cause heterogeneity is low to moderate, event causes are imbalanced, or information for rare causes is limited. When true cause‐specific effects are highly heterogeneous, the advantage of partial pooling diminishes, reflecting the expected bias–variance trade‐off. A Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) colorectal cancer application further demonstrates the practical utility of the method: the treatment‐extended specification evaluated with achieves the best predictive performance, and hierarchical pooling narrows posterior credible intervals for selected rare‐cause coefficients. These findings support as a stable Bayesian posterior predictive framework for imbalanced multi‐cause competing‐risks prediction.

A Practical Tutorial on Interpreting Machine Learning Models in Precision Medicine Using SHAP, LIME, and Large Language Models

Zihuan Liu, Xin Huang

摘要

Machine learning (ML) models are increasingly used in precision medicine to predict outcomes, prioritize candidate biomarkers, and support treatment decision‐making. Despite their growing adoption, clinical interpretability remains challenging because different optimization objectives correspond to distinct statistical estimands, while post hoc explanation methods often produce complex outputs that are difficult for nontechnical users to interpret. Therefore, frameworks are needed to clarify what model outputs represent and translate technical explanations into clinically meaningful language. To demonstrate how clinical practitioners can select appropriate models, loss functions, and interpretability techniques, we present a tutorial designed to enhance the interpretability of supervised ML models in clinical settings. Our framework integrates feature attribution methods with large language models (LLMs). We first clarify how commonly used loss functions define distinct clinical objectives, ranging from prognostic outcome prediction to individualized treatment rule (ITR) estimation. We then introduce SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and local interpretable model‐agnostic explanations (LIME) as complementary approaches for global and local interpretation, illustrating how their visualizations summarize model‐based feature contributions. To reduce the programming burden of implementing end‐to‐end ML workflows, we develop an interactive Shiny application that guides users through data input, model training, feature attribution, and LLM‐assisted interpretation. Once installed and configured, users can perform these analyses through a graphical interface without writing analysis code. LLMs serve as an interpretation layer that translates model objectives, predictions, and SHAP/LIME results into concise, clinically meaningful explanations. We also evaluate the reproducibility, faithfulness, and sensitivity of these LLM‐assisted explanations.

A Markovian SuASR Epidemic Framework With Stochastic External Factors

Vasileios E. Papageorgiou, Georgios Vasiliadis

摘要

Epidemic waves are often shaped by uncertain external drivers, such as environmental variability or behavioral and policy changes, that can randomly amplify or dampen transmission. Capturing this randomness requires models whose dynamics evolve under time‐varying, stochastic influences. In this paper, we introduce a stochastic susceptible–asymptomatic–symptomatic–removed (SuASR) framework that embeds random external factors directly into the epidemic mechanism through an extended continuous‐time Markov chain (CTMC). The proposed framework is flexible enough to represent epidemic dynamics both with reinfection and with infection‐induced immunity, depending on the epidemiological setting under study. Unlike standard Markovian epidemic models, our formulation explicitly tracks two concurrent infectious sources (asymptomatic and symptomatic), yielding coupled infection pathways and substantially richer transient behavior. A key contribution is analytical tractability, where we derive computable expressions for the joint distribution of asymptomatic and symptomatic infection‐event counts and develop tools tailored to extreme‐outbreak risk, including the distribution of the maximum infectious burden. These results enable probabilistic quantification of epidemic severity beyond mean trajectories, providing direct estimates of the likelihood of rare but consequential surges. We validate the model through an extensive sensitivity analysis and an application to the H1N1 influenza epidemic, illustrating how stochastic external influences reshape outbreak profiles, total infections from both sources, and the probability of extreme epidemic scenarios.

A Constrained Maximum Likelihood Method for Pleiotropy‐Robust Mendelian Randomization Using Family‐Based GWAS Summary Statistics

Jia‐Hao Mai, Shun Zhang, Qiu‐Wen Zhu, Wei‐Ying Zhou, Yang Zhang, Jun‐Da Yu, Ji‐Yuan Zhou

摘要

Mendelian randomization (MR) using genome‐wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics is widely used for causal inference. However, conventional MR methods relying on population‐based GWAS are vulnerable to biases arising from family‐level confounders. While family‐based GWAS (FGWAS) designs offer a solution to address these effects, existing MR methodologies are not optimized for the unique structure of FGWAS data, often resulting in reduced statistical power. Therefore, in this paper, we propose the MRcML‐Family (MR based on constrained maximum likelihood for families), a novel method specifically tailored for FGWAS summary statistics. The core innovation of MRcML‐Family lies in its unified framework which simultaneously integrates genetic information from offspring (direct effects) and parents (indirect effects) as instrumental variables. By applying different constraints, the method effectively identifies and corrects for horizontal pleiotropy. Comprehensive simulation studies demonstrate that MRcML‐Family yields nearly unbiased causal estimates and maintains robust Type I error rate control whether or not the instrument strength independent of direct effect assumption holds, even in challenging scenarios with a limited number of SNPs and small FGWAS sample sizes. Furthermore, real‐world data analyses using UK Biobank data show that MRcML‐Family provides causal estimates highly consistent with existing literature and recovers the established “gold standard” relationship between height and body mass index. Additionally, it showed higher statistical power than the compared methods in the evaluated scenarios, mitigating the efficiency loss typically associated with family‐based designs.

Regression Adjusted Target Effects Using Auxiliary Strata Under Design-Based Inference

Zixuan Zhao, Yuehan Yang

摘要

We propose a design-based covariate adjustment that leverages information from auxiliary strata to improve the efficiency of average treatment effect estimation when target samples are sparse. The estimator borrows strength from related strata that may be correlated with the target, yielding gains for studies with limited observations or rare subpopulations. We present the estimator, develop a Neyman-style conservative variance estimator for hypothesis testing and confidence interval construction, and establish its large-sample properties. The procedure is straightforward to implement within standard randomization-based workflows and does not require a correctly specified outcome model or stochastic error assumptions. In simulations, the method delivers lower variance and narrower confidence intervals than conventional design-based or regression-adjusted estimators across a range of sparsity and correlation patterns. Applications to a schooling experiment and the ACTG 175 randomized trial illustrate practical gains in precision.

Sample Size and Power Calculation for Continuous Outcomes in Open-Cohort Stepped Wedge Designs With Multiple Interventions

Shiyu Feng, Meng Zheng, Yi Zhang, Xuezhi Liang, Minglu Yuan, Wen Chen

摘要

Stepped wedge designs with multiple interventions (M-SWDs) are increasingly used to evaluate complex implementation strategies. In many such trials, participants enter and leave over time, giving rise to open-cohort sampling and complex correlation structures. Existing sample size and power calculation methods are largely restricted to single-intervention designs, closed cohorts, or simplified correlation assumptions. We develop a unified, parameter-based framework for sample size and power calculation in open-cohort M-SWDs. The proposed methodology accommodates several common multi-intervention designs, including concurrent, cross-over, supplementation, and factorial designs. Flexible open-cohort sampling schemes are incorporated to account for participant turnover, and both block-exchangeable and decay correlation structures are considered to capture within-cluster and within-individual dependence over time. Closed-form and generalized variance expressions for intervention effect estimators are derived, enabling efficient power calculation under a wide range of design settings. Extensive simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed formulas provide accurate power estimates, while further power analyses illustrate how the key correlation parameters and participant retention jointly influence power. This work provides practical and theoretically grounded guidance for planning multi-intervention stepped wedge trials under open-cohort sampling, facilitating more realistic and transparent design-stage decision-making.

Factor-Augmented Neural Conditional Density Estimation with Application to Drug Response Prediction

Weineng Zhu, Xiaoyu Sheng

摘要

High-dimensional pharmacogenomic data pair thousands of molecular features with hundreds of cancer cell lines, complicating conditional density estimation. We study factor-augmented CINDES, which estimates conditional response densities from principal-component scores through binary classification. For an honest pilot/main-sample construction, assuming fresh-score PCA recovery, we derive a risk bound separating conditional-density learning, PCA score error, and representation bias. This guarantee does not cover the implemented procedure, which reuses observations for PCA and CINDES. In favorable strong-factor simulations, PCA improved a fixed CINDES pipeline, but PCA-MDN achieved lower negative log-likelihood (NLL) and integrated L 1 error in the matched comparison. Stress tests showed losses from omitted response-relevant low-variance directions and weak factor recovery, with sample-size-dependent factor-count effects. Across all 692 eligible source-specific genomics of drug sensitivity in cancer (GDSC) units and five splits, mean held-out NLL was 1.5414 for PCA-CINDES, versus 1.5661 and 1.5557 for ElasticNet and PCA/Ridge Gaussian models; PCA-MDN was better at 1.5156. A previously fixed 35-unit audit favored PCA-CINDES over Raw-X CINDES (mean paired NLL difference - 0 . 178 ) and found lower measured computational cost. The evidence supports PCA compression when retained factors capture response information and are accurately estimated; it does not establish robustness to general nonlinear or nonfactor structures, or CINDES as the preferred second-stage estimator. The application concerns preclinical prediction rather than clinical utility.

Scale-Transformed Power Priors for External Data Integration in Longitudinal Studies

Md Tuhin Sheikh

摘要

Incorporating external data can improve precision in longitudinal studies, but effective borrowing requires that information from different studies be comparable. Within a Bayesian framework, power priors are widely used for external data integration through likelihood discounting. However, they assume that parameters are on a common information scale. In longitudinal settings, differences in follow-up duration, measurement variability, and correlation structure, along with random effects, can lead to differences in effective Fisher information across studies. This complicates parameter comparability and poses challenges for existing methods, which can result in biased or inefficient inference. We propose a scale-transformed power prior for external data integration in longitudinal studies. The method is developed within a linear mixed-effects modeling framework and rescales historical parameters to the current study scale using Fisher information before borrowing. Conditional Fisher information accounts for unobserved random effects and adjusts for study-specific variability. To address differences in variability and correlation structure, we implement a partial borrowing strategy that applies borrowing to fixed effects while allowing variance components to remain study-specific. We introduce a generalized formulation with an additive adjustment component to allow for remaining differences after scaling. Theoretical results characterize how differences in information scale lead to bias under conventional power priors and establish conditions under which the proposed method improves bias and precision. Simulation studies demonstrate improved performance and provide guidance on settings where scale transformation is beneficial. An application to prostate cancer clinical trial data demonstrates the importance of the proposed method.

P4MC: A Monte Carlo Classification Method Based on Partial Variables for Postoperative Complication Prediction

Lingyi Hu, Yao Cui, Qi Yang, Shan Tang, Shanjun Mao

摘要

Predicting postoperative complications is a critical task for improving patient care and optimizing resource management. We formulate the task as a binary classification problem based on two‐stage clinical data: preoperative and intraoperative variables. A key difficulty is that intraoperative data are systematically missing for preoperative prediction. Existing methods, which typically rely solely on preoperative data or single intraoperative indicator imputation, generally fail to capture important latent intraoperative information. To provide a more robust solution, we propose P4MC (Preoperative, Partial intraoPerative, and Postoperative Monte Carlo classification). This approach explicitly models the dependence between the two stages to recover latent intraoperative information. Specifically, P4MC first applies partial sufficient dimension reduction (PSDR) to compress intraoperative variables into a low‐dimensional sufficient reduction. We then estimate the conditional distribution of this reduction given preoperative variables using nonparametric density estimation and generate latent intraoperative features for classification via Monte Carlo sampling. A simulation study demonstrates the improved predictive performance and stability of P4MC when combined with standard machine learning methods. Applications to two clinical cohorts, laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy and hepatocellular carcinoma, further show that P4MC outperforms conventional approaches and yields clinically interpretable insights. An open‐source R program is provided in the P4MC package, available at https://github.com/501fox/P4MC.git .

Bivariate Copula Models With Event-Free Cure Effect for Survival Extrapolations and Individual-Level Correlation Analysis

Daniel J Sharpe, Georgia Yates, Tuli De, Renuka Kandikatla, Prianka Singh, Matthew Dixon, Kiran Davé, Jackie Vanderpuye-Orgle, Murat Kurt

摘要

OBJECTIVES: Overall survival (OS) extrapolations from mixture cure models (MCMs) are often sensitive to the cure fraction, which is challenging to estimate when data are immature. Furthermore, in some cancer populations, it may be unreasonable to assume curative effects among patients who are not event-free. We addressed these problems using bivariate copula MCMs to jointly model OS and progression- (PFS) or recurrence-free survival (RFS), with a single event-free cure fraction parameter. METHODS: We proposed a practical framework for parametric survival copula model selection and interpretation, which involves estimating five representative candidate copula functions and examining OS distributions conditioned on intermediate event times. Bivariate MCMs with event-free cure fraction, based on previously selected marginal distributions, were estimated for two studies of nivolumab with contrasting survival patterns, namely, in previously treated advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (OS-PFS correlation) and resected melanoma (OS-RFS correlation). RESULTS: OS extrapolations from bivariate MCMs with an effective PFS (or RFS) cure fraction were more conservative than from univariate MCMs. Although patient-level OS-PFS/RFS correlation coefficients were sensitive to the copula, model averaging and inspecting estimated conditional OS distributions enabled clinically meaningful inference on the prognostic impact of intermediate events. CONCLUSIONS: Bivariate MCMs with event-free cured subpopulation incorporated curative effects into OS extrapolations more cautiously than conventional MCMs, based on a more rapidly maturing composite endpoint. The model is especially useful when OS data are immature and if post-event cure is clinically implausible. Employing alternative copulas that cover archetypal association patterns mitigated model selection bias in patient-level correlation analysis.

Two-Stage Estimation for Causal Inference Involving Semi-Continuous Exposures

Xiaoya Wang, Richard J Cook, Yeying Zhu, Tugba Akkaya-Hocagil, R Colin Carter, Sandra W Jacobson, Joseph L Jacobson, Louise M Ryan

摘要

Methods for causal inference are well developed for binary and continuous exposures, but in many settings, the exposure has a substantial mass at zero-such exposures are called semi-continuous. We propose a general causal framework for such semi-continuous exposures, together with a two-stage estimation strategy. A two-part propensity structure is introduced for the semi-continuous exposure, with one component for exposure status (exposed vs unexposed) and another for the exposure level among those exposed, and incorporates both into a marginal structural model that disentangles the effects of exposure status and dose. The two-stage procedure sequentially targets the causal dose-response among exposed individuals and the causal effect of exposure status at a reference dose, allowing flexibility in the choice of propensity score methods in the second stage. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality for the resulting estimators, and characterize their limiting values under misspecification of the propensity score models. Simulation studies evaluate finite sample performance and robustness, and an application to a study of prenatal alcohol exposure and child cognition demonstrates how the proposed methods can be used to address a range of scientific questions about both exposure status and exposure intensity.

Online Updating Mixture Cure Models for Large-Scale Survival Data Streams

Li Jin, Tao Hu, Liming Xiang

摘要

The growing availability of large-scale survival data streams present significant challenges for statistical inference particularly in the presence of a cure fraction, which complicates estimation and increases computational burden. To address these challenges, we propose an online updating mixture cure model framework. Our approach employs a batchwise renewable estimation procedure that aggregates historical information into summary statistics to update the model without retaining historical individual-level data, and thus eliminating the need for full data storage. Developing an Innovative Jackknife Estimator for Quantifying Between-Study Variance in Meta-Analysis of Proportions

Muhammad Mohsin, Stephan Morgenthaler

摘要

The DerSimonian and Laird (DL) method, commonly used in meta-analysis, often underestimates the between-study variance. The underestimation causes the confidence intervals to have less than the expected 95% coverage, resulting in inconclusive research findings. The present study introduces the use of the jackknife technique to improve the DL between-study variance estimator for meta-analyses of proportions from single-arm studies, as well as suggests certain transformations in combination with the jackknife technique. The proposed methodology is intended for settings in which the parameter of interest is an event probability or proportion, particularly for small numbers of studies with large variation. Simulation studies for binomial observations are conducted to compare the relative biases and coverage probabilities of the mean ( μ ) obtained by the DL, Paule and Mandel (PM), Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML), and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) methods with our proposed DerSimonian and Laird jackknife (DLJ) and transformed DerSimonian and Laird jackknife (TDLJs) methods. The simulation results show that our proposed DLJ and TDLJ estimators better quantify the between-study variance in meta-analysis of binary events, especially for small numbers of studies with larger variation and high probability of μ respectively. Three real life examples are presented to determine the performance of our new proposed between-study variance estimators.

Calibrated Bayes Analysis of Cluster-Randomized Trials

Ruyi Liu, Joshua L Warren, Yuki Ohnishi, Donna Spiegelman, Liangyuan Hu, Fan Li
