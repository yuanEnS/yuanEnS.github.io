A multivariate Bayesian biomarker screening algorithm that incorporates clinical covariates

Maria Veronica Vinattieri, Federico Panero, Qingchun Jin, Anna Sf Lok, Francesco C Stingo, Nabihah Tayob

摘要

Advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has limited treatment options and poor survival. Early detection of HCC improves survival, but current ultrasound screening is inconsistent and poorly adhered to. Biomarker-based blood tests offer a more accurate, accessible, and reliable alternative. Recent developments in methods for early detection of HCC have focused on the trajectories of multiple biomarkers, assuming that biomarkers follow a joint hierarchical mixture model with random changepoints. We extend this model, allowing covariates to influence three aspects of the model: biomarker trajectories, diagnosis times, and subject-specific prior risks. We evaluated whether covariates could capture unexplained heterogeneity in biomarker levels to improve early detection. A Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm was implemented to derive posterior distributions used in risk calculations among future patients and to determine whether a patient has a positive screen. The screening algorithm was evaluated through simulation studies under a range of possible covariate extensions. The Hepatitis C Antiviral Long-term Treatment against Cirrhosis (HALT-C) trial was used to further study the proposed biomarker screening algorithms for HCC using cross-validation.

Variable Selection for Additive Global Fréchet Regression

Haoyi Yang, Satarupa Bhattacharjee, Lingzhou Xue, Bing Li

摘要

We present a novel framework for variable selection in Fréchet regression with responses in general metric spaces, a setting increasingly relevant for analyzing non-Euclidean data such as probability distributions and covariance matrices. Building on the concept of (weak) Fréchet conditional means, we develop an additive regression model that represents the metric-based discrepancy of the response as a sum of covariate-specific nonlinear functions in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS). To address the absence of linear structure in the response space, we transform the response via squared distances, enabling an interpretable and tractable additive decomposition. Variable selection is performed using Elastic Net regularization, extended to the RKHS setting, and further refined through a local linear approximation scheme that incorporates folded concave penalty functions. We establish theoretical guarantees, including variable selection consistency and the strong oracle property, under minimal assumptions tailored to metric-space-valued responses. Simulations and applications to distributional and matrix-valued data demonstrate the scalability, interpretability, and practical effectiveness of the proposed approach. This work provides a principled foundation for statistical learning with random object data. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

Semi-supervised Clustering Through Representation Learning of High-dimensional Count Data

Linshanshan Wang, Mengyan Li, Zongqi Xia, Molei Liu, Tianxi Cai

摘要

High-dimensional count data arise in many modern applications, such as electronic health records (EHR) and RNA sequencing, with sparse, heterogeneous observations driven by complex latent structure. These characteristics make modeling and prediction challenging, especially when labels are limited. We propose SCORE, a semi-supervised representation learning framework for clustering and embedding construction from high-dimensional count features. SCORE is built on a Poisson-adapted latent factor mixture model that supports incorporating external pre-trained feature embeddings when available, enabling efficient extraction of latent cluster membership and low-dimensional representations. To scale inference and learning to large datasets, SCORE uses a hybrid algorithm that combines expectation-maximization with a Gaussian variational approximation, leveraging a small labeled subset to refine estimation on a large pool of unlabeled samples. We establish convergence guarantees for this hybrid procedure, quantify approximation errors introduced by the Gaussian variational approximation, and derive error rates under increasing embedding dimensions. Our theory and experiments show that incorporating unlabeled data improves accuracy and reduces sensitivity to label scarcity, yielding strong finite-sample performance relative to existing approaches. We demonstrate SCORE for predicting disability status in multiple sclerosis using EHR data, where it learns informative patient embeddings with strong predictive performance, highlighting its practical value. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

Modeling Amplitude and Phase Variation of Multivariate Random Processes in Metric Spaces*

Yaqing Chen, Kyum Kim

摘要

In functional data analysis, distinguishing between amplitude and phase variation is essential to prevent distorted statistical inferences. While extensive work has been devoted to developing time-warping methods to address this issue for Euclidean functional data, much less is known about how to characterize and model these two sources of variability when they co-exist in random processes taking values in general metric spaces. In this paper, we develop a comprehensive statistical framework characterizing amplitude and phase variation for multivariate metric-space-valued functional data. We propose a geodesic latent deformation model (GLDM) for jointly analyzing both types of variation by using tools from metric geometry, and define tempo indices to quantify the temporal dynamics. We devise a novel estimation procedure and establish (uniform) convergence rates for the model estimators. The method’s broad applicability is demonstrated through a real-world application to annual gender-specific age-at-death distributions providing new insights into global longevity dynamics as well as simulations on multivariate positive semi-definite (PSD) matrix-valued and network-valued functional data. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

Confounder-robust causal discovery and inference in Perturb-seq using proxy and instrumental variables

Kwangmoon Park, Hongzhe Li

摘要

Emerging single-cell technologies that combine CRISPR-based genetic perturbations with single-cell RNA sequencing, such as Perturb-seq, offer unprecedented opportunities to uncover cause-and-effect relationships among genes. Nonetheless, Perturb-seq experiments are subject to unobserved factors that, if not properly handled, can severely bias the inferred causal relationships between genes. These latent factors may arise not only from intrinsic molecular features of the regulatory elements, but also from unmeasured genes omitted due to cost-constrained experimental designs. Although methods for analyzing large-scale Perturb-seq data are rapidly maturing, approaches that explicitly account for such unobserved confounders when inferring causal gene networks are still lacking. Here, we propose a novel approach to accurately reconstruct causal gene networks from Perturb-seq data even when important confounders are missing. Our framework leverages proxy and instrumental variable strategies to exploit the rich information embedded in the perturbations, enabling unbiased estimation of the underlying directed acyclic graph (DAG) of gene expression. Applications to both comprehensive synthetic data and real CRISPR interference experiments in K562 cells demonstrate that our method outperforms baseline approaches that lack principled adjustments for unmeasured confounding, yielding more accurate and biologically relevant recovery of the true causal DAGs. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

A Spatial Interference Approach to Account for Mobility in Air Pollution Studies with Multivariate Continuous Treatments

Heejun Shin, Danielle Braun, Kezia Irene, Michelle Audirac, Joseph Antonelli

摘要

We develop new methodology to improve our understanding of the causal effects of multivariate air pollution exposures on public health accounting for mobility. Typically, in environmental health studies, exposure to air pollution for an individual is assigned based on their residential address, though many people spend time in different regions with potentially different levels of air pollution. To account for this, we incorporate estimates of the mobility of individuals from cell phone mobility data to obtain a more accurate estimate of their air pollution exposure. We treat this as an interference problem, where individuals in one geographic region can be affected by exposures in other regions due to mobility into those areas. We propose policy-relevant estimands and derive expressions showing the extent of bias one would obtain by ignoring individuals’ mobility. We additionally highlight the benefits of the proposed interference framework relative to a measurement error framework to account for mobility. Utilizing flexible Bayesian methodology we develop novel estimation strategies to estimate causal effects that account for this spatial spillover. Lastly, we use the proposed methodology to study the health effects of ambient air pollution on mortality among Medicare enrollees in the United States. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

A Generalized Tangent Approximation based Variational Inference Framework for Strongly Super-Gaussian Likelihoods

Somjit Roy, Pritam Dey, Debdeep Pati, Bani K. Mallick

摘要

Variational inference, as an alternative to Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling, has played a transformative role in enabling scalable computation for complex Bayesian models. Nevertheless, existing approaches often depend on either rigid model-specific formulations or stochastic black-box optimization routines. Tangent approximation is a principled class of structured variational methods that exploits the geometry of the underlying probability model. However, its utility has largely been confined to logistic regression and related modeling regimes. In this article, we propose a novel variational framework based on tangent transformation for a broad class of probability models characterized by strongly super-Gaussian likelihoods. Our method leverages convex duality to construct tangent minorants of the log-likelihood, thereby inducing conjugacy with Gaussian priors over model parameters in an otherwise intractable setup. Under mild assumptions on the data-generating mechanism, we establish algorithmic convergence guarantees, a contribution that stands in contrast to the limited theoretical assurances typically available for black-box variational methods. Additionally, we derive near-parametric variational risk bounds. Superior performance of our proposed methodology is illustrated on simulated and real-data scenarios that challenge state-of-the-art variational algorithms in terms of scalability and their ability to consistently capture complex underlying data structure. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

Valid Inference for Two Sample Summary Statistics Mendelian Randomization

Kai Wang, Grace Z Wang

摘要

Mendelian randomization (MR) studies increasingly rely on summary statistics from genome-wide association studies (GWASs). However, current summary-data methods treat marginal GWAS effect estimates as independent across instrumental single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which holds only when the SNPs have no effect. Without imposing this independence assumption, we derive expressions for the two-stage least squares (TSLS), two-sample TSLS (TSTSLS), and generalized method-of-moments estimators together with their variance estimators using only GWAS summary statistics, assuming the instrumental SNPs are in linkage equilibrium. We show that the widely used inverse-variance weighted (IVW) estimator is generally not equivalent to either TSLS or TSTSLS, contrary to a common claim in the MR literature. We further demonstrate that the conventional IVW standard error is generally inconsistent and provide a consistent alternative. Extensive simulations confirm the theoretical results and reveal that Wald confidence intervals based on summary statistics can be anti-conservative. An application to publicly available GWAS summary statistics on body mass index and systolic blood pressure illustrates the practical implications of the proposed methodology.

Federated Time-to-Event Data Analysis Using Piecewise Exponential Regression in DataSHIELD

Bodil Svennblad, Benedicte Delcoigne, Paul Burton, Demetris Avraam

摘要

Time-to-event analyses on individual patient data are commonly performed with Cox or Poisson regression. The statistical power of epidemiological findings from such analyses is often sufficient when a large amount of data is analyzed, especially when studying rare outcomes or rare exposures. The increase of sample size can be achieved by co-analyzing data collected from different cohorts and hosted at different locations. For co-analyzing data located at different centers, the common options are either (i) to physically transfer and pool the data into a single dataset and analyze them jointly or (ii) to conduct a two-step meta-analysis where a separate analysis is done at each center and the center-specific results are then pooled and combined into a single estimate. DataSHIELD offers a third option where individual patient data from different locations are analyzed in a federated manner without transporting any data. In this paper, we describe how a federated Poisson regression can be conducted on time-to-event data using this infrastructure, providing relevant estimates.

Dynamic Prediction in Mixture Cure Models: A Model-Based Landmarking Approach

Marta Cipriani, Marco Alfò, Mirko Signorelli

摘要

Mixture cure models are widely used in survival analysis when a portion of patients is considered cured and is no longer at risk for the event of interest. In clinical settings, dynamic survival prediction is particularly important to refine prognosis by incorporating patient information updated over time. Landmarking methods have emerged as a flexible approach for this purpose, as they allow to summarize longitudinal covariates up to a given landmark time and use these summaries in subsequent prediction. For mixture cure models, the only landmarking strategy available in the literature relies on summarizing the dynamics of observed longitudinal markers through the last observation carried forward (LOCF) method. However, LOCF discards most of the longitudinal information, does not correct for measurement error, and may rely on outdated values if observation times are far apart. To overcome these limitations, we propose a sequential approach that integrates model-based landmarking within a mixture cure model. Initially, longitudinal covariates are modeled using (generalized) linear mixed models, from which individual-specific summaries are predicted. The predicted summaries are then incorporated as covariates into a Cox proportional hazards cure model. We investigated the performance of the proposed approach under different cure fractions, sample sizes, and longitudinal data structures through an extensive simulation study. The results show that the model-based strategy provides more refined predictions compared to LOCF, even when the model is misspecified in favor of the LOCF approach. Finally, we illustrate our method using a real-world dataset on renal transplant patients.

Calibrating Machine Learning Approaches for Probability Estimation Without Calibration Data

Eleonora Di Carluccio, Georgios Koliopanos, Francisco M Ojeda, Christian Weimar, Andreas Ziegler

摘要

Statistical prediction models for binary outcomes are becoming increasingly popular. One significant challenge is calibrating these models to suit the characteristics of a target population that is structurally different from the original population. Calibration is especially challenging when there is no training data available from the target population. To address this problem, we propose a novel calibration method, SimCal, which uses synthetic data generated from the model development data in conjunction with marginal statistics from the calibration cohort. We show that expert judgment modeling (EJM) may be used for calibration if cross-sectional data from the target population are available comprising expert judgments about the potential outcome and the covariates. We describe three alternative calibration approaches when calibration data are lacking: similarity-binning averaging (SBA), adaptive calibration of predictions (ACP), and Elkan calibration. In a simulation study, we compare SBA, ACP, Elkan calibration, and SimCal. R code for applying these methods is provided from the re-analysis of data on coronary artery disease. We illustrate all 5 calibration approaches with a real data set for predicting functional outcome after stroke and all approaches but EJM in the re-analysis of the Cleveland Clinic data. None of the approaches performed convincingly well in all situations. SimCal performed well when model parameters were correctly specified. EJM failed on the stroke data. Further research is urgently required for calibration in the absence of calibration data.

Locally Estimated Scatterplot Smoothing for Censored Survival Data

David van Klaveren, Peter C Austin, Patrick W Serruys, Ben van Calster, Frank E Harrell

摘要

In the absence of censoring, locally estimated scatterplot smoothing (LOESS) is a powerful tool to estimate the relationship between a continuous covariate and the probability of an event and is often used to visually assess calibration of prognostic models. Since follow-up in prognostic studies is often incomplete, we aimed to study the extension of LOESS to right-censored data. In line with other work, we first obtained inverse-probability-of-censoring (IPC) weights through Kaplan-Meier estimation. When censoring varied by covariate levels, we used Cox regression with a spline function of the covariate to obtain IPC weights. We then applied LOESS to the weighted event indicators to estimate the relationship between the covariate and the probability of an event within a specific time horizon. We illustrated the application of LOESS for censored data (LOESScd) using patients with complex coronary artery disease. We used the LOESScd to estimate the association between continuous predictors and the probability of death within 10 years. LOESScd estimates closely followed grouped Kaplan-Meier estimates, especially in case of weak smoothing. Covariate-dependent IPC weights improved LOESScd when censoring varied by covariate levels. We then examined the finite-sample behavior of LOESScd in simulations motivated by four typical prognostic model validation scenarios. In 2000 samples of 1800 patients, bias was limited and coverage was close to nominal. In conclusion, LOESScd is a promising estimator of the relationship of a continuous covariate with the probability of an event in the presence of right-censoring. LOESScd is especially useful for estimating calibration curves when validating prognostic models.

Unified conformalized multiple testing with full data efficiency

Yuyang Huo, Xiaoyang Wu, Changliang Zou, Haojie Ren

摘要

Conformalized multiple testing offers a model-free way to control predictive uncertainty in decision-making. Existing methods typically use only part of the available data to build score functions tailored to specific settings. We propose a unified framework that puts data utilization at the centre: it uses all available data—null, alternative, and unlabelled—to construct scores and calibrate p-values through a full permutation strategy. This unified use of all available data significantly improves power by enhancing nonconformity score quality and maximizing calibration set size while rigorously controlling the false discovery rate. Crucially, our framework provides a systematic design principle for conformal testing and enables automatic selection of the best conformal procedure among candidates without extra data splitting. Extensive numerical experiments demonstrate that our enhanced methods deliver superior efficiency and adaptability across diverse scenarios.

Saddlepoint approximations for plug-in resampling

Z Niu, Z Huang, J Ray Choudhury, E Katsevich

摘要

Resampling-based procedures can improve on normal approximations in sparse, large-scale testing problems, but their computational cost can be prohibitive. We recognize that several existing procedures belong to a faster plug-in resampling subclass, fixing fitted nuisance parameters during resampling. When the resampled statistic is a sum of conditionally independent terms, the saddlepoint approximation for the resampling p -value offers further acceleration, replacing resampling with an analytical tail approximation. However, standard Edgeworth-based approximation-error bounds impose regularity conditions that are hard to verify for plug-in resampling laws. We use an alternative approach to establish a finite-sample relative-error bound for the Lugannani–Rice approximation under more tractable conditions, which we apply in two contexts. In statistical genetics, we identify response resampling procedures as the targets of existing saddlepoint approximations and establish guarantees in a representative setting. In conditional independence testing, we introduce a saddlepoint approximation for the distilled conditional randomization test, which has been applied successfully in biology. Our rates quantify the effects of sparsity and signal strength, with matching lower bounds in special cases. We additionally establish asymptotic Type-I error control of the corresponding plug-in resampling procedures under growing sparsity. In simulations and single-cell CRISPR data analysis, our new test closely approximates distilled conditional randomization test p -values and preserves its statistical performance while accelerating computation by up to 250-fold.

Optimal Two-Phase Sampling Designs for Generalized Raking Estimators With Multiple Parameters of Interest

Jasper B Yang, Bryan E Shepherd, Thomas Lumley, Pamela A Shaw

摘要

Large observational datasets, including those derived from electronic health records, are a valuable resource for medical research but are often affected by missingness, measurement error, and misclassification. Two-phase sampling with generalized raking (GR) estimation is an efficient and robust approach to statistical inference in such settings. In this approach, variables that are unavailable or measured with error in a large Phase 1 cohort are obtained with higher-quality measurements in a Phase 2 subsample. Previous research has studied optimal Phase 2 sampling designs for inverse-probability-weighted (IPW) estimators in nonadaptive, multiparameter settings, and for GR estimators in single-parameter settings. In this work, we extend these results by deriving optimal adaptive, multiwave sampling designs for IPW and GR estimators when multiple parameters are of interest. We propose several practical allocation strategies, examine the design choices that arise when implementing them, and evaluate their performance through extensive simulations and an example using data from people living with HIV attending the Vanderbilt Comprehensive Care Clinic (VCCC). Our results show that independently optimizing allocation for each parameter improves efficiency over traditional case-control sampling. We also derive an integer-valued, A-optimal allocation method that typically outperforms independent optimization. Notably, we find that optimal designs for GR can differ substantially from those for IPW, and that this distinction can meaningfully affect estimator efficiency in the multiple-parameter setting. These findings offer practical guidance for future two-phase studies involving incomplete or error-prone data.

Multiscale Spatio-Temporal Data Monitoring Using Forward Elimination and Scale Information Criterion

Kai Yang, Peihua Qiu

摘要

Spatio-temporal process monitoring has broad applications in disease surveillance, environment monitoring, epidemiology, and many other areas. Most existing spatio-temporal process monitoring methods are developed for analyzing observed data at a single geospatial scale (e.g., state, county, or census tract), and usually impose some parametric assumptions on spatio-temporal data structure. For instance, observed data are often assumed to follow a parametric distribution (e.g., normal or Poisson) and the mean and covariance of a related spatio-temporal model are assumed to have parametric forms, which are rarely valid and/or difficult to justify in many applications. In practice, spatio-temporal data (e.g., incidence rates of infectious diseases) are usually managed at multiple geospatial scales and have complicated structure. In this paper, we develop a new method for multiscale spatio-temporal data monitoring. At each observation time, the new method first detects anomalous regions at each spatial scale. To reduce computing time, a forward elimination procedure is suggested for this purpose. Then, an optimal spatial scale at the current observation time is determined using the detected anomalous regions and a proposed scale information criterion. Finally, a charting statistic is constructed based on the detected anomalous regions at the optimal scale. This method is fully nonparametric in the sense that no parametric assumptions are imposed on the structure of observed spatio-temporal data. It is also scalable and computationally efficient, making it a reliable and effective tool for online multiscale spatio-temporal data monitoring problems. *(abstract via OpenAlex)*

Clinically Interpretable Time-To-Event Testing in Randomized Clinical Trials: Milestone Survival Combined With Restricted Mean Survival Time

Siwen He, Ludovic Trinquart

摘要

The log-rank test and hazard ratio (HR) are widely used to compare survival curves in event-driven randomized clinical trials (RCTs). The optimality of the log-rank test and interpretation of the HR rely on the proportional hazards (PH) assumption, which is frequently violated in oncology due to delayed, waning, or crossing hazards. Although combination procedures built from weighted log-rank tests improve robustness to non-PH, hazard-based summaries remain difficult to interpret clinically. We propose combination tests that integrate two clinically interpretable estimands: the between-group difference in milestone survival probability S ( τ ) and in restricted mean survival time (RMST) μ ( 0 , τ ) . Using Monte Carlo simulations under a milestone-based design (uniform follow-up to τ ) and supplementary event-driven designs, we compared the proposed combinations with the log-rank and MaxCombo tests across diverse survival patterns, sample sizes, and censoring rates. We evaluated Type I error and power. The milestone combination procedures showed rejection probabilities close to the nominal level under the estimand-null configurations examined and showed competitive to superior power in non-PH settings, including transient effects and crossing hazards. Applications to three oncology RCTs illustrate that, when HRs hover near unity while absolute estimands differ, the milestone combinations can detect benefits at clinically meaningful horizons. These findings support aligning design, estimands, and analysis around a prespecified milestone. Careful selection of τ is essential to balance information and interpretability. Milestone-based combination tests provide a viable, patient-centered alternative or complement to HR-centric methods, particularly when non-PH is anticipated.

摘要

In clinical trials, the comparison between treatment arms of a measurement is often complicated by an event such as death or progression that can make this measurement irrelevant or difficult to perform. Semantic Similarity-Informed Bayesian Borrowing for Quantitative Signal Detection of Adverse Events

François Haguinet, Jeffery L Painter, Gregory E Powell, Andrea Callegaro, Andrew Bate

摘要

We present a Bayesian dynamic borrowing approach to enhance quantitative identification of adverse events in spontaneous reporting systems. The method embeds a robust meta-analytic predictive prior and incorporates semantic similarity measures (SSMs) to enable weighted information sharing from clinically similar MedDRA preferred terms (PTs) to the target PT. This continuous similarity-based borrowing overcomes limitations of rigid hierarchical grouping in disproportionality analysis (DPA). Using data from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System and a time-stamped reference set, we evaluate our approach-termed IC SSM-against the current standard approach of "traditional" information component (IC) analysis and borrowing at the MedDRA HLGT level (IC HLGT). The primary IC SSM approach at the standard threshold (2.5% posterior quantile > 0 ) detected true signals over five months earlier than traditional IC. It showed higher sensitivity (57.0%) but lower precision (14.5%) than traditional IC (50.1%, 16.1%), while outperforming IC HLGT (55.7%, 13.9%). In subsequent analyses, even when tightening the alerting quantile of IC SSM to 1% to improve precision (16.4%), superior sensitivity (51.4%) was retained compared to traditional IC. Similarly, relaxing traditional IC's quantile to 5% yielded only 55.6% sensitivity with 14.2% precision, failing to match IC SSM's performance or timeliness and confirming its inherent advantage. IC SSM's superior timeliness and sensitivity stem from integrating clinically meaningful information, making it a scalable and context-aware enhancement to traditional DPA. The method has potential for exploratory analysis in sparse datasets, and refinements to the borrowing strategies and similarity metrics could yield further performance improvements.

Rao-Blackwellized e-variables

D de Roos, B Chugg, P Grünwald, A Ramdas

摘要

Summary: We show that for any concave utility, the expected utility of an e-variable can only increase after conditioning on a sufficient statistic, echoing the classic Rao-Blackwell theorem. ROC-based methods for comparing two survival curves under non-proportional hazards with right, left, and doubly censored data

Mohammod Mahmudur Rahman, Leonidas E Bantis

摘要

Studies comparing two survival curves to assess treatment differences are commonplace. The widely used log-rank test is designed to operate best under the proportional hazards (PH) assumption. Alternative methods, such as the Fleming-Harrington weighted log-rank tests, rely on prior knowledge of survival patterns, which is often difficult to specify before a study. The recently developed MaxCombo test does not require such prior information; however, it leaves substantial room for power improvement, particularly when survival curves cross. To address these issues, we propose new nonparametric and semiparametric methods for comparing two survival curves based on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve length and the overlap coefficient, along with a joint inference approach combining both measures. Our methods are applicable under non-PH settings and can accommodate right, left, and double censoring. We evaluate our methods through extensive simulation studies under different non-PH configurations.
