Scale-Transformed Power Priors for External Data Integration in Longitudinal Studies

Md Tuhin Sheikh

摘要

Incorporating external data can improve precision in longitudinal studies, but effective borrowing requires that information from different studies be comparable. Within a Bayesian framework, power priors are widely used for external data integration through likelihood discounting. However, they assume that parameters are on a common information scale. In longitudinal settings, differences in follow-up duration, measurement variability, and correlation structure, along with random effects, can lead to differences in effective Fisher information across studies. This complicates parameter comparability and poses challenges for existing methods, which can result in biased or inefficient inference. We propose a scale-transformed power prior for external data integration in longitudinal studies. The method is developed within a linear mixed-effects modeling framework and rescales historical parameters to the current study scale using Fisher information before borrowing. Conditional Fisher information accounts for unobserved random effects and adjusts for study-specific variability. To address differences in variability and correlation structure, we implement a partial borrowing strategy that applies borrowing to fixed effects while allowing variance components to remain study-specific. We introduce a generalized formulation with an additive adjustment component to allow for remaining differences after scaling. Theoretical results characterize how differences in information scale lead to bias under conventional power priors and establish conditions under which the proposed method improves bias and precision. Simulation studies demonstrate improved performance and provide guidance on settings where scale transformation is beneficial. An application to prostate cancer clinical trial data demonstrates the importance of the proposed method.

Sample Size and Power Calculation for Continuous Outcomes in Open-Cohort Stepped Wedge Designs With Multiple Interventions

Shiyu Feng, Meng Zheng, Yi Zhang, Xuezhi Liang, Minglu Yuan, Wen Chen

摘要

Stepped wedge designs with multiple interventions (M-SWDs) are increasingly used to evaluate complex implementation strategies. In many such trials, participants enter and leave over time, giving rise to open-cohort sampling and complex correlation structures. Existing sample size and power calculation methods are largely restricted to single-intervention designs, closed cohorts, or simplified correlation assumptions. We develop a unified, parameter-based framework for sample size and power calculation in open-cohort M-SWDs. The proposed methodology accommodates several common multi-intervention designs, including concurrent, cross-over, supplementation, and factorial designs. Flexible open-cohort sampling schemes are incorporated to account for participant turnover, and both block-exchangeable and decay correlation structures are considered to capture within-cluster and within-individual dependence over time. Closed-form and generalized variance expressions for intervention effect estimators are derived, enabling efficient power calculation under a wide range of design settings. Extensive simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed formulas provide accurate power estimates, while further power analyses illustrate how the key correlation parameters and participant retention jointly influence power. This work provides practical and theoretically grounded guidance for planning multi-intervention stepped wedge trials under open-cohort sampling, facilitating more realistic and transparent design-stage decision-making.

Regression Adjusted Target Effects Using Auxiliary Strata Under Design-Based Inference

Zixuan Zhao, Yuehan Yang

摘要

We propose a design-based covariate adjustment that leverages information from auxiliary strata to improve the efficiency of average treatment effect estimation when target samples are sparse. The estimator borrows strength from related strata that may be correlated with the target, yielding gains for studies with limited observations or rare subpopulations. We present the estimator, develop a Neyman-style conservative variance estimator for hypothesis testing and confidence interval construction, and establish its large-sample properties. The procedure is straightforward to implement within standard randomization-based workflows and does not require a correctly specified outcome model or stochastic error assumptions. In simulations, the method delivers lower variance and narrower confidence intervals than conventional design-based or regression-adjusted estimators across a range of sparsity and correlation patterns. Applications to a schooling experiment and the ACTG 175 randomized trial illustrate practical gains in precision.

P4MC: A Monte Carlo Classification Method Based on Partial Variables for Postoperative Complication Prediction

Lingyi Hu, Yao Cui, Qi Yang, Shan Tang, Shanjun Mao

摘要

Predicting postoperative complications is a critical task for improving patient care and optimizing resource management. We formulate the task as a binary classification problem based on two-stage clinical data: preoperative and intraoperative variables. A key difficulty is that intraoperative data are systematically missing for preoperative prediction. Existing methods, which typically rely solely on preoperative data or single intraoperative indicator imputation, generally fail to capture important latent intraoperative information. To provide a more robust solution, we propose P4MC (Preoperative, Partial intraoPerative, and Postoperative Monte Carlo classification). This approach explicitly models the dependence between the two stages to recover latent intraoperative information. Specifically, P4MC first applies partial sufficient dimension reduction (PSDR) to compress intraoperative variables into a low-dimensional sufficient reduction. We then estimate the conditional distribution of this reduction given preoperative variables using nonparametric density estimation and generate latent intraoperative features for classification via Monte Carlo sampling. A simulation study demonstrates the improved predictive performance and stability of P4MC when combined with standard machine learning methods. Applications to two clinical cohorts, laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomy and hepatocellular carcinoma, further show that P4MC outperforms conventional approaches and yields clinically interpretable insights. An open-source R program is provided in the P4MC package, available at https://github.com/501fox/P4MC.git.

Factor-Augmented Neural Conditional Density Estimation with Application to Drug Response Prediction

Weineng Zhu, Xiaoyu Sheng

摘要

High-dimensional pharmacogenomic data pair thousands of molecular features with hundreds of cancer cell lines, complicating conditional density estimation. We study factor-augmented CINDES, which estimates conditional response densities from principal-component scores through binary classification. For an honest pilot/main-sample construction, assuming fresh-score PCA recovery, we derive a risk bound separating conditional-density learning, PCA score error, and representation bias. This guarantee does not cover the implemented procedure, which reuses observations for PCA and CINDES. In favorable strong-factor simulations, PCA improved a fixed CINDES pipeline, but PCA-MDN achieved lower negative log-likelihood (NLL) and integrated L 1 error in the matched comparison. Stress tests showed losses from omitted response-relevant low-variance directions and weak factor recovery, with sample-size-dependent factor-count effects. Across all 692 eligible source-specific genomics of drug sensitivity in cancer (GDSC) units and five splits, mean held-out NLL was 1.5414 for PCA-CINDES, versus 1.5661 and 1.5557 for ElasticNet and PCA/Ridge Gaussian models; PCA-MDN was better at 1.5156. A previously fixed 35-unit audit favored PCA-CINDES over Raw-X CINDES (mean paired NLL difference - 0 . 178 ) and found lower measured computational cost. The evidence supports PCA compression when retained factors capture response information and are accurately estimated; it does not establish robustness to general nonlinear or nonfactor structures, or CINDES as the preferred second-stage estimator. The application concerns preclinical prediction rather than clinical utility.

Bivariate Copula Models With Event-Free Cure Effect for Survival Extrapolations and Individual-Level Correlation Analysis

Daniel J Sharpe, Georgia Yates, Tuli De, Renuka Kandikatla, Prianka Singh, Matthew Dixon, Kiran Davé, Jackie Vanderpuye-Orgle, Murat Kurt

摘要

OBJECTIVES: Overall survival (OS) extrapolations from mixture cure models (MCMs) are often sensitive to the cure fraction, which is challenging to estimate when data are immature. Furthermore, in some cancer populations, it may be unreasonable to assume curative effects among patients who are not event-free. We addressed these problems using bivariate copula MCMs to jointly model OS and progression- (PFS) or recurrence-free survival (RFS), with a single event-free cure fraction parameter. METHODS: We proposed a practical framework for parametric survival copula model selection and interpretation, which involves estimating five representative candidate copula functions and examining OS distributions conditioned on intermediate event times. Bivariate MCMs with event-free cure fraction, based on previously selected marginal distributions, were estimated for two studies of nivolumab with contrasting survival patterns, namely, in previously treated advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (OS-PFS correlation) and resected melanoma (OS-RFS correlation). RESULTS: OS extrapolations from bivariate MCMs with an effective PFS (or RFS) cure fraction were more conservative than from univariate MCMs. Although patient-level OS-PFS/RFS correlation coefficients were sensitive to the copula, model averaging and inspecting estimated conditional OS distributions enabled clinically meaningful inference on the prognostic impact of intermediate events. CONCLUSIONS: Bivariate MCMs with event-free cured subpopulation incorporated curative effects into OS extrapolations more cautiously than conventional MCMs, based on a more rapidly maturing composite endpoint. The model is especially useful when OS data are immature and if post-event cure is clinically implausible. Employing alternative copulas that cover archetypal association patterns mitigated model selection bias in patient-level correlation analysis.

Two-Stage Estimation for Causal Inference Involving Semi-Continuous Exposures

Xiaoya Wang, Richard J Cook, Yeying Zhu, Tugba Akkaya-Hocagil, R Colin Carter, Sandra W Jacobson, Joseph L Jacobson, Louise M Ryan

摘要

Methods for causal inference are well developed for binary and continuous exposures, but in many settings, the exposure has a substantial mass at zero-such exposures are called semi-continuous. We propose a general causal framework for such semi-continuous exposures, together with a two-stage estimation strategy. A two-part propensity structure is introduced for the semi-continuous exposure, with one component for exposure status (exposed vs unexposed) and another for the exposure level among those exposed, and incorporates both into a marginal structural model that disentangles the effects of exposure status and dose. The two-stage procedure sequentially targets the causal dose-response among exposed individuals and the causal effect of exposure status at a reference dose, allowing flexibility in the choice of propensity score methods in the second stage. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality for the resulting estimators, and characterize their limiting values under misspecification of the propensity score models. Simulation studies evaluate finite sample performance and robustness, and an application to a study of prenatal alcohol exposure and child cognition demonstrates how the proposed methods can be used to address a range of scientific questions about both exposure status and exposure intensity.

Online Updating Mixture Cure Models for Large-Scale Survival Data Streams

Li Jin, Tao Hu, Liming Xiang

摘要

The growing availability of large-scale survival data streams present significant challenges for statistical inference particularly in the presence of a cure fraction, which complicates estimation and increases computational burden. To address these challenges, we propose an online updating mixture cure model framework. Our approach employs a batchwise renewable estimation procedure that aggregates historical information into summary statistics to update the model without retaining historical individual-level data, and thus eliminating the need for full data storage. A renewable penalized expectation-maximization algorithm is developed to enable simultaneous parameter estimation and variable selection. Furthermore, a tuning-free strategy is incorporated to enhance computational efficiency. The asymptotic properties of the proposed method are established. Simulation studies and an application to breast cancer data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results database demonstrate the method's estimation accuracy, predictive performance, and computational scalability.

Developing an Innovative Jackknife Estimator for Quantifying Between-Study Variance in Meta-Analysis of Proportions

Muhammad Mohsin, Stephan Morgenthaler

摘要

The DerSimonian and Laird (DL) method, commonly used in meta-analysis, often underestimates the between-study variance. The underestimation causes the confidence intervals to have less than the expected 95% coverage, resulting in inconclusive research findings. The present study introduces the use of the jackknife technique to improve the DL between-study variance estimator for meta-analyses of proportions from single-arm studies, as well as suggests certain transformations in combination with the jackknife technique. The proposed methodology is intended for settings in which the parameter of interest is an event probability or proportion, particularly for small numbers of studies with large variation. Simulation studies for binomial observations are conducted to compare the relative biases and coverage probabilities of the mean ( μ ) obtained by the DL, Paule and Mandel (PM), Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML), and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman (HKSJ) methods with our proposed DerSimonian and Laird jackknife (DLJ) and transformed DerSimonian and Laird jackknife (TDLJs) methods. The simulation results show that our proposed DLJ and TDLJ estimators better quantify the between-study variance in meta-analysis of binary events, especially for small numbers of studies with larger variation and high probability of μ respectively. Three real life examples are presented to determine the performance of our new proposed between-study variance estimators.

Calibrated Bayes Analysis of Cluster-Randomized Trials

Ruyi Liu, Joshua L Warren, Yuki Ohnishi, Donna Spiegelman, Liangyuan Hu, Fan Li

摘要

In cluster-randomized trials (CRTs), entire clusters of individuals are randomized to treatment, and outcomes within a cluster are typically correlated. While frequentist approaches are standard practice for CRT analysis, Bayesian methods have emerged as a strong alternative. Previous work has investigated the use of Bayesian hierarchical models for continuous, binary, and count outcomes in CRTs, but these approaches focus on model-based treatment effect coefficients as the target estimands, which may have ambiguous interpretation under model misspecification and informative cluster size. In this article, we introduce a calibrated Bayesian procedure for estimand-aligned analysis of CRTs even in the presence of potentially misspecified models. We propose estimators targeting both the cluster-average treatment effect (cluster-ATE) and individual-average treatment effect (individual-ATE), particularly in scenarios with informative cluster sizes. We additionally explore strategies for summarizing the posterior samples that can achieve the frequentist coverage even under working model misspecification. We provide simulation evidence to demonstrate the model-robustness property of the proposed estimators in CRTs, and further investigate the impact of covariate adjustment as well as the use of more flexible Bayesian nonparametric working models in the CRT context.

Which Small-Sample Correction Should be Used When Analyzing Stepped-Wedge Designs With Time-Varying Treatment Effects?

Yongdong Ouyang, Monica Taljaard, James P Hughes, Fan Li

摘要

Stepped-wedge cluster randomized trials (SW-CRTs) evaluate interventions that are sequentially rolled out across clusters. Standard analyses of SW-CRTs typically use immediate-treatment (IT) models, which assume the treatment effects begin at crossover and remain constant thereafter. When treatment effects vary with exposure duration, IT models may misrepresent the target effects. Exposure-time indicator (ETI) models address this by allowing treatment effects to differ by time since exposure and can properly target the time-averaged treatment effect (TATE) and long-term effect (LTE). Like IT models, ETI models require specification of a random-effects structure, which may be misspecified. While robust variance estimators (RVEs) theoretically protect against such misspecification, their empirical performance under ETI models is not well understood. We review RVEs for ETI models and evaluate them in comprehensive simulation studies with continuous and binary outcomes under correctly specified (binary only) and misspecified random-effects structures. We compare the classic sandwich variance, Kauermann-Carroll (KC), Mancl-DeRouen (MD), and Morel-Bokossa-Neerchal (MBN) bias-corrected sandwich variance estimators for inference on the TATE and LTE in primary simulations and additionally evaluate Fay-Graubard (FG) in a sub-study. Our simulations show that under misspecified random-effects structures, model-based standard errors (SE) produced undercoverage, whereas RVEs improved performance under the ETI models. For continuous outcomes, MD with a t-distribution and degrees of freedom equal to the number of clusters minus two gave the closest to nominal coverage probabilities. Surprisingly, however, MBN was the only consistently reliable option for binary outcomes. MD, however, could be unstable in one-cluster-per-sequence designs because of data sparsity. Across scenarios, both model-based SE and RVE for LTE were unstable, indicating that greater caution is needed when targeting LTE under ETI models.

Partially Linear Additive Reversed Mean Model for Panel Count Data With Informative Terminal Event

Anyin Feng, Xiangbin Hu, Wen Su, Xingqiu Zhao

摘要

This article investigates a partially linear additive reversed mean model for panel count data with an informative terminal event. By anchoring the time origin at the terminal event, the proposed model characterizes the recurrent event process prior to the occurrence of the terminal event. The partially linear additive form provides a flexible framework for modeling covariate effects. The B-spline sieve approximation is employed for both the baseline reversed mean function and the link function. To make the optimization of the loss function feasible, we treat the distribution function of the terminal event as a nuisance functional parameter and develop a two-stage estimation procedure. We establish the asymptotic properties of the proposed estimator, including consistency, convergence rate, and asymptotic distribution. Simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed estimator performs well in finite-sample settings. The proposed method is further illustrated through an application to the Chinese longitudinal healthy longevity survey (CLHLS).

Marginal Analysis of Clustered Interval-Censored Competing Risks Data With Informative Cluster Size

Xinyi Wang, Peijie Wang, Xinyuan Song

摘要

Motivated by the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC) study, this paper addresses the marginal analysis of clustered data under a complex setting involving interval censoring, competing risks, and informative cluster size, for which existing methods are not readily applicable. We consider a class of marginal generalized odds-rate transformation models for the cumulative incidence function in the presence of interval-censored competing risks, with the Fine-Gray model and the sub-distribution proportional odds model included as special cases. For statistical inference, a cluster-weighted sieve maximum pseudo-likelihood estimation procedure is developed, in which B-splines are used to approximate the nonparametric components of the semiparametric models. This method effectively reduces the original infinite-dimensional estimation problem to a finite-dimensional one. We establish the asymptotic properties of the resulting parameter estimators and conduct extensive simulation studies to evaluate their finite-sample performance. The proposed methodology is applied to the motivating NACC data to investigate potential risk factors for the cumulative incidence of Lewy body dementia.

Estimating Marginal Odds Ratios for Cluster Randomized Trials: The Cluster-Level GLM

Jennifer A Thompson, Brennan C Kahan, Andrew Copas

摘要

BACKGROUND: Methods for estimating marginal effects in cluster randomized trials are limited. We describe and evaluate a cluster-level generalized linear model (CL-GLM) that applies a GLM to cluster-level summaries (means, proportions, or rates) and can estimate differences, ratios, or odds ratios. We focus on odds ratios. METHODS: We conducted a simulation study with 10-50 clusters, intracluster correlation coefficients of 0.05 or 0.15, and a mean cluster size of 120, either fixed or variable. Informative cluster size, where the intervention effect varies with cluster size, was also considered. Control arm prevalence was set to 50%. We compared cluster-level GLMs with normal and gamma distributions and logit link to generalized estimating equations (GEE) and independence estimating equations (IEEs). Weighted and unweighted analyses were examined to target cluster-average and individual-average estimands. RESULTS: CL-GLM with a normal distribution produced unbiased marginal cluster-average odds ratios with appropriate confidence interval coverage. CL-GLM with a gamma distribution resulted in over-coverage. Unweighted IEE produced unbiased marginal individual-average odds ratios with correct coverage. GEE was unbiased when cluster size was uninformative but biased under informative cluster size. Weighted CL-GLM led to under-coverage, whereas weighted IEE resulted in over-coverage. The CL-GLM had similar power to GEE and unweighted IEE. CONCLUSIONS: The cluster-level GLM with a normal distribution provides a robust alternative to estimating equation approaches for marginal odds ratios in cluster randomized trials, with good performance under both informative and uninformative cluster size and no loss of power. We recommend the cluster-level GLM with a normal distribution when the marginal cluster-average estimand is of interest, and unweighted IEEs when the marginal individual-average estimand is of interest.

Bayesian Methods Incorporating Baseline Covariates for Borrowing Historical Control Data in Randomized Clinical Trials: A Neutral Comparison Study

David Jesse, Diane Uschner, Markus C Elze, Tim Friede, Francois Mercier

摘要

The idea of leveraging historical control data in randomized clinical trials (RCTs) has gained a lot of attention, in particular for rare disease and pediatric indications where conducting traditional and fully powered RCTs can be challenging. In this regard, Bayesian dynamic borrowing (BDB) methods, which adapt the amount of borrowing based on the outcome heterogeneity between the current and historical data, are well established. In recent years, an increasing number of related methods have been published that combine the BDB framework with approaches that account for baseline covariates. The underlying rationale is that some of the heterogeneity may be explained systematically by variations in the distributions of these characteristics. While many such methods are now available, direct comparisons of them are scarce and there is no consensus on which one can be considered the state of the art. We address this issue by conducting a neutral comparison of three methods of this class, namely the propensity score weighted commensurate prior, propensity score weighted multi-source exchangeability models, and Bayesian additive regression trees. We assess their performance for continuous outcomes through extensive simulations and further evaluate them using two examples based on real RCT data. Our study reveals potential issues with some of these approaches and provides recommendations for choosing a particular method for real RCTs.

An Integrative Approach for Subtyping Mental Disorders Using Multimodal Data

Yinjun Zhao, Yuanjia Wang, Ying Liu

摘要

Understanding the biological and behavioral heterogeneity underlying psychiatric disorders is critical for advancing precision diagnosis, treatment, and prevention. This paper addresses the scientific question of how multimodal data, which spans clinical, cognitive, and neuroimaging measures, can be integrated to identify biologically meaningful subtypes of mental disorders. We introduce Mixed INtegrative Data Subtyping (MINDS), a Bayesian hierarchical model designed to jointly analyze mixed-type data for simultaneous dimension reduction and clustering. Using data from the adolescent brain cognitive development (ABCD) Study, MINDS integrates clinical symptoms, cognitive performance, and brain structure measures to subtype attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD). These findings show how integrative multimodal modeling can enhance the reproducibility and clinical relevance of psychiatric subtyping, funding data-driven policies for early identification and targeted interventions in mental health.

Addressing Missing Data in Clinical Trials With a Hybrid Control Arm

Joshua Wasserman, Herbert Pang, Jiawen Zhu

摘要

Randomized trials have long been the standard for evaluating experimental treatments in medical and pharmacological studies, but the associated time and cost have spurred research into designs that augment randomized studies with data from external sources. Hybrid control arms offer the promise of improved precision-at the possible expense of bias-by forming a control arm from patients randomized to control along with historical controls or patients found in a registry. Although recent reviews have published guidance on minimizing and addressing missing data in clinical trials, the issue has received little attention in the context of trials with a hybrid control arm. We propose novel treatment effect estimators that handle missing outcomes in both the trial and external control datasets, even when the missingness patterns differ between them. These estimators are built from augmented weighted estimating equations and are doubly robust when outcomes are missing at random (MAR). In addition, we introduce a sensitivity analysis using a pattern-mixture model to assess robustness of the effect estimates under violations of the MAR assumption. We compare these estimators to a popular parametric modeling technique through simulation and an application to a real-world trial.

Valid Inference for Two Sample Summary Statistics Mendelian Randomization

Kai Wang, Grace Z Wang

摘要

Mendelian randomization (MR) studies increasingly rely on summary statistics from genome-wide association studies (GWASs). However, current summary-data methods treat marginal GWAS effect estimates as independent across instrumental single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which holds only when the SNPs have no effect. Without imposing this independence assumption, we derive expressions for the two-stage least squares (TSLS), two-sample TSLS (TSTSLS), and generalized method-of-moments estimators together with their variance estimators using only GWAS summary statistics, assuming the instrumental SNPs are in linkage equilibrium. We show that the widely used inverse-variance weighted (IVW) estimator is generally not equivalent to either TSLS or TSTSLS, contrary to a common claim in the MR literature. We further demonstrate that the conventional IVW standard error is generally inconsistent and provide a consistent alternative. Extensive simulations confirm the theoretical results and reveal that Wald confidence intervals based on summary statistics can be anti-conservative. An application to publicly available GWAS summary statistics on body mass index and systolic blood pressure illustrates the practical implications of the proposed methodology.

Xijin Chen, Pavel Mozgunov, Richard D Baird, Thomas Jaki

摘要

Dose-finding trials for oncology studies are traditionally designed to assess safety in the early stages of drug development. With the rise of molecularly targeted therapies and immuno-oncology compounds, biomarker-driven approaches are increasingly importance. Using Causal Diagrams to Assess Parallel Trends in Difference-in-Differences Studies

Audrey Renson, Oliver Dukes, Zach Shahn

摘要

Difference-in-differences (DID) is popular because it can allow for unmeasured confounding when the key assumption of parallel trends holds. However, there exists little guidance on how to decide a priori whether this assumption is reasonable. We attempt to develop such guidance by considering the relationship between a causal diagram and the parallel trends assumption. This is challenging because parallel trends is scale-dependent and causal diagrams are generally scale-independent. We develop conditions under which, given a nonparametric causal diagram, one can reject or fail to reject parallel trends. In particular, we adopt a linear faithfulness assumption, which states that all graphically connected variables are correlated, and which is often reasonable in practice. We show that parallel trends can be rejected if either (i) the treatment is affected by pre-treatment outcomes, or (ii) there exist unmeasured confounders for the effect of treatment on pre-treatment outcomes that are not confounders for the post-treatment outcome, or vice versa. We also argue that parallel trends should be strongly questioned if (iii) the pre-treatment outcomes causally affect the post-treatment outcomes, since there exist reasonable semiparametric models in which such an effect violates parallel trends. When (i-iii) are absent, a necessary and sufficient condition for parallel trends is that the association between unmeasured confounders and potential outcomes is constant on an additive scale, pre- and post-treatment. We discuss our approach in the context of the effect of Medicaid expansion under the US Affordable Care Act on health insurance coverage rates.

The Role of Congeniality in Multiple Imputation for Doubly Robust Causal Estimation

Lucy D'Agostino McGowan

摘要

This paper provides clear and practical guidance on the specification of imputation models when multiple imputation is used in conjunction with doubly robust estimation methods for causal inference. Through theoretical arguments and targeted simulations, we demonstrate that if a confounder has missing data, the corresponding imputation model must include all variables appearing in either the propensity score model or the outcome model, in addition to both the exposure and outcome, and that these variables must appear in the same functional form as in the final analysis. Violating these conditions can lead to biased treatment effect estimates, even when both components of the doubly robust estimator are correctly specified.
