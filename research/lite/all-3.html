Semiparametric learning from open-set label shift data

Siyan Liu, Yukun Liu, Qinglong Tian, Pengfei Li, Jing Qin

摘要

We study the open-set label shift problem, where the test data may include a novel class absent from the training data. This setting is challenging because both the class proportions and the distribution of the novel class are not identifiable without extra assumptions. Existing approaches often rely on restrictive separability conditions, prior knowledge, or computationally infeasible procedures, and some may lack theoretical guarantees. We propose a semiparametric density ratio model framework that ensures identifiability while allowing overlap between novel and known classes. Within this framework, we develop maximum empirical likelihood estimators and confidence intervals for class proportions, establish their asymptotic validity, and design a stable Expectation–Maximization algorithm for computation. We also develop a goodness-of-fit test for model assessment and an approximately optimal classifier with theoretical guarantees. Simulations and a real-data application confirm that our methods improve both estimation accuracy and classification performance compared with existing approaches.

JASA High-Dimensional Latent Variables & Mixtures · 收录于 2026-10-26

Semi-supervised Clustering Through Representation Learning of High-dimensional Count Data

Linshanshan Wang, Mengyan Li, Zongqi Xia, Molei Liu, Tianxi Cai

摘要

High-dimensional count data arise in many modern applications, such as electronic health records (EHR) and RNA sequencing, with sparse, heterogeneous observations driven by complex latent structure. These characteristics make modeling and prediction challenging, especially when labels are limited. We propose SCORE, a semi-supervised representation learning framework for clustering and embedding construction from high-dimensional count features. SCORE is built on a Poisson-adapted latent factor mixture model that supports incorporating external pre-trained feature embeddings when available, enabling efficient extraction of latent cluster membership and low-dimensional representations. To scale inference and learning to large datasets, SCORE uses a hybrid algorithm that combines expectation-maximization with a Gaussian variational approximation, leveraging a small labeled subset to refine estimation on a large pool of unlabeled samples. We establish convergence guarantees for this hybrid procedure, quantify approximation errors introduced by the Gaussian variational approximation, and derive error rates under increasing embedding dimensions. Our theory and experiments show that incorporating unlabeled data improves accuracy and reduces sensitivity to label scarcity, yielding strong finite-sample performance relative to existing approaches. We demonstrate SCORE for predicting disability status in multiple sclerosis using EHR data, where it learns informative patient embeddings with strong predictive performance, highlighting its practical value.

SMMR Survival Analysis Nonparametric & Semiparametric · 收录于 2026-10-26

ROC-based methods for comparing two survival curves under non-proportional hazards with right, left, and doubly censored data

Mohammod Mahmudur Rahman, Leonidas E Bantis

摘要

Studies comparing two survival curves to assess treatment differences are commonplace. The widely used log-rank test is designed to operate best under the proportional hazards (PH) assumption. Alternative methods, such as the Fleming-Harrington weighted log-rank tests, rely on prior knowledge of survival patterns, which is often difficult to specify before a study. The recently developed MaxCombo test does not require such prior information; however, it leaves substantial room for power improvement, particularly when survival curves cross. To address these issues, we propose new nonparametric and semiparametric methods for comparing two survival curves based on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve length and the overlap coefficient, along with a joint inference approach combining both measures. Our methods are applicable under non-PH settings and can accommodate right, left, and double censoring. We evaluate our methods through extensive simulation studies under different non-PH configurations. Our results show improved performance of the proposed methods compared to existing nonparametric methods, particularly under early and crossing effects. Real-world examples involving various non-PH scenarios are provided, illustrating the superiority of our methods in detecting survival differences. We further provide an R package named ''ROCsurvcomp" that implements all our proposed approaches and is available on CRAN.

Partially Linear Additive Reversed Mean Model for Panel Count Data With Informative Terminal Event

Anyin Feng, Xiangbin Hu, Wen Su, Xingqiu Zhao

摘要

This article investigates a partially linear additive reversed mean model for panel count data with an informative terminal event. By anchoring the time origin at the terminal event, the proposed model characterizes the recurrent event process prior to the occurrence of the terminal event. The partially linear additive form provides a flexible framework for modeling covariate effects. The B-spline sieve approximation is employed for both the baseline reversed mean function and the link function. To make the optimization of the loss function feasible, we treat the distribution function of the terminal event as a nuisance functional parameter and develop a two-stage estimation procedure. We establish the asymptotic properties of the proposed estimator, including consistency, convergence rate, and asymptotic distribution. Simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed estimator performs well in finite-sample settings. The proposed method is further illustrated through an application to the Chinese longitudinal healthy longevity survey (CLHLS).

JASA Functional Data · 收录于 2026-10-26

Modeling Amplitude and Phase Variation of Multivariate Random Processes in Metric Spaces*

Yaqing Chen, Kyum Kim

摘要

In functional data analysis, distinguishing between amplitude and phase variation is essential to prevent distorted statistical inferences. While extensive work has been devoted to developing time-warping methods to address this issue for Euclidean functional data, much less is known about how to characterize and model these two sources of variability when they co-exist in random processes taking values in general metric spaces. In this paper, we develop a comprehensive statistical framework characterizing amplitude and phase variation for multivariate metric-space-valued functional data. We propose a geodesic latent deformation model (GLDM) for jointly analyzing both types of variation by using tools from metric geometry, and define tempo indices to quantify the temporal dynamics. We devise a novel estimation procedure and establish (uniform) convergence rates for the model estimators. The method’s broad applicability is demonstrated through a real-world application to annual gender-specific age-at-death distributions providing new insights into global longevity dynamics as well as simulations on multivariate positive semi-definite (PSD) matrix-valued and network-valued functional data.

Marginal Analysis of Clustered Interval-Censored Competing Risks Data With Informative Cluster Size

Xinyi Wang, Peijie Wang, Xinyuan Song

摘要

Motivated by the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC) study, this paper addresses the marginal analysis of clustered data under a complex setting involving interval censoring, competing risks, and informative cluster size, for which existing methods are not readily applicable. We consider a class of marginal generalized odds-rate transformation models for the cumulative incidence function in the presence of interval-censored competing risks, with the Fine-Gray model and the sub-distribution proportional odds model included as special cases. For statistical inference, a cluster-weighted sieve maximum pseudo-likelihood estimation procedure is developed, in which B-splines are used to approximate the nonparametric components of the semiparametric models. This method effectively reduces the original infinite-dimensional estimation problem to a finite-dimensional one. We establish the asymptotic properties of the resulting parameter estimators and conduct extensive simulation studies to evaluate their finite-sample performance. The proposed methodology is applied to the motivating NACC data to investigate potential risk factors for the cumulative incidence of Lewy body dementia.

Joint modeling of longitudinal measurements and survival outcomes in heart valve transplantation data

Avinash Kumar, M S Panwar, Sushil Kumar

摘要

A joint modeling technique for longitudinal data and time-to-event data is advanced statistical methodology to analyze repeated measurements and time-to-event outcomes. In the present article, a shared random effects approach is considered to account for both longitudinal data and survival outcomes. The longitudinal data is submodeled by a linear mixed-effects model that captures the individual response over time. Whereas the time-to-event sub-model uses the generalized exponential distribution as the baseline distribution for the proportional hazard model to incorporate survival times as well as time-independent covariates. To estimate model parameters, the Expectation-Maximization approach has been used, which is effective with latent random effects since random effects are unobservable in nature. In the E-step, the expected log-likelihood is derived based on current parameter estimates, and in the M-step, we maximize this likelihood to update the parameters. To show the efficacy of the proposed approach, an extensive simulation study has been conducted. Furthermore, the model is tested on a real-world medical dataset, heart valve data, to demonstrate its efficacy in analyzing the link between longitudinal responses and survival outcomes.

Estimating Marginal Odds Ratios for Cluster Randomized Trials: The Cluster-Level GLM

Jennifer A Thompson, Brennan C Kahan, Andrew Copas

摘要

BACKGROUND: Methods for estimating marginal effects in cluster randomized trials are limited. We describe and evaluate a cluster-level generalized linear model (CL-GLM) that applies a GLM to cluster-level summaries (means, proportions, or rates) and can estimate differences, ratios, or odds ratios. We focus on odds ratios. METHODS: We conducted a simulation study with 10-50 clusters, intracluster correlation coefficients of 0.05 or 0.15, and a mean cluster size of 120, either fixed or variable. Informative cluster size, where the intervention effect varies with cluster size, was also considered. Control arm prevalence was set to 50%. We compared cluster-level GLMs with normal and gamma distributions and logit link to generalized estimating equations (GEE) and independence estimating equations (IEEs). Weighted and unweighted analyses were examined to target cluster-average and individual-average estimands. RESULTS: CL-GLM with a normal distribution produced unbiased marginal cluster-average odds ratios with appropriate confidence interval coverage. CL-GLM with a gamma distribution resulted in over-coverage. Unweighted IEE produced unbiased marginal individual-average odds ratios with correct coverage. GEE was unbiased when cluster size was uninformative but biased under informative cluster size. Weighted CL-GLM led to under-coverage, whereas weighted IEE resulted in over-coverage. The CL-GLM had similar power to GEE and unweighted IEE. CONCLUSIONS: The cluster-level GLM with a normal distribution provides a robust alternative to estimating equation approaches for marginal odds ratios in cluster randomized trials, with good performance under both informative and uninformative cluster size and no loss of power. We recommend the cluster-level GLM with a normal distribution when the marginal cluster-average estimand is of interest, and unweighted IEEs when the marginal individual-average estimand is of interest.

JASA Causal Inference Networks & Graphs Genetics & Omics · 收录于 2026-10-26

Confounder-robust causal discovery and inference in Perturb-seq using proxy and instrumental variables

Kwangmoon Park, Hongzhe Li

摘要

Emerging single-cell technologies that combine CRISPR-based genetic perturbations with single-cell RNA sequencing, such as Perturb-seq, offer unprecedented opportunities to uncover cause-and-effect relationships among genes. Nonetheless, Perturb-seq experiments are subject to unobserved factors that, if not properly handled, can severely bias the inferred causal relationships between genes. These latent factors may arise not only from intrinsic molecular features of the regulatory elements, but also from unmeasured genes omitted due to cost-constrained experimental designs. Although methods for analyzing large-scale Perturb-seq data are rapidly maturing, approaches that explicitly account for such unobserved confounders when inferring causal gene networks are still lacking. Here, we propose a novel approach to accurately reconstruct causal gene networks from Perturb-seq data even when important confounders are missing. Our framework leverages proxy and instrumental variable strategies to exploit the rich information embedded in the perturbations, enabling unbiased estimation of the underlying directed acyclic graph (DAG) of gene expression. Applications to both comprehensive synthetic data and real CRISPR interference experiments in K562 cells demonstrate that our method outperforms baseline approaches that lack principled adjustments for unmeasured confounding, yielding more accurate and biologically relevant recovery of the true causal DAGs.

Cluster-level analysis in three-level cluster randomised trials with multi-level covariate-dependent missingness

Satish Pradhan, Priyanka Majumder

摘要

Community-based public health interventions often use three-level cluster randomised trials (CRTs), for example, individuals are nested within households which are again nested within communities. In these trials, outcome missingness commonly depends on baseline covariates measured at multiple levels. Although two-level CRTs are well studied, the effects of subcluster-level attrition and hierarchical covariate correlations on bias and variance are not yet fully explored. We develop an analytical framework for three-level CRTs under multi-level covariate-dependent missingness (CDM), explicitly incorporating within- and between-subcluster covariate correlations. We examine the performance of unadjusted and covariate-adjusted cluster-level treatment effect estimators using Monte Carlo simulations and a plasmode simulation based on the 2021 Madagascar Demographic and Health Survey. Our study reveals that unadjusted cluster-level analyses produce biased estimates under multi-level CDM. Linear mixed models are unbiased when correctly specified under missing-at-random assumptions but may be computationally unstable in small samples. The covariate-adjusted cluster-level estimator remains unbiased when covariate effects and missingness mechanisms are homogeneous across treatment arms. Subcluster-level attrition can lead to greater variance inflation than individual-level attrition. For CRTs with fewer than 30 clusters per arm and homogeneous treatment effects, covariate-adjusted cluster-level analysis may serve as a practical alternative to mixed models. A decision flowchart is provided to guide method selection.

Blockwise dynamic selection of smoothing parameters for kernel estimators of nonstationary densities of streaming data

F Camirand Lemyre, A Delaigle, J Mao

摘要

We consider nonparametric estimation of the nonstationary density of streaming data collected continuously over time. Those types of data are often available only for a limited time after being observed, for example due to storage or data privacy constraints. Therefore, analyzing them requires dynamic approaches that do not require repeated access to past data. A suitable iterative kernel estimator of nonstationary densities has been developed by Rutkowski (1989); it requires choosing important tuning parameters each time a new observation arises, for which no adequate practical selection criterion has been developed yet.We propose a data-driven dynamic selection of those parameters, which can be implemented iteratively and requires only sequential access to consecutive blocks of the most recent data. To choose the sizes of the blocks, we propose a theoretically founded approach based on properties of cross-validation criteria. We illustrate the broader potential of our ideas by suggesting a dynamic blockwise practical implementation of another kernel estimator of nonstationary densities developed by Hall et al. (2006).We apply our technique to simulated data and to light curve data from the NASA Kepler mission.

Bayesian Methods Incorporating Baseline Covariates for Borrowing Historical Control Data in Randomized Clinical Trials: A Neutral Comparison Study

David Jesse, Diane Uschner, Markus C Elze, Tim Friede, Francois Mercier

摘要

The idea of leveraging historical control data in randomized clinical trials (RCTs) has gained a lot of attention, in particular for rare disease and pediatric indications where conducting traditional and fully powered RCTs can be challenging. In this regard, Bayesian dynamic borrowing (BDB) methods, which adapt the amount of borrowing based on the outcome heterogeneity between the current and historical data, are well established. In recent years, an increasing number of related methods have been published that combine the BDB framework with approaches that account for baseline covariates. The underlying rationale is that some of the heterogeneity may be explained systematically by variations in the distributions of these characteristics. While many such methods are now available, direct comparisons of them are scarce and there is no consensus on which one can be considered the state of the art. We address this issue by conducting a neutral comparison of three methods of this class, namely the propensity score weighted commensurate prior, propensity score weighted multi-source exchangeability models, and Bayesian additive regression trees. We assess their performance for continuous outcomes through extensive simulations and further evaluate them using two examples based on real RCT data. Our study reveals potential issues with some of these approaches and provides recommendations for choosing a particular method for real RCTs.

An Integrative Approach for Subtyping Mental Disorders Using Multimodal Data

Yinjun Zhao, Yuanjia Wang, Ying Liu

摘要

Understanding the biological and behavioral heterogeneity underlying psychiatric disorders is critical for advancing precision diagnosis, treatment, and prevention. This paper addresses the scientific question of how multimodal data, which spans clinical, cognitive, and neuroimaging measures, can be integrated to identify biologically meaningful subtypes of mental disorders. We introduce Mixed INtegrative Data Subtyping (MINDS), a Bayesian hierarchical model designed to jointly analyze mixed-type data for simultaneous dimension reduction and clustering. Using data from the adolescent brain cognitive development (ABCD) Study, MINDS integrates clinical symptoms, cognitive performance, and brain structure measures to subtype attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD). Our method leverages Pólya-Gamma augmentation for computational efficiency and robust inference. Simulations demonstrate improved stability and accuracy compared to existing clustering approaches. Application to the ABCD data reveals clinically interpretable subtypes of ADHD and OCD with distinct cognitive and neurodevelopmental profiles. These findings show how integrative multimodal modeling can enhance the reproducibility and clinical relevance of psychiatric subtyping, funding data-driven policies for early identification and targeted interventions in mental health.

Addressing Missing Data in Clinical Trials With a Hybrid Control Arm

Joshua Wasserman, Herbert Pang, Jiawen Zhu

摘要

Randomized trials have long been the standard for evaluating experimental treatments in medical and pharmacological studies, but the associated time and cost have spurred research into designs that augment randomized studies with data from external sources. Hybrid control arms offer the promise of improved precision-at the possible expense of bias-by forming a control arm from patients randomized to control along with historical controls or patients found in a registry. Although recent reviews have published guidance on minimizing and addressing missing data in clinical trials, the issue has received little attention in the context of trials with a hybrid control arm. We propose novel treatment effect estimators that handle missing outcomes in both the trial and external control datasets, even when the missingness patterns differ between them. These estimators are built from augmented weighted estimating equations and are doubly robust when outcomes are missing at random (MAR). In addition, we introduce a sensitivity analysis using a pattern-mixture model to assess robustness of the effect estimates under violations of the MAR assumption. We compare these estimators to a popular parametric modeling technique through simulation and an application to a real-world trial.

A seamless dose-optimization design for monotherapy and combination therapy

Kentaro Takeda, Masahiro Kojima

摘要

The emergence of molecular-targeted agents and immune-oncology therapies has fundamentally transformed oncology drug development, necessitating evolution beyond traditional dose-finding approaches designed for cytotoxic agents. While conventional agents exhibit predictable monotonic dose-response relationships, novel anticancer agents often demonstrate plateau-effect patterns where higher doses may compromise therapeutic benefit, requiring identification of optimal biological doses that balance efficacy and tolerability. The FDA's Project Optimus initiative emphasizes comprehensive dose optimization through parallel randomized cohorts and patient backfilling to better understand pharmacological profiles across multiple dose levels. Contemporary drug development increasingly prioritizes combination therapy alongside monotherapy evaluation, yet existing designs typically assume equivalent roles for both agents, diverging from clinical practice where novel agents combine with established treatments having limited dose options. This article proposes a seamless dose-optimization design that adaptively evaluates both monotherapy and combination therapy based on efficacy and toxicity outcomes through adaptive subtrials with patient backfilling capabilities. The model-assisted framework employs predetermined Bayesian optimal boundaries, eliminating real-time model fitting while accommodating evaluation of both monotherapy and combination therapy and enabling sequential enrollment with strategic backfilling. Simulation studies demonstrate robust performance across diverse dose-response patterns relevant to contemporary oncology. The design addresses critical gaps between methodological assumptions and clinical reality, offering a practical approach that integrates monotherapy and combination therapy evaluation with efficacy-toxicity-based backfilling. This framework ensures straightforward implementation while maintaining flexibility for complex dose-response relationships characteristic of novel anticancer agents, providing significant advantages for clinical implementation in the era of targeted and immune-oncology therapeutics.

A multivariate Bayesian biomarker screening algorithm that incorporates clinical covariates

摘要

Advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has limited treatment options and poor survival. Early detection of HCC improves survival, but current ultrasound screening is inconsistent and poorly adhered to. Biomarker-based blood tests offer a more accurate, accessible, and reliable alternative. Recent developments in methods for early detection of HCC have focused on the trajectories of multiple biomarkers, assuming that biomarkers follow a joint hierarchical mixture model with random changepoints. A Spatial Interference Approach to Account for Mobility in Air Pollution Studies with Multivariate Continuous Treatments

Heejun Shin, Danielle Braun, Kezia Irene, Michelle Audirac, Joseph Antonelli

摘要

We develop new methodology to improve our understanding of the causal effects of multivariate air pollution exposures on public health accounting for mobility. Typically, in environmental health studies, exposure to air pollution for an individual is assigned based on their residential address, though many people spend time in different regions with potentially different levels of air pollution. To account for this, we incorporate estimates of the mobility of individuals from cell phone mobility data to obtain a more accurate estimate of their air pollution exposure. We treat this as an interference problem, where individuals in one geographic region can be affected by exposures in other regions due to mobility into those areas. We propose policy-relevant estimands and derive expressions showing the extent of bias one would obtain by ignoring individuals’ mobility. We additionally highlight the benefits of the proposed interference framework relative to a measurement error framework to account for mobility. Utilizing flexible Bayesian methodology we develop novel estimation strategies to estimate causal effects that account for this spatial spillover. Lastly, we use the proposed methodology to study the health effects of ambient air pollution on mortality among Medicare enrollees in the United States.

JASA Bayesian Methods · 收录于 2026-10-26

A Generalized Tangent Approximation based Variational Inference Framework for Strongly Super-Gaussian Likelihoods

Somjit Roy, Pritam Dey, Debdeep Pati, Bani K. Mallick

摘要

Variational inference, as an alternative to Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling, has played a transformative role in enabling scalable computation for complex Bayesian models. Nevertheless, existing approaches often depend on either rigid model-specific formulations or stochastic black-box optimization routines. Tangent approximation is a principled class of structured variational methods that exploits the geometry of the underlying probability model. However, its utility has largely been confined to logistic regression and related modeling regimes. In this article, we propose a novel variational framework based on tangent transformation for a broad class of probability models characterized by strongly super-Gaussian likelihoods. Our method leverages convex duality to construct tangent minorants of the log-likelihood, thereby inducing conjugacy with Gaussian priors over model parameters in an otherwise intractable setup. Under mild assumptions on the data-generating mechanism, we establish algorithmic convergence guarantees, a contribution that stands in contrast to the limited theoretical assurances typically available for black-box variational methods. Additionally, we derive near-parametric variational risk bounds. Superior performance of our proposed methodology is illustrated on simulated and real-data scenarios that challenge state-of-the-art variational algorithms in terms of scalability and their ability to consistently capture complex underlying data structure.

A Factor Model Based Time-Varying Auto-Align Overlapping Clustering

Yanxin Jin, Peiyao Cai, Janice Chen, Yang Ning, Kean Ming Tan

摘要

In many scientific fields such as genomics and neuroscience, data often exhibit complex structures where features can belong to multiple clusters simultaneously and vary over time. Traditional clustering methods such as k-means and hierarchical clustering typically assume static cluster structures and perform hard assignments, limiting their ability to capture the underlying overlapping and time-varying cluster structures. To address these challenges, we propose a novel time-varying Auto-Align clustering (AAclust) framework that effectively estimates and tracks dynamic changes in overlapping cluster memberships over time. Specifically, we employ a time-varying factor model, in which the overlapping cluster structures are modeled through a factor loading matrix that is allowed to vary over time. Theoretically, we show that the population factor loading matrix is identifiable up to a signed permutation across time, i.e., the population overlapping cluster memberships are identifiable. Furthermore, we show that our framework can consistently estimate the number of clusters and the overlapping cluster memberships for the features. By leveraging cross-covariance matrices between any two time points, our proposed method automatically aligns cluster labels over time without requiring post-hoc label matching based on similarity measures like the Rand index. We evaluate the performance of our proposed method through numerical experiments and an application to a functional magnetic resonance imaging dataset under continuous natural stimuli.

Valid Inference for Two Sample Summary Statistics Mendelian Randomization

Kai Wang, Grace Z Wang

摘要

Mendelian randomization (MR) studies increasingly rely on summary statistics from genome-wide association studies (GWASs). However, current summary-data methods treat marginal GWAS effect estimates as independent across instrumental single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which holds only when the SNPs have no effect. Without imposing this independence assumption, we derive expressions for the two-stage least squares (TSLS), two-sample TSLS (TSTSLS), and generalized method-of-moments estimators together with their variance estimators using only GWAS summary statistics, assuming the instrumental SNPs are in linkage equilibrium. We show that the widely used inverse-variance weighted (IVW) estimator is generally not equivalent to either TSLS or TSTSLS, contrary to a common claim in the MR literature. We further demonstrate that the conventional IVW standard error is generally inconsistent and provide a consistent alternative. Extensive simulations confirm the theoretical results and reveal that Wald confidence intervals based on summary statistics can be anti-conservative. An application to publicly available GWAS summary statistics on body mass index and systolic blood pressure illustrates the practical implications of the proposed methodology.

Valid Data-Driven Subgroup Selection With Uniform Guarantees in Clinical Trials

摘要

Subgroup selection in clinical trials is essential for identifying patient groups that react differently to a treatment, thereby enabling personalized medicine. In particular, subgroup selection can identify patient groups that respond particularly well to a treatment or that encounter adverse events more often. However, this is a post-selection inference problem, which may pose challenges for traditional techniques used for subgroup analysis, such as increased Type I error rates and potential biases from data-driven subgroup identification. In this paper, we discuss two recently developed methods for subgroup selection in regression problems: one based on generalized linear modeling and another on isotonic regression. A thorough simulation study is conducted to evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of each method, providing detailed insight into the sensitivity of the Type I error rate control to modeling assumptions.

Using Joint Models in Phase I Dose-Finding Designs in Oncology: Considerations for Frequentist Approaches

Xijin Chen, Pavel Mozgunov, Richard D Baird, Thomas Jaki

摘要

Dose-finding trials for oncology studies are traditionally designed to assess safety in the early stages of drug development. With the rise of molecularly targeted therapies and immuno-oncology compounds, biomarker-driven approaches are increasingly importance. In this article, we propose a novel approach to dose-finding that incorporates biomarkers which predict for the subsequent development of clinical toxicity. The proposed joint model framework, which incorporates repeated biomarker measurements, is compared to the likelihood-based continual reassessment method (CRM) using only binary toxicity data, across various dose-toxicity relationship scenarios. Using Causal Diagrams to Assess Parallel Trends in Difference-in-Differences Studies

Audrey Renson, Oliver Dukes, Zach Shahn

摘要

Difference-in-differences (DID) is popular because it can allow for unmeasured confounding when the key assumption of parallel trends holds. However, there exists little guidance on how to decide a priori whether this assumption is reasonable. We attempt to develop such guidance by considering the relationship between a causal diagram and the parallel trends assumption. This is challenging because parallel trends is scale-dependent and causal diagrams are generally scale-independent. We develop conditions under which, given a nonparametric causal diagram, one can reject or fail to reject parallel trends. In particular, we adopt a linear faithfulness assumption, which states that all graphically connected variables are correlated, and which is often reasonable in practice. We show that parallel trends can be rejected if either (i) the treatment is affected by pre-treatment outcomes, or (ii) there exist unmeasured confounders for the effect of treatment on pre-treatment outcomes that are not confounders for the post-treatment outcome, or vice versa. We also argue that parallel trends should be strongly questioned if (iii) the pre-treatment outcomes causally affect the post-treatment outcomes, since there exist reasonable semiparametric models in which such an effect violates parallel trends. When (i-iii) are absent, a necessary and sufficient condition for parallel trends is that the association between unmeasured confounders and potential outcomes is constant on an additive scale, pre- and post-treatment. We discuss our approach in the context of the effect of Medicaid expansion under the US Affordable Care Act on health insurance coverage rates.

Unified conformalized multiple testing with full data efficiency

Yuyang Huo, Xiaoyang Wu, Changliang Zou, Haojie Ren

摘要

Conformalized multiple testing offers a model-free way to control predictive uncertainty in decision-making. Existing methods typically use only part of the available data to build score functions tailored to specific settings. We propose a unified framework that puts data utilization at the centre: it uses all available data—null, alternative, and unlabelled—to construct scores and calibrate p-values through a full permutation strategy. This unified use of all available data significantly improves power by enhancing nonconformity score quality and maximizing calibration set size while rigorously controlling the false discovery rate. Crucially, our framework provides a systematic design principle for conformal testing and enables automatic selection of the best conformal procedure among candidates without extra data splitting. Extensive numerical experiments demonstrate that our enhanced methods deliver superior efficiency and adaptability across diverse scenarios.

Transportable inference using target population summary statistics under covariate shift

Ying Sheng, Yifei Sun, Chiung-Yu Huang

摘要

Transporting findings from a study population to a target population is central to evidence-based decision-making in real-world settings. Most existing methods require individual-level data from both populations to account for covariate shift. However, privacy regulations and data-sharing constraints often preclude access to such data from the target population, leaving only covariate summaries available for analysis. In this paper, we develop transportability methods that enable valid inference using source individual-level data and target covariate summaries.
