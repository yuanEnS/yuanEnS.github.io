Self-selection bias in evaluating the effectiveness of mammography screening: A sensitivity analysis on data from the German screening program

Maria Veronica Vinattieri, Laura Buschmann, Jürgen Wellmann, André Karch, Heike Minnerup, Keith Humphreys

摘要

Self-selection bias in observational studies assessing the effectiveness of a breast cancer screening program can arise when non-participants of screening have worse general health conditions or lower compliance with treatments than screening participants. Building on recently described methodology for tumour progression modelling and causal survival analysis of screening data, our main contribution is to describe approaches to conduct sensitivity analyses for self-selection effects when studying screening effects on all-cause survival and breast cancer cumulative incidence, among screen-detected cases, in a population where all individuals are offered screening. Sensitivity analyses are inspired by an approach to correcting for self-selection bias that has been used for observational studies of mortality. We report simulation studies that demonstrate the impact that a correction factor has on all-cause survival and on the cumulative incidence of breast cancer death following diagnosis. We also apply our methods to estimate the benefit of early detection in non-overdiagnosed screen-detected cancers using data from the German mammography screening program. For sensitivity parameter values, we leverage on data on other (non-breast cancer) cause survival among participants and non-participants of mammography screening in the same study and estimate how counterfactual all-cause survival is improved, and breast cancer cumulative incidence is reduced for patients with screen-detected cancers, when accounting for self-selection bias.

Robustness intervals for competing risks analysis with causes of failure missing not at random

Giorgos Bakoyannis, Aristofanis Rontogiannis, Ying Zhang, Wanzhu Tu, Ann Mwangi, Constantin T Yiannoutsos

摘要

Analysis of competing risks data is often complicated by incomplete or selectively missing information on the cause of failure. Standard approaches typically assume that the cause of failure is missing at random (MAR), an assumption that is generally untestable and frequently implausible in observational studies. We propose a novel sensitivity analysis framework for the proportional cause-specific hazards model that accommodates missing-not-at-random (MNAR) scenarios. A sensitivity parameter is used to quantify the association between missingness and the unobserved cause of failure. Regression coefficients are estimated as functions of this parameter, and a simultaneous confidence band is constructed via a wild bootstrap procedure. This allows identification of a range of MNAR scenarios for which effects remain statistically significant; we refer to this range as a robustness interval. The validity of the proposed approach is justified both theoretically, via empirical process theory, and empirically, through simulation studies. We apply the method to the analysis of data from an HIV cohort study in sub-Saharan Africa, where a substantial proportion of causes of failure are missing and the MAR assumption is implausible. The analysis shows that key findings regarding risk factors for care interruption and mortality are robust across a broad spectrum of MNAR scenarios, underscoring the method's utility in situations with MNAR causes of failure.

Robust transfer learning for accelerated failure time model

Chaoqi Wang, Jie Ding, Xiaoguang Wang

摘要

Limited sample sizes can impair the statistical accuracy of traditional survival models, resulting in potentially unsatisfactory conclusions. To address this issue, we propose a robust transfer learning framework for the accelerated failure time model (RTrans-AFT) that enables efficient and flexible information borrowing from an external source. The method accommodates both individual-level and summary-level source data and models differences in regression coefficients between cohorts without requiring identical covariate distributions. To control the degree of transfer, we impose an adaptive lasso penalty to control the discrepancy between the source and target parameters. This allows the method to selectively borrow information from the source cohort in the presence of heterogeneity, while fully incorporating external information when the two cohorts are comparable. Importantly, the framework is robust to potential data irregularities, such as outliers, which are common in real-world survival datasets. We establish the consistency and asymptotic normality of the proposed estimator. Simulation studies demonstrate that RTrans-AFT substantially improves estimation accuracy and achieves notable variance reduction, leading to enhanced statistical efficiency. The practical utility of the method is further illustrated through an analysis of lung adenocarcinoma data, where the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) registry data serve as the external source.

Bivariate Copula Models With Event-Free Cure Effect for Survival Extrapolations and Individual-Level Correlation Analysis

Daniel J Sharpe, Georgia Yates, Tuli De, Renuka Kandikatla, Prianka Singh, Matthew Dixon, Kiran Davé, Jackie Vanderpuye-Orgle, Murat Kurt

摘要

OBJECTIVES: Overall survival (OS) extrapolations from mixture cure models (MCMs) are often sensitive to the cure fraction, which is challenging to estimate when data are immature. Furthermore, in some cancer populations, it may be unreasonable to assume curative effects among patients who are not event-free. We addressed these problems using bivariate copula MCMs to jointly model OS and progression- (PFS) or recurrence-free survival (RFS), with a single event-free cure fraction parameter. METHODS: We proposed a practical framework for parametric survival copula model selection and interpretation, which involves estimating five representative candidate copula functions and examining OS distributions conditioned on intermediate event times. Bivariate MCMs with event-free cure fraction, based on previously selected marginal distributions, were estimated for two studies of nivolumab with contrasting survival patterns, namely, in previously treated advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (OS-PFS correlation) and resected melanoma (OS-RFS correlation). CONCLUSIONS: Bivariate MCMs with event-free cured subpopulation incorporated curative effects into OS extrapolations more cautiously than conventional MCMs, based on a more rapidly maturing composite endpoint. The model is especially useful when OS data are immature and if post-event cure is clinically implausible. Employing alternative copulas that cover archetypal association patterns mitigated model selection bias in patient-level correlation analysis.

摘要

The growing availability of large-scale survival data streams present significant challenges for statistical inference particularly in the presence of a cure fraction, which complicates estimation and increases computational burden. To address these challenges, we propose an online updating mixture cure model framework. Our approach employs a batchwise renewable estimation procedure that aggregates historical information into summary statistics to update the model without retaining historical individual-level data, and thus eliminating the need for full data storage. 摘要

ROC-based methods for comparing two survival curves under non-proportional hazards with right, left, and doubly censored data

Mohammod Mahmudur Rahman, Leonidas E Bantis

摘要

Studies comparing two survival curves to assess treatment differences are commonplace. The widely used log-rank test is designed to operate best under the proportional hazards (PH) assumption. Alternative methods, such as the Fleming-Harrington weighted log-rank tests, rely on prior knowledge of survival patterns, which is often difficult to specify before a study. The recently developed MaxCombo test does not require such prior information; however, it leaves substantial room for power improvement, particularly when survival curves cross. To address these issues, we propose new nonparametric and semiparametric methods for comparing two survival curves based on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve length and the overlap coefficient, along with a joint inference approach combining both measures. Our methods are applicable under non-PH settings and can accommodate right, left, and double censoring. We evaluate our methods through extensive simulation studies under different non-PH configurations. Our results show improved performance of the proposed methods compared to existing nonparametric methods, particularly under early and crossing effects. Real-world examples involving various non-PH scenarios are provided, illustrating the superiority of our methods in detecting survival differences. We further provide an R package named ''ROCsurvcomp" that implements all our proposed approaches and is available on CRAN.

Xinyi Wang, Peijie Wang, Xinyuan Song

摘要

Motivated by the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC) study, this paper addresses the marginal analysis of clustered data under a complex setting involving interval censoring, competing risks, and informative cluster size, for which existing methods are not readily applicable. Joint modeling of longitudinal measurements and survival outcomes in heart valve transplantation data

Avinash Kumar, M S Panwar, Sushil Kumar

摘要

A joint modeling technique for longitudinal data and time-to-event data is advanced statistical methodology to analyze repeated measurements and time-to-event outcomes. In the present article, a shared random effects approach is considered to account for both longitudinal data and survival outcomes. The longitudinal data is submodeled by a linear mixed-effects model that captures the individual response over time. Whereas the time-to-event sub-model uses the generalized exponential distribution as the baseline distribution for the proportional hazard model to incorporate survival times as well as time-independent covariates. To show the efficacy of the proposed approach, an extensive simulation study has been conducted. Furthermore, the model is tested on a real-world medical dataset, heart valve data, to demonstrate its efficacy in analyzing the link between longitudinal responses and survival outcomes.

Fuyu Guo, Zihan Qian, Wenyi Wang, Fenglei Wang, Bernard Rosner, Molin Wang

摘要

External exposures take time to accumulate and affect human health, leading to a time lag between initial exposure and the manifestation of clinical signs. Susceptible time window analysis aims to describe the relationship between the time since exposure and its effects. To obtain unbiased estimates in susceptible time window analysis, researchers may need to adjust for exposures measured at different times simultaneously. Conventional Cox proportional hazards models often fail in this regard due to the strong multicollinearity between lagged exposure terms. In addition to estimation challenges, missing data in historical exposures present another obstacle for long-term analysis. To address these challenges, we first impose flexible parametric models on the effects across historical exposures by assuming that they follow restricted cubic splines. Based on our proposed restricted cubic spline model, we develop two model selection algorithms to help researchers determine the optimal knot locations in the splines. Additionally, we propose using multiple imputations to handle the missing exposure data. We conducted simulation studies to evaluate the performance of the restricted cubic spline models and the imputation algorithms in various realistic settings. The results show that our model demonstrates strong performance, offering great flexibility and robustness to misspecified maximum length of effect. Compared to the complete-person-time analysis, our multiple imputation algorithm exhibits significantly lower variance, with only a slight increase in bias. Finally, as an illustrative example, we apply our proposed models to analyze the effect of folate consumption on colorectal cancer incidence in the Nurses' Health Study from 1976 to 2016.

Strategy to Control Biases in Prior Event Rate Ratio Method, With Application to Palliative Care in Patients With Advanced Cancer

Xiangmei Ma, Grace Meijuan Yang, Qingyuan Zhuang, Yin Bun Cheung

摘要

Prior event rate ratio (PERR) is a method shown to perform well in mitigating confounding and is gaining popularity in real-world evidence research. However, it depends on several model assumptions. We propose an analytic strategy to correct biases arising from violation of two model assumptions, namely, population homogeneity and event-independent treatment. We propose a reformulation of PERR estimation by embedding a treatment-by-period interaction term in the Andersen-Gill model for recurrent event data, which is robust to bias arising from unobserved heterogeneity. Based on this model, we propose a set of methods to examine the presence of event-dependent treatment and to correct the resultant bias. We evaluate the proposed methods by simulation and apply it to a de-identified dataset on palliative care and emergency department visits in patients with advanced cancer. Simulation results showed that the proposed method could mitigate the two sources of bias in PERR. In the palliative care study, analysis by the Cox model showed that patients who had started receiving palliative care had higher incidence of emergency department visits than their match controls (hazard ratio 3.31; 95% confidence interval 2.78-3.94). Using PERR without the proposed bias control strategy indicated a 19% reduction of the incidence (0.81; 0.64-1.02). However, there was evidence of event-dependent treatment. The proposed correction method showed no effect of palliative care on ED visits (1.00; 0.79-1.26). In conclusion, the proposed analytic strategy can control two sources of biases in the PERR approach. It enriches the armamentarium for real-world evidence research.

Nonparametric Bayesian Adjustment of Unmeasured Confounders in Cox Proportional Hazards Models

Shunichiro Orihara, Shonosuke Sugasawa, Tomohiro Ohigashi, Keita Hirano, Tomoyuki Nakagawa, Masataka Taguri

摘要

Unmeasured confounders pose a major challenge in accurately estimating causal effects in observational studies. To address this issue when estimating hazard ratios (HRs) using Cox proportional hazards models, several methods, including instrumental variables (IVs) approaches, have been proposed. However, these methods often face limitations, such as weak IV problems and restrictive assumptions regarding unmeasured confounder distributions. In this study, we introduce a novel nonparametric Bayesian procedure that provides accurate HR estimates while addressing these limitations. A key assumption of our approach is that unmeasured confounders exhibit a cluster structure. Under this assumption, we integrate two remarkable Bayesian techniques, the Dirichlet process mixture (DPM) and general Bayes (GB), to simultaneously (1) detect latent clusters based on the likelihood of exposure and outcome variables and (2) estimate HRs using the likelihood constructed within each cluster. Notably, leveraging DPM, our procedure eliminates the need for IVs by identifying unmeasured confounders under an alternative condition. Additionally, GB techniques remove the need for explicit modeling of the baseline hazard function, distinguishing our procedure from traditional Bayesian approaches. Simulation experiments demonstrate that the proposed Bayesian procedure outperforms existing methods in some performance metrics. Moreover, it achieves statistical efficiency comparable to the efficient estimator while accurately identifying cluster structures. These features highlight its ability to overcome challenges associated with traditional IV approaches for time-to-event data.

Meta-analytical federated surveillance for incubation period with interval- and right-censored data with stochastic variational inference

Daisuke Yoneoka, Takayuki Kawashima, Yuta Tanoue, Shuhei Nomura, Akifumi Eguchi

摘要

Accurate estimation of incubation period is fundamental to surveillance for infectious diseases. This task faces two major challenges: (1) surveillance data are often coarsely observed, comprising interval- and right-censored observations, and (2) patient-level data are frequently fragmented across multiple facilities and cannot be pooled due to privacy constraints. While traditional meta-analysis (MA) aggregates summary statistics, its one-shot nature limits the ability to fit complex hierarchical models. We propose a novel federated Bayesian framework, Federated Stochastic Variational Inference (Fed-SVI), which extends the summary-statistic philosophy of MA into an iterative, model-based federated approach. Our method fits a hierarchical lognormal model, explicitly modeling heterogeneity while estimating a global distribution. To fit this model privately, Fed-SVI iteratively exchanges summary statistics (local variational parameters). Each facility analytically computes these local parameters, rigorously incorporating the likelihood contributions of coarsely-observed data via expectations of the truncated normal distribution. A central server aggregates these parameters using stochastic natural-gradient ascent, enabling efficient information borrowing and mini-batch updates. Through extensive simulations, we demonstrate that Fed-SVI achieves accuracy nearly identical to a fully centralized analysis, while substantially outperforming naive federated or MA methods that discard censored information. We apply Fed-SVI to a distributed COVID-19 line list from 177 facilities, yielding results consistent with centralized estimates. Fed-SVI provides a scalable, accurate, and privacy-preserving solution for robust surveillance in modern data networks.

Locally Estimated Scatterplot Smoothing for Censored Survival Data

David van Klaveren, Peter C Austin, Patrick W Serruys, Ben van Calster, Frank E Harrell

摘要

In the absence of censoring, locally estimated scatterplot smoothing (LOESS) is a powerful tool to estimate the relationship between a continuous covariate and the probability of an event and is often used to visually assess calibration of prognostic models. Since follow-up in prognostic studies is often incomplete, we aimed to study the extension of LOESS to right-censored data. In line with other work, we first obtained inverse-probability-of-censoring (IPC) weights through Kaplan-Meier estimation. When censoring varied by covariate levels, we used Cox regression with a spline function of the covariate to obtain IPC weights. We then applied LOESS to the weighted event indicators to estimate the relationship between the covariate and the probability of an event within a specific time horizon. We illustrated the application of LOESS for censored data (LOESScd) using patients with complex coronary artery disease. We used the LOESScd to estimate the association between continuous predictors and the probability of death within 10 years. LOESScd estimates closely followed grouped Kaplan-Meier estimates, especially in case of weak smoothing. Covariate-dependent IPC weights improved LOESScd when censoring varied by covariate levels. We then examined the finite-sample behavior of LOESScd in simulations motivated by four typical prognostic model validation scenarios. In 2000 samples of 1800 patients, bias was limited and coverage was close to nominal. In conclusion, LOESScd is a promising estimator of the relationship of a continuous covariate with the probability of an event in the presence of right-censoring. LOESScd is especially useful for estimating calibration curves when validating prognostic models.

Group Sequential Sample Size for Comparing Two Survival Probabilities at a Specific Time Point

Susan Halabi, Lu Liu, Chenxi Yu, Yuan Wu

摘要

We propose a novel method that simultaneously determines the sample size for testing two survival probabilities at a pre-specified time while guaranteeing Type I error control in both fixed and group-sequential trial designs. Simulations across varying hypothesized differences, failure distributions, censoring proportions, and nominal powers demonstrate consistent performance, while interim analyses highlight maintained nominal Type I error control and increased power at each look relative to the standard binomial approach, regardless of the underlying failure time distribution or spending function. Importantly, our method is especially useful for evaluating survival outcomes at a fixed time in randomized trials where one treatment arm includes neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgery while the other involves surgery alone. The method is also applicable to randomized Phase II trials, where smaller sample sizes and the use of intermediate or surrogate time-to-event endpoints demand efficient data use and robust error control. We illustrate the approach with motivating examples in renal and prostate cancer. An accompanying R Shiny application enables investigators to compute sample sizes interactively, facilitating practical trial planning in diverse settings.

Clinically Interpretable Time-To-Event Testing in Randomized Clinical Trials: Milestone Survival Combined With Restricted Mean Survival Time

Siwen He, Ludovic Trinquart

摘要

The log-rank test and hazard ratio (HR) are widely used to compare survival curves in event-driven randomized clinical trials (RCTs). The optimality of the log-rank test and interpretation of the HR rely on the proportional hazards (PH) assumption, which is frequently violated in oncology due to delayed, waning, or crossing hazards. Although combination procedures built from weighted log-rank tests improve robustness to non-PH, hazard-based summaries remain difficult to interpret clinically. We propose combination tests that integrate two clinically interpretable estimands: the between-group difference in milestone survival probability S ( τ ) and in restricted mean survival time (RMST) μ ( 0 , τ ) . Using Monte Carlo simulations under a milestone-based design (uniform follow-up to τ ) and supplementary event-driven designs, we compared the proposed combinations with the log-rank and MaxCombo tests across diverse survival patterns, sample sizes, and censoring rates. We evaluated Type I error and power. The milestone combination procedures showed rejection probabilities close to the nominal level under the estimand-null configurations examined and showed competitive to superior power in non-PH settings, including transient effects and crossing hazards. Applications to three oncology RCTs illustrate that, when HRs hover near unity while absolute estimands differ, the milestone combinations can detect benefits at clinically meaningful horizons. These findings support aligning design, estimands, and analysis around a prespecified milestone. Careful selection of τ is essential to balance information and interpretability. Milestone-based combination tests provide a viable, patient-centered alternative or complement to HR-centric methods, particularly when non-PH is anticipated.

Assessing the Benefits and Burdens of Preventive Interventions

摘要

Cancer prevention is recognized as a key strategy for reducing disease incidence, mortality, and the overall burden on individuals and society. However, determining when to begin preventive interventions presents a significant challenge: starting too early may lead to more interventions and increased lifetime burdens due to repeated administrations, while delaying may miss opportunities to prevent cancer.
